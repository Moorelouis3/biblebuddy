Yesterday Esau walked out of the tent with a full stomach and no birthright.

Jacob stayed by the pot with the biggest share of the richest household in the land now sworn to him.

Two grown sons, two parents with favorites, and a crack running right down the middle of the family.

Then the rain stops.

A famine moves across the land. The grass dies, the wells drop, the herds get thin.

Isaac is now the head of everything Abraham built, hundreds of people and thousands of animals, and he has to feed all of them.

His father faced this once and did what everybody did. He went down to Egypt.

And it turned into one of the worst chapters of his life.

So before Isaac can pack the camels, God speaks to him. Directly, for the first time in the Bible. Do not go to Egypt.

Stay here. I will be with you. And then God hands Isaac the whole promise He gave Abraham, word for word.

Isaac obeys. He stays.

And then, in the very next verse, he does the one thing his father did twice, and it is painful to watch.

Today is about a man who follows God’s direction and is still afraid.

If you have ever done both at the same time, this day is for you.

# Obeying While Afraid

> “Sojourn in this land, and I will be with thee, and will bless thee.” (Genesis 26:3)

Today we are reading Genesis chapter 26, verses 1 to 11, and it opens with the kind of sentence that changes everything for a family that lives off the land.

> “And there was a famine in the land, beside the first famine that was in the days of Abraham. And Isaac went unto Abimelech king of the Philistines unto Gerar.” (Genesis 26:1)

A famine is not a bad month at the grocery store.

It is a year or more when the rain does not come, the crops fail, the grass burns off the hills, and the animals start dying.

For a household like Isaac’s, with flocks and herds and hundreds of mouths to feed, a famine is a slow death sentence unless you move.

And notice the Bible itself reminds you of the last one. *Beside the first famine that was in the days of Abraham*.

That is Genesis 12.

If you walked with us through The Obedience of Abraham, you remember what Abraham did when the food ran out.

He went down to Egypt, because Egypt had the Nile, and the Nile kept the fields green when everyone else’s were brown.

And in Egypt he got scared, told everyone Sarah was his sister, watched her get taken into Pharaoh’s house, and had to be rescued by God and thrown out of the country.

He came home rich, and he came home with an Egyptian servant girl named Hagar. You know what that led to.

So Isaac, facing the same famine his father faced, heads the same direction. Southwest, toward Egypt.

And on the way he stops in Gerar.

Let me put Gerar on the map for you.

It is a town in the southwest corner of the land, inland from the coast, on the road that runs down toward Egypt.

Still inside the land God promised, but right at the edge of it.

Abraham had lived there for a while, back in Genesis 20, and that is where he told his second lie about Sarah.

Keep that in mind. Isaac is walking into the exact town where his father got caught.

And the people there are the Philistines.

If that name rings a bell, it is because they are all over the later books of the Bible. Samson fights them.

Goliath is one. They live along the southwest coast in cities like Gaza and Ashkelon and Gath.

In Isaac’s day they are a smaller group, settled around Gerar, with a king of their own.

The big Philistine armies of Samson’s time came later, but the name belongs to this corner of the land, and the Bible uses it here so you know where you are.

Their king is called Abimelech.

That is the same name as the king who took Sarah in Genesis 20 and made a treaty with Abraham in Genesis 21.

But a lot of years have gone by. Isaac was a baby then, and now he has grown sons.

So I think the way to read this is the way you would read the name Pharaoh.

A royal name that passes down the line, probably to the old king’s son or successor.

Same name, same throne, almost certainly a different man.

Hold that thought, because the treaty that old Abimelech made with Abraham is going to matter today.

## Go Not Down Into Egypt

Now, before Isaac can go any farther, God stops him.

> “And the LORD appeared unto him, and said, Go not down into Egypt; dwell in the land which I shall tell thee of: Sojourn in this land, and I will be with thee, and will bless thee; for unto thee, and unto thy seed, I will give all these countries, and I will perform the oath which I sware unto Abraham thy father.” (Genesis 26:2–3)

This is the first time in the Bible that God speaks to Isaac directly. Think about that.

Isaac is sixty years old at least, probably a good deal more. He has heard about God his whole life.

He was on the mountain. He has watched his father build altars for decades.

But every word from God so far has come to Abraham.

Now the LORD appears to Isaac himself, and the first thing He says is, do not go to Egypt.

I hear a callback in that. God does not explain Himself, but Isaac did not need Him to.

Everybody in that camp knew what happened the last time this family ran to Egypt in a famine.

The lie, the taken wife, the Egyptian servant, and the thirteen years of trouble she brought home with her.

God is closing that road before Isaac can take it. Stay here. I will handle the famine.

And look at the words *the land which I shall tell thee of*. Abraham heard almost the same thing twice.

In Genesis 12 God said, go *unto a land that I will shew thee*.

In Genesis 22 He said, go to *one of the mountains which I will tell thee of*.

God tells this family where to go one step at a time. He does not hand out the whole map.

Isaac is being asked to do what his father did. Move when God says move, and trust Him for the next instruction.

Sojourn means to live somewhere as a stranger, as a guest, without owning it.

That is what Abraham did his whole life, and now it is Isaac’s turn. And then God piles the promises up.

## As the Stars of Heaven

> “And I will make thy seed to multiply as the stars of heaven, and will give unto thy seed all these countries; and in thy seed shall all the nations of the earth be blessed; Because that Abraham obeyed my voice, and kept my charge, my commandments, my statutes, and my laws.” (Genesis 26:4–5)

Count them. I will be with you. I will bless you. This land is yours and your children’s.

I will keep the oath I swore to your father. Your descendants will be like the stars.

And through your family every nation on earth will be blessed.

That is the whole promise God gave Abraham, the one He swore on His own name at the top of the mountain, now handed to Isaac personally.

I want to be careful with one thing. This is not God choosing Isaac for the first time.

He chose Isaac before he was born, back in Genesis 17, and the last verse we read in The Obedience of Abraham said that after Abraham died, *God blessed his son Isaac*.

What is happening here is that the promise Isaac inherited is now spoken to his face.

The son is hearing it in his own ears, from God Himself, instead of from his father.

And why? *Because that Abraham obeyed my voice*. Not because Isaac earned it.

Because his father walked a hundred years in God’s direction. Isaac is standing on his father’s obedience.

The promise came to him before he had done anything at all, which is exactly what Rebekah learned yesterday about her younger son.

That is how this family works. God’s choice first, then the person’s walk.

## She Is My Sister

> “And Isaac dwelt in Gerar.” (Genesis 26:6)

Four words. He obeyed. God said stay, and Isaac stayed. No Egypt.

Mark that down, because the next verse is going to make you forget it, and I do not want you to.

> “And the men of the place asked him of his wife; and he said, She is my sister: for he feared to say, She is my wife; lest, said he, the men of the place should kill me for Rebekah; because she was fair to look upon.” (Genesis 26:7)

There it is. One verse after God says *I will be with thee*, Isaac is afraid for his life and lying about his wife.

And you have heard this before. *Fair to look upon*.

Those are almost the exact words Abraham used about Sarah on the road to Egypt, *a fair woman to look upon*.

The fear is the same too. Abraham said the Egyptians would kill him and keep her.

In Gerar he said, *they will slay me for my wife’s sake*.

Now his son says, *the men of the place should kill me for Rebekah*.

Same place, same fear, same lie, one generation later.

So where did Isaac get it? I think he got it at his father’s fire. He grew up hearing the stories.

Egypt, Pharaoh, Gerar, Abimelech.

And somewhere along the way the lesson he took was not, God rescued us in spite of the lie. It was, the lie worked.

We walked out alive. That is the thing about the sins of a father. The children do not just inherit the herds.

They inherit the reflexes.

There is one difference worth noticing. With Abraham and Sarah, we were told they had an arrangement.

Abraham said in Genesis 20 that he had asked Sarah, back when they first set out, to call him her brother everywhere they went.

She was part of the plan. Here we only hear Isaac. The men ask, and he answers.

The Bible does not give us one word from Rebekah about it. I picture her finding out the way everyone else did.

## Obedience and Imperfect Trust

Now here is the question that bothered me, and I think it should bother you.

Why would Isaac expect to be killed in Gerar at all?

Go back to Genesis 21. The old Abimelech came to Abraham with his army commander and asked him to swear an oath.

The words are specific.

He asked Abraham to swear *that thou wilt not deal falsely with me, nor with my son, nor with my son’s son*, and to show the same kindness to Abimelech’s family that Abimelech had shown to him.

The Bible calls it a covenant, a binding agreement between the two houses, and it reached down to the grandchildren on purpose.

Isaac’s generation was named in it. There was supposed to be peace between Abraham’s family and this town.

So Isaac walks into Gerar with a treaty behind him and a promise from God in front of him, and he still assumes the men there will murder him for his wife.

That tells you something about fear. It does not read treaties. It does not remember promises.

It just looks at the men in the gate and sees knives. And notice something else. The treaty said no dealing falsely.

The first thing Isaac does in Gerar is deal falsely.

Let me be plain about why this is wrong, because the Bible does not spell it out and I do not want you to shrug at it.

Isaac is not protecting Rebekah. He is protecting himself with Rebekah.

If his lie works, the price is paid by her, because a beautiful single woman in a strange town is exactly what he is pretending she is.

He has taken the person God gave him, the wife his father’s servant crossed a desert to find, and put her out front as a shield.

That is a failure of a husband’s first job.

And it is a lie, told to the very people his family had sworn to deal with honestly.

And he does all of this while obeying God. That is the part I keep coming back to.

Verse 6, he stays in Gerar because God said so. Verse 7, he lies because he is afraid.

Both are true of the same man in the same week.

He follows God on the big thing, where to live, and then tries to control the outcome on his own when the fear hits.

I know that man. I have been that man.

You pray about the decision, you make the move God told you to make, and then you get there and start managing the risk yourself, because somewhere in you the promise has not sunk all the way in.

Obedience and imperfect trust can live in the same tent. Isaac proves it.

And see how close this is to yesterday. Esau treated something precious as nothing because he was hungry.

Isaac treats something precious, his wife and his honesty, as nothing because he is afraid.

Different pressure, same move.

When the pressure is high enough, people throw the valuable thing on the fire to make the pressure stop.

## The Lesson in the Window

> “And it came to pass, when he had been there a long time, that Abimelech king of the Philistines looked out at a window, and saw, and, behold, Isaac was sporting with Rebekah his wife.” (Genesis 26:8)

*A long time*. The lie held. Nobody took Rebekah, nobody killed Isaac. Weeks turned into months.

And I think that is when a lie gets dangerous, when it seems to be working. You stop guarding it.

Then the king looks out a window. Just a window, just an ordinary day. And he sees Isaac sporting with Rebekah.

That old word means playing affectionately.

Caressing, laughing, touching the way a husband and wife do when they think nobody is watching.

There is even a little joke in the Hebrew, because Isaac’s name means he laughs, and the word for sporting comes from the same root.

Isaac is being Isaac with his wife. And it is enough. The king takes one look and knows.

Brothers and sisters do not act like that.

Here is the lesson in the window. Nothing hidden stays hidden. Isaac could manage what he said.

He could not manage who he was. The truth leaked out through the way he loved his wife.

> “And Abimelech called Isaac, and said, Behold, of a surety she is thy wife: and how saidst thou, She is my sister? And Isaac said unto him, Because I said, Lest I die for her.” (Genesis 26:9)

*Of a surety* means certainly. For sure. The king calls him in and lays it out. She is your wife.

Why did you say sister? And Isaac tells the truth at last. I thought I would die because of her. No excuse, no story.

Just the fear, said out loud to a king.

## A Lecture on Morality From a Philistine

> “And Abimelech said, What is this thou hast done unto us? one of the people might lightly have lien with thy wife, and thou shouldest have brought guiltiness upon us.” (Genesis 26:10)

Now read the king carefully, because he turns Isaac’s whole fear upside down.

Isaac was afraid these people were killers. The king is horrified at the thought of sin.

*One of the people might lightly have lien with thy wife*. One of the people means any man in town.

Lightly means easily, without thinking twice. Lien with means slept with.

The king is saying, some ordinary man could have taken her, believing she was single, and the whole town would have been guilty of adultery without even knowing it.

*Thou shouldest have brought guiltiness upon us*. You nearly made us sin.

That is the reversal of the day. The man with the promise assumed the pagans were murderers.

The pagan king is more worried about guilt before God than the man with the promise was. Isaac expected knives.

He got a lecture on morality from a Philistine. The same thing happened to his father in this same town.

Old Abimelech asked Abraham, what did I do to you that you brought this on my kingdom?

Now the son hears it from the son.

> “And Abimelech charged all his people, saying, He that toucheth this man or his wife shall surely be put to death.” (Genesis 26:11)

And then the king does the thing Isaac was sure he would never do. He protects him. A public order to the whole town.

Touch this man or his wife and you die. The man Isaac feared becomes the man guarding his marriage.

## I Will Be With Thee

Look at how God kept His word. He said *I will be with thee*, and He was, even in the middle of Isaac’s mess.

But notice how. In Genesis 20 God spoke to the king in a dream and told him he was a dead man. Here there is no dream.

Just a window, and a king with more sense than Isaac gave him credit for. God did not need to show up in thunder.

He used an ordinary afternoon and a decent man to put a wall around Rebekah that Isaac should have put there himself.

So here is the one thing to carry out of today.

You can follow God’s direction and still be afraid, and the fear will try to make you take the outcome into your own hands.

Isaac stayed where God said. That was real obedience, and God honored it.

But inside that obedience he still reached for his father’s old lie, because he could not quite believe that the God who said *I will be with thee* would actually be with him in a town full of strangers.

God was. He kept the promise anyway. He protected the wife Isaac failed to protect.

He did not wait for Isaac to get his trust perfect before He kept His word.

That is not permission to lie. The lie was wrong, it put Rebekah at risk, and it broke a sworn agreement.

It is something better than permission.

It is the news that God’s faithfulness to you does not rise and fall with your courage on any given day.

He said He would be with Isaac. He was with Isaac. Isaac’s fear did not cancel it.

But I will be honest with you. The next verse is going to raise a question you should ask.

Right after this lie, Isaac plants a crop in the middle of a famine and reaps a hundred times what he sowed.

Blessing on the heels of deception. His father got rich in Egypt the same way. What do we do with that?

Hold the question overnight.

Tomorrow we are going to watch the blessing grow, watch the Philistines turn on him because of it, and watch Isaac dig well after well through a wall of opposition until God meets him again.

For now, take the reflection questions first, then today’s action, and end your day in prayer.

## What To Watch For In Genesis 26

Verse 1 points you back to a famine in Abraham’s day.

If you have your Bible open, glance at Genesis 12:10 to 13 and Genesis 20:1 to 2 before you start.

Today will make much more sense if those two scenes are fresh.

Count the promises God makes in verses 3 and 4. There are more than you think.

Then watch how fast verse 7 comes after verse 6. Obedience and fear are one verse apart.

**As you read, notice:**

* 🧭 Where God tells Isaac not to go, and where He tells him to stay
* 📜 The reason God gives in verse 5 for the promise
* 😨 Why Isaac says Rebekah is his sister
* 🪟 What the king sees through the window, and what he does about it

And think about this question: can you obey God and be afraid at the same time?

## Reflection Questions

1. **Isaac obeyed God about where to live and then lied because he was afraid. Where are you following God on the big decision but managing the risk yourself on the small ones?** Name the move you made because God said so. Then name the place inside it where you are still hedging.
2. **Isaac inherited his father’s reflex, not just his herds. What fear or habit did you pick up at your parents’ fire that you have never questioned?** Write it down in one sentence. Then ask whether it is wisdom or just something that worked once.
3. **Isaac protected himself with Rebekah instead of protecting her. Who pays the price when you cover yourself?** A spouse, a child, a friend, a coworker. Be specific about what your self protection costs them.

## Today's One Thing

**Close the Window**

Isaac managed what he said and got caught by who he was.

Find the place where you are doing the same, and fix it before the king looks out the window.

1. Write down one thing you have been letting people believe that is not true, because the truth feels risky. Not a huge scandal. The small sister story you have been telling.
2. Correct it today, to the person who needs to hear it. A sentence is enough. Isaac told the king the truth in seven words.
3. Write Genesis 26:3 on a card, I will be with thee, and will bless thee, and put it where you will see it the next time fear tells you to handle things yourself.

One lie corrected, one promise where you can see it. Do it, and check it off.

## Today's Prayer

Father,

You spoke to Isaac in a famine and told him to stay, and he stayed.

Thank You for the times I have actually done what You said. You saw every one of them.

But You also saw the next verse.

The place where I obeyed You with my feet and still reached for a lie with my mouth, because I did not believe You would be with me in a town full of strangers.

Forgive me for that.

I want to trust You inside my obedience, not just at the start of it.

Show me the people I have been using as a shield. The ones who pay when I protect myself.

Give me the courage to put the wall around them that I should have built in the first place.

And thank You that Your faithfulness does not rise and fall with my courage.

You said You would be with Isaac, and You were, through a window and a pagan king. Be with me like that.

Keep Your word to me even on the days I do not keep mine.

In Jesus’ name, amen.
