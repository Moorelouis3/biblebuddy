Yesterday we watched two brothers cry on a road. Esau ran, Jacob bowed, and twenty years of fear ended in a hug.

Then Esau rode home to Seir.

Today we rewind one night.

It is dark on the bank of the Jabbok. The wives and the children and the servants have all crossed the water.

The herds have crossed. The gift for Esau is already miles down the road.

Everything Jacob owns and everyone Jacob loves is on the other side of the stream, and he is standing on this side by himself.

Tomorrow morning, four hundred men.

And out of the dark, somebody grabs him.

Nine verses. That is all the Bible gives this night.

But in those nine verses a man gets a dislocated hip, a new name, a blessing he refused to let go of, and a limp he carries for the rest of his life.

The nation of Israel gets its name here. We are going to walk through it one verse at a time and not hurry.

Today the heel grabber holds on to God and will not let go.

# I Will Not Let Go

> “And he said, I will not let thee go, except thou bless me.” (Genesis 32:26)

Yesterday we read all the way through the reunion.

Now come back with me to the river, to the night before, and start at verse 24.

> “And Jacob was left alone; and there wrestled a man with him until the breaking of the day.” (Genesis 32:24)

Left alone. Think about how strange that is. Jacob has hundreds of people in his camp.

He has just moved every one of them across the Jabbok in the dark.

And then he stays behind, by himself, on the far bank. Maybe he wanted to pray.

Maybe he wanted one quiet hour before the worst morning of his life.

I also think about what a man alone looks like to an approaching army.

Abraham had three hundred and eighteen trained men. Jacob has no army at all.

He has gifts on the road and a plan to bow. A man standing by himself at a river is not a threat to anybody.

Everything about him is saying I come in peace.

And then a man wrestles him until dawn. No warning. No introduction. The Bible does not tell us where he came from.

One moment Jacob is alone, and the next he is on the ground in a fight that lasts all night.

Who is this man? Hold the question for a minute, because the passage answers it as it goes. For now notice the word.

A man.

If you walked with us through The Obedience of Abraham, you remember three men who came to Abraham’s tent and ate his food, and two of them walked into Sodom to pull Lot out.

The Bible called them men too, and one of them was the LORD.

Hosea, writing hundreds of years later, looks back on this night and says Jacob *had power over the angel, and prevailed*.

By the end of the chapter Jacob himself will tell us he has seen God face to face.

So this is no stranger passing through. Jacob is wrestling with God, who has come to him looking like a man.

## The Hollow of His Thigh

> “And when he saw that he prevailed not against him, he touched the hollow of his thigh; and the hollow of Jacob’s thigh was out of joint, as he wrestled with him.” (Genesis 32:25)

Here is where you have to read carefully. *Prevailed not* means the man could not pin Jacob down.

All night, and Jacob is still fighting. Then the man touches him. One touch.

The hollow of the thigh is the hip socket, where the leg joins the body.

And *out of joint* means exactly what it sounds like. The hip comes out of its socket. Not a pulled muscle. Dislocated.

If you have ever seen that happen to someone, you know the kind of pain we are talking about.

Now put those two halves of the verse together, because they tell you what this whole night was really about.

The man could not beat Jacob by wrestling. And the man could put Jacob’s hip out with a touch. Both are true.

So the man was never trying his hardest. He could have ended this in the first second.

He let it go on all night, and when the sun was about to come up, He showed Jacob with one finger who had actually been holding back.

And Jacob does not let go. Read the next verse with that hip in your mind.

## Holding On to God When It Hurts

> “And he said, Let me go, for the day breaketh. And he said, I will not let thee go, except thou bless me.” (Genesis 32:26)

The man says let me go, it is getting light.

And Jacob, with his leg hanging wrong and the pain shooting through him, says no.

I will not let you go unless you bless me. He is not fighting anymore. He cannot. He is just holding on.

That is the picture I want you to carry out of today.

A man who is hurt, who cannot win, who has figured out who he is holding, and who will not open his hands until he gets a blessing.

That is holding on to God when it hurts. Not when it is easy.

When your hip is out and the only thing left in you is your grip.

## What Did Jacob Still Want

Now here is the question that stopped me when I studied this, and I want you to sit in it with me.

What did Jacob still want?

Think about what he already has. He has Isaac’s blessing from Day 7, the one he lied to get.

Plenty of corn and wine, nations bowing, lord over his brothers.

He has the second blessing too, the one Isaac gave him on purpose before he left, the blessing of Abraham.

He has God’s own promise at Bethel, *I am with thee, and will keep thee in all places whither thou goest, and will bring thee again into this land*.

He has God’s word from Haran, *Return unto the land of thy fathers, and I will be with thee*. And he is rich.

He just sent five hundred and fifty animals down the road as a gift and barely felt it.

Blessing on blessing on blessing. What is left to ask for?

Go back to his prayer from yesterday.

*Deliver me, I pray thee, from the hand of my brother, from the hand of Esau: for I fear him, lest he will come and smite me, and the mother with the children.*

That is what he wants. Not a new promise about the far future. He wants this night and tomorrow morning covered.

Four hundred men. His wives and children across the river.

The mess of his own choices from twenty years ago walking up the road to meet him.

Somewhere in the dark it dawned on him who he was wrestling, and when it did, he wanted this God to speak over that situation before He left.

And here is the thing about all those earlier promises. They did not make Jacob stop asking.

They were the reason he could ask. God had said I will keep you. God had said I will bring you home.

So Jacob holds on and says, then do it. Bless me. Say it over this.

A promise you already have is not a reason to stop praying. It is the ground you stand on when you pray.

I read this whole night as God’s answer to the prayer in verse 11.

Jacob asked to be delivered from Esau, and God came down to the river in person. Not with a message. With Himself.

## What Is Thy Name

> “And he said unto him, What is thy name? And he said, Jacob.” (Genesis 32:27)

What is your name? The man knows his name. This is not a question for information.

Think about the last time somebody asked Jacob who he was.

Back on Day 7, a blind old man in a tent said, *who art thou, my son*, and Jacob answered, *I am Esau thy first born*.

He got the blessing by saying the wrong name.

Now, in the dark, with his hip out, holding on to God for a blessing, he is asked the same question.

And he tells the truth. Jacob.

Remember what that name carries. He was called Jacob because he came out of the womb holding Esau’s heel.

And Esau turned it into an insult on Day 8. *Is not he rightly named Jacob? for he hath supplanted me these two times.*

Heel grabber. Cheat. The one who takes your place. When Jacob says his name here, he is owning all of that.

This is who I am. This is what I have done. And I am still asking You to bless me.

## Struggled With God and With Men

> “And he said, Thy name shall be called no more Jacob, but Israel: for as a prince hast thou power with God and with men, and hast prevailed.” (Genesis 32:28)

And God gives him a new one. Israel. Let me explain it, because the old English can send you the wrong way.

The King James says *as a prince hast thou power*.

That translates one Hebrew word that means you have struggled, you have striven.

It does not make Jacob royalty and it does not make him stronger than God.

The name Israel is built from that word for striving joined to El, the word for God. God explains it in the verse.

You have struggled with God and with men and you have come through.

The name can also be heard the other way around, God fights. Both are in there, and both are true of this man.

Prevailed means you succeeded, you got through. Not that Jacob overpowered God.

We just saw that God could drop him with one touch.

Jacob prevailed because he held on, hurt, until he got the blessing he came for.

That is what winning looked like at the Jabbok.

You may remember Rachel using the same word on Day 13 when Naphtali was born, *with great wrestlings have I wrestled with my sister, and I have prevailed*.

Jacob’s wife said it first. Now God says it over Jacob.

A new name from God is a big deal in this family.

God changed Abram to Abraham and Sarai to Sarah, and He tied both names to promises about the family and the nations that would come from them.

Now the grandson gets his.

In the next book God will say it over him again at Bethel, and the whole nation will carry it.

From this night on, every time you read *the children of Israel* in your Bible, you are reading about the descendants of a man who held on to God with a broken hip.

The Bible keeps calling him Jacob too, by the way. The old name does not vanish. But the new one is who he is now.

## I Have Seen God Face to Face

> “And Jacob asked him, and said, Tell me, I pray thee, thy name. And he said, Wherefore is it that thou dost ask after my name? And he blessed him there.” (Genesis 32:29)

Now Jacob asks the same question back. Tell me your name. And the man does not answer. Why do you ask?

And then, right there, He blesses him. Notice the trade. Jacob gave his name and got a new one.

Jacob asked for a name and got a blessing instead.

Years later a man named Manoah, the father of Samson, asks an angel the same question and gets nearly the same answer.

God does not hand out His name the way we hand out ours. But He gives the blessing.

The Bible does not tell us the words of it. I do not think Jacob needed them written down.

He knew what he had asked for.

> “And Jacob called the name of the place Peniel: for I have seen God face to face, and my life is preserved.” (Genesis 32:30)

Here is Jacob’s own answer to who the man was. Peniel means face of God. Pene is face of, and El is God.

I have seen God face to face, and I am still alive. *Preserved* means spared.

He expected that seeing God up close would kill him, and it did not.

Now go back to yesterday and listen again to what Jacob said to Esau on the road.

*I have seen thy face, as though I had seen the face of God, and thou wast pleased with me.*

The night before, he saw God’s face and lived. The next morning he saw Esau’s face and lived.

Same word, same man, one night apart. When Jacob said that to his brother, he was not making a pretty speech.

He had just come from Peniel. He was telling Esau exactly what the last twelve hours had been like.

## He Halted Upon His Thigh

> “And as he passed over Penuel the sun rose upon him, and he halted upon his thigh.” (Genesis 32:31)

Penuel is the same place as Peniel, just spelled a little differently.

The sun comes up, Jacob crosses over to rejoin his family, and he halted upon his thigh. That means he limped.

His hip is still out. He walks toward the four hundred men dragging a leg.

I love that detail. Think about what Jacob is bringing into that meeting. Not strength.

He cannot run, he cannot fight, he can barely walk. If Esau wants to kill him, there is nothing Jacob can do about it.

All he has is a new name, a blessing from God, and a plan to bow.

He goes in weak on purpose, because God made him weak the night before.

And it is the weak, limping Jacob that Esau runs to and hugs.

God took away the last of Jacob’s own strength right before He answered his prayer, so that nobody could say Jacob managed it himself.

> “Therefore the children of Israel eat not of the sinew which shrank, which is upon the hollow of the thigh, unto this day: because he touched the hollow of Jacob’s thigh in the sinew that shrank.” (Genesis 32:32)

A sinew is a tough cord of tissue near the hip joint.

Because of this night, Jacob’s descendants would not eat that part of an animal, and *unto this day* means it was still true when Genesis was written down.

Every time a family in Israel sat down to a meal and cut around that sinew, they were remembering the night their father held on to God and limped away blessed.

And look at the words. *The children of Israel.* This is the first time your Bible uses that phrase.

A nation is named in the middle of a verse about a hip.

So step back with me and look at the whole night.

Jacob did everything a man could do. He split the camp. He prayed. He sent the gift. He moved the family.

He prepared like a man who meant it.

And then God met him alone at the river, and nothing Jacob had prepared mattered for the next nine verses.

He could not win. He got hurt. And instead of quitting, he held on.

He told God his real name, the ugly one, and God gave him a new one and blessed him.

That is what I want you to take from Peniel. Prayer and preparation are good. Jacob did both and I want you to do both.

But in the end, the blessing did not come from the plan.

It came from holding on to God through the pain, saying your real name out loud, and refusing to let go until He answers.

And God answered. The next morning the brother Jacob feared ran to him and wept on his neck.

The man who prevailed with God prevailed with men, just like the name said.

Tomorrow we follow Esau home.

For now, take the reflection questions first, then today’s action, and end your day in prayer.

## What To Watch For In Genesis 32

These are the verses we skipped yesterday.

They happen the night before Jacob meets Esau, after he sends his family over the river in verse 23. Read them slowly.

There are only nine.

Watch what happens to Jacob’s body in verse 25, and what he still says in verse 26.

Notice who asks for a name and who gives one. Then look at what Jacob calls the place.

**As you read, notice:**

* 🌌 Who Jacob is with in verse 24
* ✋ What the man does when he cannot win
* ❓ The question in verse 27 and the answer
* 🌅 How Jacob walks away in verse 31

And think about this question: Jacob already had the blessing, the promise, and the wealth. What did he still want?

## Reflection Questions

1. **Jacob had every promise already and still held on for a blessing over the thing in front of him. What is the situation you need God to speak over right now, today?** Not the far future. This week. Name it the way Jacob named Esau in his prayer.
2. **When God asked his name, Jacob said Jacob. Heel grabber. What is the honest name for the thing you have done that you would rather not say out loud to God?** He blessed Jacob after the true name, not before. Say yours.
3. **Jacob walked into the meeting with Esau limping, with no strength of his own left. Where has God taken away your strength right before you needed it most?** Think about whether that weakness might be the point. The limping Jacob is the one Esau hugged.

## Today's One Thing

**Say Your Name**

Jacob got the blessing after he said his real name and held on. Do the same thing tonight.

1. Write the one thing you are asking God to bless. One sentence. The situation in front of you, not a wish list.
2. Under it, write your honest name for yourself in that situation. The thing you did, the way you have been acting. Jacob said heel grabber. Be that honest.
3. Pray over that page for ten full minutes without stopping. Set a timer. Say your name out loud to God, then say I will not let go until You bless this, and keep praying until the timer ends.

One request, one honest name, ten minutes of holding on. Do it, and check it off.

## Today's Prayer

Father,

You came down to the river in person. Jacob asked You to deliver him, and You did not send a message.

You came and took hold of him. Thank You for being a God who shows up in the dark.

You asked his name, and he told You the truth. So here is mine. You know what I have done and who I have been.

I am not going to pretend to be somebody else to get Your blessing.

I will not let You go. Bless me, Lord. Speak over the thing in front of me.

When it hurts, give me Jacob’s grip. When I cannot win, help me hold on anyway.

When You take my strength away, help me trust that You are about to answer.

And give me a new name. Not heel grabber. Not cheat. The name You have for me.

Let me limp into tomorrow carrying it, and let the people I fear run to me the way Esau ran to him.

In Jesus’ name, amen.
