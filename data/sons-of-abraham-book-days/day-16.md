Yesterday we left Jacob rich.

Six years of striped sticks and strong stock, and the man who walked into Haran with a staff now owns flocks, servants, camels and donkeys.

He finally has a house of his own.

But he is still living in Laban’s camp. And in a camp like that, you cannot get rich without somebody noticing.

Laban’s sons are watching Jacob’s flocks grow while their father’s shrink, and they are talking.

Laban is watching too, and his face has changed.

Twenty years ago, on a stone pillow at Bethel, God told a frightened young man, I will bring thee again into this land.

Jacob has been waiting two decades for the second half of that sentence. Today he hears it. Return.

So he calls his wives out to the field, away from every ear in the camp, and lays out twenty years of cheating in one speech.

He asks them to leave their father. And the answer they give tells you what it was really like to be Laban’s daughters.

Then, while Laban is away with the shearers, the whole household gets on the camels and goes.

And one of the wives takes something she should not have.

# The Word He Waited for

> “Return unto the land of thy fathers, and to thy kindred; and I will be with thee.” (Genesis 31:3)

Today we are reading Genesis chapter 31, verses 1 to 21. Yesterday ended with Jacob increasing exceedingly.

Today starts with what that increase cost him.

> “And he heard the words of Laban’s sons, saying, Jacob hath taken away all that was our father’s; and of that which was our father’s hath he gotten all this glory. And Jacob beheld the countenance of Laban, and, behold, it was not toward him as before.” (Genesis 31:1–2)

Laban’s sons. We met them yesterday, when Laban handed them all the marked animals and sent them three days away.

Now they are back in the conversation, and they are angry. *Jacob hath taken away all that was our father’s.*

That is not true, and they know it. Jacob took the wages he was promised. But think about it from where they stand.

They are Laban’s heirs.

Everything Laban owns is supposed to come to them one day, and for six years they have watched it walk across the field into Jacob’s pens.

Strong animals, marked animals, more every season. Glory here means wealth, the visible signs of a rich man.

Jacob has it, and they feel robbed.

And Laban’s countenance, his face, the look he gives Jacob, has changed.

Twenty years ago he ran to meet this nephew and kissed him at the well. Now he looks at him like a problem.

Jacob can read it. You can always read it on the face of a man who thinks you are costing him money.

## I Will Be With Thee

> “And the LORD said unto Jacob, Return unto the land of thy fathers, and to thy kindred; and I will be with thee.” (Genesis 31:3)

There it is. The word Jacob has been waiting for since Day 9.

Go back to Bethel.

A young man running from his brother, sleeping on a rock, and God standing above a ladder saying, *“I am with thee, and will keep thee in all places whither thou goest, and will bring thee again into this land” (Genesis 28:15)*.

Twenty years.

Two wives he did not plan on, twelve children, ten changed wage agreements, and a father in law who never let go.

Through all of it, that promise sat there waiting. And now God says the second half. Return.

And He adds the same thing He said on the rock. *I will be with thee.*

I want you to notice the timing. God does not tell Jacob to leave the day he got rich.

He tells him to leave the day the camp turned against him.

The sons are muttering, the face has changed, the air has gone cold, and that is when God speaks.

Sometimes God lets the place you are in become uncomfortable right before He tells you to move.

The cold look on Laban’s face was part of the call.

## Changed My Wages Ten Times

> “And Jacob sent and called Rachel and Leah to the field unto his flock, And said unto them, I see your father’s countenance, that it is not toward me as before; but the God of my father hath been with me.” (Genesis 31:4–5)

Look where he holds this meeting. Not in a tent. Out in the field, by his flock, away from the camp.

Laban’s camp has ears, and what Jacob is about to say cannot be overheard. He calls both wives.

Rachel first, because she is the one he loves, but Leah too.

He needs both of them, because what he is asking is enormous. Leave your father.

Leave the only home you have ever known. Come to a land you have never seen, to a family you have never met.

And he starts with the two facts that matter. Your father’s face has changed. And God has been with me.

Those two things, side by side. The man who gave me nothing, and the God who gave me everything.

> “And ye know that with all my power I have served your father. And your father hath deceived me, and changed my wages ten times; but God suffered him not to hurt me.” (Genesis 31:6–7)

Suffered here means allowed. God did not allow Laban to hurt him. And catch the number. *Changed my wages ten times.*

Yesterday we saw Laban change the deal once, the same afternoon he made it.

Jacob is telling us that was only the beginning. Ten times in six years.

Every time the marked animals came, Laban moved the line.

If the speckled ones were Jacob’s, suddenly the speckled ones stopped being Jacob’s and the striped ones were.

Whatever Jacob was earning, Laban changed the rule so he would earn less.

> “If he said thus, The speckled shall be thy wages; then all the cattle bare speckled: and if he said thus, The ringstraked shall be thy hire; then bare all the cattle ringstraked. Thus God hath taken away the cattle of your father, and given them to me.” (Genesis 31:8–9)

And every time Laban moved the line, God moved the flock. Laban says speckled, the flock throws speckled.

Laban switches to striped, the flock throws striped.

Ten times Laban changed the deal, and ten times the animals came out whatever colour Jacob needed them to be.

That is not sticks in a trough. Jacob knows it, and he says so.

*God hath taken away the cattle of your father, and given them to me.* He is not telling his wives how clever he was.

He is telling them God did it.

## The God of Bethel

> “And it came to pass at the time that the cattle conceived, that I lifted up mine eyes, and saw in a dream, and, behold, the rams which leaped upon the cattle were ringstraked, speckled, and grisled. And the angel of God spake unto me in a dream, saying, Jacob: And I said, Here am I.” (Genesis 31:10–11)

Here is the dream we jumped ahead to yesterday.

Jacob sees the rams mating with the flock, and every one of them is marked. Ringstraked is striped. Speckled is spotted.

Grisled means grey, with patches of white and dark mixed together. And an angel calls his name. *Jacob.*

And he answers the way Abraham answered on the mountain, the way Moses will answer at the bush. *Here am I.*

> “And he said, Lift up now thine eyes, and see, all the rams which leap upon the cattle are ringstraked, speckled, and grisled: for I have seen all that Laban doeth unto thee. I am the God of Bethel, where thou anointedst the pillar, and where thou vowedst a vow unto me: now arise, get thee out from this land, and return unto the land of thy kindred.” (Genesis 31:12–13)

Read that slowly, because this is the heart of the day. *I have seen all that Laban doeth unto thee.*

Every changed wage. Every removed animal. Every three day march. God saw it.

Jacob was not alone in that field with a cheating man. God was watching the whole time, and He was answering with lambs.

And then God tells Jacob who He is. *I am the God of Bethel.* The God of the rock and the ladder.

The God who heard a scared young man make a vow with conditions on it, if God will be with me, if He will keep me, if He will give me bread and clothes and bring me home in peace, then the LORD shall be my God.

God is saying, I remember that night. I remember what you asked for. I did it all.

Now it is time to come home and keep your end.

## He Hath Sold Us

> “And Rachel and Leah answered and said unto him, Is there yet any portion or inheritance for us in our father’s house? Are we not counted of him strangers? for he hath sold us, and hath quite devoured also our money. For all the riches which God hath taken from our father, that is ours, and our children’s: now then, whatsoever God hath said unto thee, do.” (Genesis 31:14–16)

Now listen to the sisters.

These two women have spent two chapters at war with each other over babies and mandrakes and nights with Jacob.

And here, for the first time, they speak together. One voice. And what they say about their father is devastating.

*Is there yet any portion or inheritance for us?* Is there anything left for us in his house? No.

*Are we not counted of him strangers?* He treats us like outsiders, not daughters. *He hath sold us.*

Here is what they mean.

In that world a man paid a bride price to the father of the girl he married, and a good father used that to set his daughter up, a gift that stayed with her for life.

Jacob’s bride price was fourteen years of labor. Laban took every bit of it and gave his daughters nothing.

*Quite devoured also our money.* He ate it. Swallowed the whole thing. Their father did not marry them off.

He sold them, and then he spent the money on himself.

So when Jacob says God has taken Laban’s cattle and given it to him, the sisters say, good.

*That is ours, and our children’s.* It was always supposed to be ours. God is just giving us back what our father ate.

And then the line that settles it. *Whatsoever God hath said unto thee, do.* Not, let us think about it.

Not, let us talk to father first. Do what God said.

Two women who grew up in that house just told you what it was like to live there.

Hold onto their words, because tomorrow Laban is going to tell a very different story about what kind of father he was.

## A Whole Nation on Camels

> “Then Jacob rose up, and set his sons and his wives upon camels; And he carried away all his cattle, and all his goods which he had gotten, the cattle of his getting, which he had gotten in Padan-aram, for to go to Isaac his father in the land of Canaan.” (Genesis 31:17–18)

Now picture it. This is not one man slipping out of camp. Eleven boys and a girl on camels. Four women.

Servants, men and women. Flocks of sheep and goats, donkeys loaded with tents and tools and food.

Padan Aram is the region around Haran, up in the north, the land Rebekah came from.

Canaan is hundreds of miles south and west. Isaac is there, old and blind, and he has not seen this son in twenty years.

The Bible keeps saying *his getting*, *which he had gotten*, three times in one verse, because it wants you to know that every animal in that caravan is Jacob’s by right.

He is not stealing. He is taking home what God gave him.

And remember how he came. One man, one staff.

He will say it himself in the next chapter, *with my staff I passed over this Jordan; and now I am become two bands*.

God said at Bethel that He would bring him back, and He is bringing him back with a whole nation on camels.

## Little Statues in a Bag

> “And Laban went to shear his sheep: and Rachel had stolen the images that were her father’s.” (Genesis 31:19)

Two things in one verse, and both matter.

First, where is Laban? Shearing sheep. Let me explain why that is the perfect moment.

Sheep shearing was one of the biggest events of the year for a man with flocks. Wool was money.

Once a year, every sheep had to be caught, held down and clipped, and the wool bundled and stored.

It took days, it took every hand you could get, and it usually ended with eating and drinking.

Later in the Bible a rich man named Nabal holds a sheep shearing feast fit for a king.

And here is a connection I noticed.

In Genesis 38, Judah, one of the boys on these camels, goes up to Timnath to shear his sheep, and Tamar hears where he will be and plants herself on the road.

Sheep shearing told everybody where a man was going to be and how long he would be busy.

Jacob knows Laban is tied up with the shearers, days away, with his sons and their flocks three days off in the other direction.

The one window in the whole year when nobody is watching. Jacob takes it.

Second, Rachel. *Rachel had stolen the images that were her father’s.*

The images are small household idols, little figures of gods that families in that part of the world kept in the tent for protection and blessing.

Laban had them. Rachel grew up with them. And on her way out the door, she takes them. Jacob does not know.

The Bible is clear about that later. Why did she take them?

Hold that question until tomorrow, when it nearly gets her killed.

For now, just notice what it tells you about the house they are leaving.

Laban talks about the LORD when it suits him, but his gods are little statues in a bag, and his daughter grew up thinking they were worth stealing.

## Out the Back Way

> “And Jacob stole away unawares to Laban the Syrian, in that he told him not that he fled. So he fled with all that he had; and he rose up, and passed over the river, and set his face toward the mount Gilead.” (Genesis 31:20–21)

Stole away unawares means he left secretly, without telling Laban. Not that Jacob stole anything.

He left without a goodbye.

Laban is called the Syrian here, which in our Bibles means an Aramean, one of the people who lived in that northern country.

The Bible is marking the line. Jacob is leaving the Arameans and going back to the land of his fathers.

And twice it says *fled*. Jacob did not walk out. He ran.

He crossed *the river*, which is the Euphrates, the great river that marked the edge of Laban’s world, and he pointed his caravan toward mount Gilead, the high hill country east of the Jordan River.

From there, Canaan is just across the water.

Now, why did he run? God said return. God said I will be with thee. Why not walk up to Laban, shake his hand, and go?

Jacob tells us tomorrow. He was afraid Laban would take his wives by force. And I believe him. Think about who Laban is.

He switched brides on Jacob’s wedding night. He changed his wages ten times. He hid the marked animals three days away.

Would that man let four women, twelve children and half the wealth of the region ride out of his camp with a wave?

Jacob did not think so, and twenty years of evidence said he was right.

But I also think there is something in Jacob that has not changed.

The young man who ran from Esau is now a grey haired father running from Laban.

He has God’s word and God’s promise, and he still goes out the back way in the dark. That is Jacob.

He trusts God and hedges his bets at the same time.

He will keep doing it right up until a night by a river when God finally takes hold of him and will not let go.

But that is a few days away.

So step back and look at the whole day. God saw everything Laban did, and He answered with twenty years of lambs.

Then He said one word, return, and a man with nothing to go back to twenty years ago went home with a nation on camels.

Here is the lesson I want you to carry. God sees what is being done to you.

The changed wages, the cold face, the deal that gets moved every time you get close. He sees all of it.

And He is not just watching.

He is working, in ways you cannot see from inside the field, and when it is time, He will say the word.

The God of Bethel does not forget a vow made on a rock. He brings people home.

Tomorrow, somebody rides to the shearing and tells Laban his daughters are gone.

He gathers his men and comes after them hard, seven days on the road, and catches the caravan in the hills of Gilead.

But today is not finished yet. Take the reflection questions first, then today’s action, and end your day in prayer.

## What To Watch For In Genesis 31

Most of this passage is Jacob talking to his wives.

He is telling them the inside story of the last six years, so listen for what he says God did.

Rachel and Leah have fought each other for two chapters. Watch what happens when they answer Jacob in verses 14 to 16.

They speak with one voice.

The last few verses move fast. Catch what Laban is doing when they leave, and what Rachel takes with her.

**As you read, notice:**

* 🗣️ What Laban’s sons are saying in verse 1
* 🏡 The exact words God speaks to Jacob in verse 3
* 💔 What the two sisters say their father did to them
* 🐑 Where Laban is in verse 19, and what Rachel steals

And think about this question: why would a man who has God’s promise still sneak away in the night?

## Reflection Questions

1. **God said, I have seen all that Laban doeth unto thee. What is being done to you right now that you think nobody sees?** Write it down and say it to God. He was watching the whole field the whole time.
2. **God told Jacob to return on the day the camp turned cold. Is there a place in your life that has gone cold, and could that be God telling you it is time to move?** Not every hard season is a call to leave. But some are. Ask Him honestly which one this is.
3. **Jacob had God’s word and still snuck out in the dark. Where are you obeying God and hedging your bets at the same time?** Name the back door you are keeping open. Then ask what it would look like to walk out the front.

## Today's One Thing

**Keep Your Vow**

At Bethel Jacob made God a promise with conditions. God kept every condition and then called him home to keep his side.

Today, go back to a promise you made God.

1. Write down one thing you promised God in a hard moment. If You get me through this, I will. Be specific about what you said.
2. Under it, write what God actually did. Did He get you through? Be honest about how much of your Bethel prayer He answered.
3. Do one concrete thing today toward keeping your side. Give the thing, make the call, start the habit. Jacob packed the camels the same day God spoke.

One promise remembered, one answer recorded, one step taken. Do it, and check it off.
