Yesterday ended with six words that are going to shape this whole family. *He loved also Rachel more than Leah.*

Jacob has two wives now, sisters, living in the same tents, sharing the same husband.

And everybody knows which one he wanted.

Put yourself in Leah’s place for a minute. Your father used you to trick a man.

The man woke up and was angry that it was you. A week later he married your little sister, the one he actually loves.

And now you live next door to that love for the rest of your life.

The Bible does not look away from her. In fact, the very next verse tells us that someone saw her. Not Jacob. The LORD.

Today eight sons are born. Eight.

And the Bible gives each of them a name with a reason attached, and the reasons are the real story.

Listen to the mothers explain the names, and you will hear two sisters, each one aching for the thing the other one has.

One has a husband’s love and no children. The other has children and no husband’s love.

And neither of them can see what she holds, because she is staring at what she lacks.

Today the twelve tribes of Israel start being born, and they are born into this. Let’s read it.

# What the Other Has

> “And when the LORD saw that Leah was hated, he opened her womb: but Rachel was barren.” (Genesis 29:31)

> “And when the LORD saw that Leah was hated, he opened her womb: but Rachel was barren.” (Genesis 29:31)

Wait. Hated? Yesterday the Bible said Jacob loved Rachel more than Leah. Now it says Leah was hated.

That is a big jump, and I do not want to skim past it.

The Hebrew word is senuah, from a root that really does mean to hate. But look at the context.

The verse right before it says Jacob loved Rachel more. So the Bible is using hated to mean the one who was not chosen.

Unloved. Second. It is not the murderous hate Esau felt toward Jacob, and the Bible uses a different word for that.

You can love one person more and still be decent to the other, and nothing here says Jacob was cruel to Leah day to day.

But do not soften it too much, because Leah’s own words are about to show you how deeply she felt it.

Here is the sentence I want you to hold all day. Jacob gave Leah a place in his household.

She is still longing for a place in his heart. That is a loneliness that can live inside a full tent.

If you walked with us through The Obedience of Abraham, you watched Hagar live on the edge of Sarah’s household, in the tent but never in the center.

Leah is a real wife, not a servant, and her position is different. But the ache is the same.

She is there, and she is not wanted.

## And the LORD Saw

And the LORD saw. Stop on that. Jacob did not see. Laban did not care.

But God looked at the unwanted wife and did something about it. He opened her womb.

Meanwhile Rachel, the loved one, the beautiful one, was barren, unable to have children.

Now, a question that came up in my own study.

Back in Genesis 16, Sarah told Abraham, *the LORD hath restrained me from bearing*. Was she right?

Look at today’s verses. God opens Leah’s womb here.

In a few verses Jacob will tell Rachel that God has withheld children from her. Later, God opens Rachel’s womb too.

So yes. The Bible puts children in God’s hands. Sarah was right about that. But be careful with what it does not mean.

It does not mean God punished Rachel for being pretty or for being loved.

It does not mean anyone struggling to have children today is being punished.

And it certainly does not mean Sarah’s plan to fix it with Hagar was a good one. God holds the womb.

He does not explain every reason.

And understand what children meant to a woman in this world, because it is more than wanting a baby.

Sons were your security. If your husband died, sons kept you fed and protected.

Sons worked the flocks, carried the family name, defended the household, and inherited. A woman with sons had standing.

A woman without them could feel like she had failed at the one job everyone expected of her.

Later in this chapter, when Rachel finally has a son, she will say God has taken away her *reproach*, her shame.

That is the pressure. It was not fair, and it did not make a woman worth less in God’s eyes, but it was real.

## Surely Now You Will Love Me

> “And Leah conceived, and bare a son, and she called his name Reuben: for she said, Surely the LORD hath looked upon my affliction; now therefore my husband will love me.” (Genesis 29:32)

The first son. And I want you to feel the weight of this. Not Leah’s first son. Jacob’s first son. The firstborn.

The whole stolen blessing story turned on what a firstborn meant in this family.

Esau sold it for stew and then wept over it.

And now the man who took the firstborn’s place has a firstborn of his own, and the unloved wife is the one who gave him to him.

Jacob is in his mid eighties. He has waited a long time for this.

*Affliction* means suffering, misery, deep distress. Not sickness, not poverty. The pain of being unwanted.

And the name Reuben means See, a son. Look, a son. Hear what she is saying. Look at what I gave you. Your first boy.

Surely now you will love me. How could this not bring us closer?

That is the hope of a woman who thinks she can earn her way into a heart. But I think you already know.

A baby can make a man proud. A baby cannot make a man love his wife.

> “And she conceived again, and bare a son; and said, Because the LORD hath heard that I was hated, he hath therefore given me this son also: and she called his name Simeon.” (Genesis 29:33)

Second son. Simeon means hearing, heard. With Reuben, God saw her. With Simeon, God heard her.

She is still telling us she is hated, which means nothing has changed in the tent.

And she is still, I think, hoping that this boy will do what the first one did not.

Before we go on, do not picture these births as one right after another.

The Bible lists them fast, but each one is a pregnancy, a birth, a baby nursed.

These four sons cover years of Leah’s life, and years of Rachel watching.

> “And she conceived again, and bare a son; and said, Now this time will my husband be joined unto me, because I have born him three sons: therefore was his name called Levi.” (Genesis 29:34)

Third son. Levi means joined, attached. And listen to her. Now this time. We have a family now. Three sons.

Noah had three sons and that was a whole new world. Surely now you will come close to me.

She has done everything a wife in that world was supposed to do, and more than most.

And she is still standing at the door of his heart, waiting to be let in.

## Now Will I Praise the LORD

> “And she conceived again, and bare a son: and she said, Now will I praise the LORD: therefore she called his name Judah; and left bearing.” (Genesis 29:35)

Fourth son. And something shifts. Judah means praise. Not prays. Praise. And look at who she is talking about.

Reuben was about her husband. Simeon was about her husband. Levi was about her husband. Judah is about the LORD.

Now will I praise the LORD. No mention of Jacob at all.

I love this progression, and I want you to see it as one story. God sees me. God hears me.

Maybe now my husband will hold on to me. And then, Now I will praise the LORD.

I am not saying she stopped wanting her husband’s love. Later in this chapter she will show you she never stopped.

But something in her turned toward the One who had actually been seeing her and hearing her the whole time.

That is the emotional story hidden inside a list of baby names.

The tribe of Judah, the tribe of King David, the tribe of Jesus, is named for the moment an unloved woman decided to praise God anyway.

*Left bearing* means she stopped having children for a while. Not forever. She will have more.

But for now, the births stop, and the camera turns to the other tent.

## Give Me Children, or Else I Die

> “And when Rachel saw that she bare Jacob no children, Rachel envied her sister; and said unto Jacob, Give me children, or else I die.” (Genesis 30:1)

Now Rachel. She has the thing Leah would give anything for. Jacob’s love.

And she has watched her sister carry and deliver four sons while her own arms stayed empty.

*Envied* is the Hebrew word qana, and it is not admiration. It is a painful, burning want for what someone else has.

Imagine what those years could have sounded like. A birth, and a glance across the camp. Another birth. A remark.

Another. Rachel loved, Leah fruitful, and each one wanting to trade.

*Give me children, or else I die.* That is not a medical forecast. That is a woman at the end of herself.

I hear her saying, I cannot bear this. What am I for, if not this?

And I hear something aimed at Jacob too: why can you have children with her and not with me?

If you remember Sarah, ninety years old before she held Isaac, you know this pain has been in this family for three generations.

Do not laugh at Rachel. Her sister has four sons and she has none.

> “And Jacob’s anger was kindled against Rachel: and he said, Am I in God’s stead, who hath withheld from thee the fruit of the womb?” (Genesis 30:2)

*Anger was kindled* is the Hebrew picture of a fire catching. His temper flares.

*Am I in God’s stead* means, am I in God’s place? Am I God? *The fruit of the womb* means children.

So Jacob says, you are demanding something I cannot give. God is the one who has withheld this, not me.

And he is right. God holds this.

But being right is not the same as being kind, and shouting the truth at a grieving woman does not comfort her.

Jacob answers her pain with a theology lesson and a raised voice.

I think he had heard this before, and it had been building. The Bible does not count the conversations.

It just shows us the fire.

## Rachel Is Reading From Sarah’s Script

> “And she said, Behold my maid Bilhah, go in unto her; and she shall bear upon my knees, that I may also have children by her.” (Genesis 30:3)

If you studied The Obedience of Abraham with me, this should sound very familiar.

In Genesis 16, Sarah could not have children, so she gave her servant Hagar to Abraham to have a child through her.

The Hebrew behind *have children by her* is the same idea Sarah used, be built up through her.

Rachel is reading from Sarah’s script.

For those just joining us, here is the essential: a childless wife, a handmaid given to the husband, a child born, and a household that was never the same afterward.

That is where this road led last time.

*Bear upon my knees* is the language of claiming. The child Bilhah bears will be received as Rachel’s.

Some picture Bilhah giving birth with Rachel supporting her at the knees.

Some picture the baby being laid on Rachel’s lap right after. Either way, the meaning is clear.

Rachel will name the child and count him as hers. But understand, Bilhah is still his mother.

She carries him, she delivers him, and the Bible keeps calling her his mother. She is not a tool.

She is a woman with no say in any of this, pulled into a fight between two sisters.

> “And she gave him Bilhah her handmaid to wife: and Jacob went in unto her. And Bilhah conceived, and bare Jacob a son. And Rachel said, God hath judged me, and hath also heard my voice, and hath given me a son: therefore called she his name Dan.” (Genesis 30:4–6)

Fifth son, and the first one Rachel can call hers. Dan means judged. But not judged as in condemned.

*God hath judged me* means God has ruled in my favor, taken my side, answered my case. And did you catch it?

*Hath also heard my voice.* That is what Leah said about Simeon. God heard her, too.

Both sisters are sure God is listening to them. And He is. That does not mean He signed off on every move they made.

> “And Bilhah Rachel’s maid conceived again, and bare Jacob a second son. And Rachel said, With great wrestlings have I wrestled with my sister, and I have prevailed: and she called his name Naphtali.” (Genesis 30:7–8)

Sixth son. Naphtali means my wrestling, my struggle. Rachel did not get in a physical fight with Leah.

The wrestling is the years of comparison, the contest she feels she has been in.

In Hebrew *great wrestlings* is literally wrestlings of God, a mighty struggle. And *I have prevailed* means I have won.

Won what? Leah has four, Rachel is counting two through Bilhah. It is not a scoreboard victory. It is how she feels.

She is in the game now. Keep that word prevailed in your pocket.

Jacob is going to hear it again, years from now, after a night of wrestling of his own.

## A Troop Cometh

> “When Leah saw that she had left bearing, she took Zilpah her maid, and gave her Jacob to wife. And Zilpah Leah’s maid bare Jacob a son. And Leah said, A troop cometh: and she called his name Gad.” (Genesis 30:9–11)

And now Leah does the same thing. She sees the births have stopped, and she hands Zilpah to Jacob.

Do you see how the rivalry spreads? It started between two sisters.

Now it has pulled in two servant girls who had no choice, and Jacob is father to children by four women under one roof.

Nobody planned a household like this. Comparison built it, one move at a time.

Seventh son. Gad. The King James has Leah say, *A troop cometh*. A troop is a band, a company, a crowd coming.

Many Bibles read her words as good fortune has come, and the name Gad is tied to fortune, luck.

Either way, hear her tone. Another one. Here comes more.

> “And Zilpah Leah’s maid bare Jacob a second son. And Leah said, Happy am I, for the daughters will call me blessed: and she called his name Asher.” (Genesis 30:12–13)

Eighth son. Asher means happy, blessed. *The daughters* means the other women, not Leah’s own girls.

She is saying, the women around here will look at me and call me fortunate. Remember what we said about standing.

Leah may not have her husband’s heart, but she has something the other women can see and count. Six sons under her name.

That is her happiness, and I will not take it from her.

## Eight Sons, Three Mothers

So let’s count. Reuben, Simeon, Levi, Judah, from Leah. Dan and Naphtali, from Bilhah. Gad and Asher, from Zilpah.

Eight sons, three mothers. And Rachel, the loved wife, still has no child of her own body.

Every one of these boys is going to become a tribe of Israel.

The nation God promised Abraham, as many as the dust, is starting right here, in these tents, in the middle of this.

Now here is the thing I want you to take from Day 13. Leah wanted the love Rachel had.

Rachel wanted the children Leah had.

Each one was holding something the other would have given anything for, and neither could enjoy it, because she was looking across the camp at her sister.

Comparison does that. It takes a real gift and makes it feel like nothing. And it does not stay in your own heart.

It spread to Bilhah and Zilpah, who were handed over like chess pieces.

It is going to spread to these eight boys, born into a contest, who will grow up knowing which mother was loved.

Genuine pain, left unchecked, turned into a rivalry that pulled in everyone.

So ask yourself honestly.

Is there someone whose life you want so badly that you have stopped seeing the good in your own?

Their marriage, their children, their money, their ease.

And while you are staring at what they have, is there something in your own tent you have stopped thanking God for?

The LORD saw Leah. He heard Rachel. He sees you too.

The question is whether you will let Him show you what you are holding.

Tomorrow, little Reuben goes out to the wheat field and comes back with something that sets off a bargain between the sisters, and we finally get to see what Rachel has to trade.

For now, take the reflection questions first, then today’s action, and end your day in prayer.

## What To Watch For In Genesis 29-30

Today’s reading runs across a chapter break, from chapter 29 verse 31 to chapter 30 verse 13.

Eighteen verses, and eight baby boys.

Every son gets a name, and every name gets a reason from the mouth of the woman who named him.

Read those reasons slowly. They are a window into her heart.

Keep count of the mothers. By the end there are three women having Jacob’s children, and one woman who is not.

**As you read, notice:**

* 💔 The word the Bible uses for Leah in chapter 29 verse 31
* 🙌 Who Leah talks about in her first three names, and who she talks about in the fourth
* 👶 What Rachel demands in chapter 30 verse 1
* 💡 Whose idea Bilhah is in verse 3
* 🔁 What Leah does when she sees she has stopped bearing in verse 9

And think about this question: have you ever wanted someone else’s life so badly that you stopped seeing the good in your own?

## Reflection Questions

1. **Leah had a place in Jacob’s house but not in his heart. Where in your life do you have a place but not a welcome?** A job, a family, a church, a marriage. Name it. Then remember who saw Leah.
2. **Each sister wanted what the other had. Whose life are you measuring yours against right now?** Write their name. Then write what you have that they do not. Be specific.
3. **Leah went from Now my husband will love me to Now will I praise the LORD. What would it take for you to make that turn?** Not giving up on what you want. Just turning toward the One who has been seeing and hearing you the whole time.

## Today's Prayer

Father,

You saw Leah. Nobody else did. Her father used her, her husband did not want her, her sister had what she longed for.

And You looked at the unwanted wife and opened her womb and let her name four sons after what You were doing for her.

Thank You that You see the people nobody picks.

Thank You that You hear the ones who cry out in the dark of their own tent.

You have seen me on the days I felt second, and You did not look away.

Open my eyes to what I am holding, Lord, instead of what someone else has.

Forgive me for the comparing. For the glances across the camp.

For the gifts You gave me that I have treated like nothing because they were not the gift I wanted.

Show me my own tent and let me be grateful in it.

And when I have done everything I know to do and the heart I want is still closed to me, turn me the way You turned Leah.

Not away from hope. Toward You. Now will I praise the LORD.

In Jesus’ name, amen.
