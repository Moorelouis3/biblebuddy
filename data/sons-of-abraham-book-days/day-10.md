Yesterday we left Jacob on the road. His brother wants him dead. His mother packed him off to her family in Haran.

His father blessed him and told him to find a wife among his cousins.

And then the door of the tent closed behind him, and the richest household in the land kept living without him.

Think about who this man is. He is the grandson of Abraham. He has the birthright, bought with a bowl of stew.

He has the blessing, taken with a goatskin and a lie.

He has heard about God his whole life, from his father, from his mother, from the old stories of a grandfather who talked with God face to face.

But here is the thing I want you to notice. Up to this moment, God has never spoken to Jacob. Not once.

He has Abraham’s God and Isaac’s God. He does not yet have a God of his own.

Tonight that changes.

Somewhere on a long road north, with the sun gone down and a stone under his head, heaven opens over a runaway.

There is a ladder. There are angels. And there is a voice that says, I am the God of your father Abraham.

Now I am talking to you.

Today we are going to watch a man meet God for himself. Let’s read it.

# Meeting God Himself

> “Surely the LORD is in this place; and I knew it not.” (Genesis 28:16)

Let’s start where the Bible starts, with a man walking away from home.

> “And Jacob went out from Beersheba, and went toward Haran.” (Genesis 28:10)

Two place names, and both carry family history. Beersheba is where Isaac’s household lives.

If you walked with us through The Obedience of Abraham, you have been here before.

Abraham dug a well here and made a treaty with a king.

Hagar wandered in this wilderness with Ishmael when the water ran out.

Isaac settled here, and God appeared to him at this very place. This is home ground.

Haran is the other end of the family.

Abraham’s father Terah led the family out of Ur and settled in Haran, and Abraham was seventy five when he left Haran for Canaan.

Abraham’s brother Nahor stayed, and his grandchildren are still there. Rebekah came from Haran.

Her brother Laban still lives there.

So Jacob is walking back along the road his grandfather came down, toward the relatives his grandfather left behind.

And it is a long road, hundreds of miles north. Weeks of walking.

Now, what did this trip look like?

When Abraham sent his servant to Haran to find Isaac a wife, the servant went with ten camels and gold and silver and a company of men.

That was the family’s style. But look at Jacob.

Years later, coming back down this road, he says to God, *with my staff I passed over this Jordan*. A walking stick.

And tonight he sleeps on the ground with a rock under his head.

I do not think he left with nothing at all, but the picture is not a rich man’s caravan.

It is a man on foot, a long way from his father’s tents and his father’s servants.

The grandson of the richest man in the land, and he cannot even get a pillow.

## A Ladder Set Up on the Earth

> “And he lighted upon a certain place, and tarried there all night, because the sun was set; and he took of the stones of that place, and put them for his pillows, and lay down in that place to sleep.” (Genesis 28:11)

*Lighted upon* just means came to, arrived at. He did not light a fire. *Tarried* means stayed.

The sun went down and he stopped where he was. No tent, no inn, no family. A certain place.

The Bible does not even name it yet, because to Jacob it was nothing. Just the spot where the daylight ran out.

And do not assume this was his first night out. This place is a good distance north of Beersheba.

The Bible skips the travel and lands us on the one night that mattered.

> “And he dreamed, and behold a ladder set up on the earth, and the top of it reached to heaven: and behold the angels of God ascending and descending on it.” (Genesis 28:12)

Here is a little nugget for you.

The Hebrew word for ladder here is sullam , and it appears exactly once in the whole Old Testament. Right here.

Nowhere else. The King James says ladder.

Many scholars think stairway or ramp fits better, like the great stepped towers of the ancient east.

It is the same Hebrew word either way, just two ways of putting it into English.

I am not going to tell you the King James got it wrong. What matters is the picture, and that part is clear.

Earth at the bottom. Heaven at the top. Something joining them.

And angels, plural, going up and coming down, busy between the two.

This runaway lying in the dirt is being shown that heaven is not shut.

There is traffic between God’s world and his, right over the spot where he fell asleep.

## The LORD God of Abraham

> “And, behold, the LORD stood above it, and said, I am the LORD God of Abraham thy father, and the God of Isaac: the land whereon thou liest, to thee will I give it, and to thy seed;” (Genesis 28:13)

Now God speaks. And listen to how He introduces Himself. *The LORD God of Abraham thy father.*

Abraham was Jacob’s grandfather, not his father.

This is the verse I told you about on Day 1, and here it is in the story.

In the Bible, father can mean ancestor, and son can mean descendant.

Keep that in your pocket for the whole book, because when we read that a people are the sons of Abraham, this is what it means.

They came from him.

And look at what God promises. The land. The exact ground Jacob is lying on. To you, and to your seed, your descendants.

This is the same promise God made to Abraham and repeated to Isaac.

Now it comes to the third generation, spoken by God Himself.

## As the Dust of the Earth

> “And thy seed shall be as the dust of the earth, and thou shalt spread abroad to the west, and to the east, and to the north, and to the south: and in thee and in thy seed shall all the families of the earth be blessed.” (Genesis 28:14)

Do you hear the echoes? God told Abraham his seed would be like the dust of the earth.

He took him outside and told him to count the stars. On the mountain, after the knife, He said sand on the seashore.

Dust, stars, sand. Now dust again, to the grandson. Same God, same promise, same family.

West, east, north, south. That is not a command to go conquer the world.

It is a picture of a family spreading out in every direction, growing until it cannot be counted.

Which is exactly what this book is about.

And then the biggest line of all. *In thee and in thy seed shall all the families of the earth be blessed.*

God said that to Abraham the very first time He called him. He said it again to Isaac.

Now Jacob hears it with his own ears. This promise is going to reach all the way to Jesus.

And it is being handed to a man who lied to his blind father a few days ago.

Before we go on, I want to put two blessings side by side. When Isaac sent Jacob off, he prayed this over him:

> “And God Almighty bless thee, and make thee fruitful, and multiply thee, that thou mayest be a multitude of people; And give thee the blessing of Abraham, to thee, and to thy seed with thee; that thou mayest inherit the land wherein thou art a stranger, which God gave unto Abraham.” (Genesis 28:3–4)

Descendants and land. That was his father’s prayer. Now compare it with verses 13 and 14. Descendants and land.

The same thing. The first time I read this I thought, God is adding to it, making it bigger. But that is not quite it.

Isaac did not invent this blessing and God improve on it. God made this promise to Abraham first. Isaac passed it on.

Now God shows up in person to confirm that what the father prayed is exactly what He intends to do.

The blessing Jacob took by trickery, God hands to him straight.

## I Am With Thee

Then God adds something Isaac could not give.

> “And, behold, I am with thee, and will keep thee in all places whither thou goest, and will bring thee again into this land; for I will not leave thee, until I have done that which I have spoken to thee of.” (Genesis 28:15)

Four promises, and they are all personal. I am with you. I will keep you wherever you go.

I will bring you back to this land. I will not leave you until I have done everything I said.

A father can pray for descendants and land. Only God can say, I am coming with you.

Now I have to be honest about a question that comes up here, because I have asked it myself.

If God says I am with you and I will keep you, why is there still so much suffering ahead?

Jacob is going to be cheated for twenty years by his own uncle. His family is going to end up slaves in Egypt.

People who belong to God lose children, lose homes, get sick, get hurt. So what does I am with thee actually mean?

Here is what it does not mean. It does not mean a pain free life. God never promised Jacob that.

Back when He cut the covenant with Abraham, God said plainly that his seed would be strangers in a land not theirs and afflicted for four hundred years, and that He would bring them out afterward.

The hard years were in the plan from the start. What God promises is presence and faithfulness. I will be there.

I will not leave. I will finish what I said.

That is a different promise from nothing bad will happen to you, and I think it is a better one.

I cannot explain every loss.

I can tell you that the God who spoke on this ladder kept every word of verse 15, and that you can bring Him your hard questions without being scolded for asking.

## Surely the LORD Is in This Place

> “And Jacob awaked out of his sleep, and he said, Surely the LORD is in this place; and I knew it not.” (Genesis 28:16)

This is my key verse for the day, and I want you to feel it. Jacob picked this spot because the sun went down.

That is all. An ordinary patch of ground, a rock for his head. And God was there. God had been there the whole time.

Abraham had built an altar near this place generations earlier, and Jacob walked right onto it without knowing.

*I knew it not.* He was not looking for God. God found him.

> “And he was afraid, and said, How dreadful is this place! this is none other but the house of God, and this is the gate of heaven.” (Genesis 28:17)

Now, that word *afraid* can trip up a King James reader, so let me explain it.

The Hebrew word is yare, and *dreadful* comes from the same root.

Together they describe someone overwhelmed, shaken to the core by how great and holy God is.

This is not Adam hiding in the bushes after he ate the fruit, waiting to be punished.

Jacob has not just done something wrong here. He has just seen heaven.

This is the fear of a man who realizes he has been lying on holy ground in his sandals. Reverence. Awe.

Wonder so strong it makes your knees weak. There is real fear in it, do not strip that out.

But it is the fear of being near something enormous and good, not the panic of being caught.

And here is a thought to sit with.

If he had known the place was holy, would he have lain down so casually, tossed a rock under his head, gone to sleep without a prayer?

Most of us have walked over holy ground without noticing. The question is what we do once we know.

House of God. Gate of heaven. Jacob is not saying God is trapped in this one field.

He is saying this is where God made Himself known, where the way between heaven and earth stood open. So he marks it.

## House of God

> “And Jacob rose up early in the morning, and took the stone that he had put for his pillows, and set it up for a pillar, and poured oil upon the top of it. And he called the name of that place Bethel: but the name of that city was called Luz at the first.” (Genesis 28:18–19)

A pillar here is a standing stone, set upright as a marker. It is not an altar.

Altars are built to offer sacrifices on, and the family has built plenty of those already.

A pillar says, something happened here, remember it. The oil marks the stone and the place as set apart, holy.

He is not feeding God or opening a magic door. He is saying, this is where I met You.

And he names it. Bethel is two Hebrew words, Beth, house, and El, God. House of God.

The town nearby had been called Luz, which means almond tree. From now on, in Jacob’s family, this place has a new name.

Later in the story, God will call him back here, and he will build an altar on this spot. But that is for another day.

## Then Shall the LORD Be My God

Now the vow. Read it carefully.

> “And Jacob vowed a vow, saying, If God will be with me, and will keep me in this way that I go, and will give me bread to eat, and raiment to put on, So that I come again to my father’s house in peace; then shall the LORD be my God: And this stone, which I have set for a pillar, shall be God’s house: and of all that thou shalt give me I will surely give the tenth unto thee.” (Genesis 28:20–22)

A vow in the Bible, the Hebrew word neder, is a solemn promise made to God. Not a wish. A man binding himself.

And that little word *if* is really there. Some people read it as pure bargaining.

Some read it more like since, a response of faith. Here is how I read it. God spoke first. God made the promises.

Jacob did not pay anything to get them.

Now Jacob answers: if You do what You just said, keep me, feed me, bring me home, then You are my God, and here is what I will give back.

It is not perfect, mature faith. It is not cold bargaining either.

It is a man taking God at His word and committing himself for the first time. I am with it.

Look at what he asks for. *Bread to eat* means food, plain daily food. *Raiment* means clothes.

Remember Esau’s clothes that Rebekah put on Jacob back in chapter 27? That is the same word.

Jacob is not asking for riches. He is asking for the basics on a dangerous road.

And *my father’s house in peace* means Isaac’s household back in Canaan.

He is going toward Haran, but his heart is already asking to come home from it.

*Then shall the LORD be my God.* This is the center of the whole day.

In the ancient world there were many gods, and every household had to decide which one it would serve.

Up to now the LORD has been his father’s God and his grandfather’s God, a God Jacob inherited like he inherited the tents.

Tonight he makes it personal. My God. The one I worship. The one I depend on. The one I belong to.

And he gives two things back. The stone will be God’s house, a place of worship.

And *the tenth*, one out of every ten, ten percent of everything God gives him, goes back to God.

This is not the first time a tenth shows up in Genesis.

Abraham gave Melchizedek a tenth of everything after the battle of the kings.

So this is a family way of honoring God with what you have. It is not a church rule being announced.

Nobody tells Jacob who to hand it to or how.

It is simply a man saying, whatever You give me, a tenth of it is Yours, because it was Yours first.

So here is what I want you to take out of Day 10. Jacob had the stories. He had the blessing.

He had religious parents and a holy grandfather. And none of that was the same as meeting God.

God came to him on an ordinary night in an ordinary field, spoke the family promise straight into his ears, and promised to go with him.

And Jacob answered. The God of his fathers became his God. That is the step every one of us has to take.

Your parents’ faith can bring you to the field. It cannot lie down there for you.

Tomorrow Jacob gets up from that stone and finishes the walk to Haran.

He is going to come to a well, and a girl is going to show up with her father’s sheep.

For now, take the reflection questions first, then today’s action, and end your day in prayer.

## What To Watch For In Genesis 28

Thirteen verses, and almost every one of them is famous. Read them slowly anyway.

Most of us have heard about Jacob’s ladder, but very few of us have sat with what God actually says on it.

There are three parts. A journey and a dream. A promise from God. And a vow from Jacob.

Watch how the promise and the vow line up with each other.

Keep your eye on one small word near the end. The word *if*. We are going to talk about it.

**As you read, notice:**

* 🧭 Where Jacob leaves from and where he is headed in verse 10
* 👑 Who God says He is in verse 13
* 🤝 Four things God promises in verse 15
* 🪨 What Jacob does with the stone in verse 18
* 🎁 What Jacob promises to give in verse 22

And think about this question: what is the difference between knowing about God and meeting Him?

## Reflection Questions

1. **Jacob had his father’s God and his grandfather’s God. When did the LORD become your God, not just your family’s?** If you are not sure it has happened yet, say so honestly. That is where Jacob was at the start of today.
2. **Jacob said, the LORD is in this place, and I knew it not. Where has God been present in your life while you were not paying attention?** Think of an ordinary place or season that turned out to be holy ground. What did you walk past?
3. **God promised to be with Jacob, not to spare him from hard years. Which of those two things have you been expecting from God?** Be honest about the difference. Then think about what I am with thee is actually worth.

## Today's One Thing

**Set Up a Stone**

Jacob marked the place where God met him and made a vow in plain words. Do both today.

1. Write down one place and one moment when God was real to you. The street, the room, the year. Your own Bethel.
2. Under it, write your own vow in one sentence that starts with the words, The LORD shall be my God. Then add one thing you will give back to Him, a tenth or something else, and say it plainly.
3. Find a small stone and put it somewhere you will see it every day this week. When you see it, remember verse 15: I am with thee.

One place, one vow, one stone. Do it, and check it off.

## Today's Prayer

Father,

You found Jacob in a field he did not pick on purpose, with a rock under his head and nothing to offer You.

You opened heaven over a runaway.

You spoke the promise You gave his grandfather straight into his ears and told him You were coming with him.

Thank You that You do not wait for me to be ready. You were in the places I walked through without noticing.

You were there, and I knew it not.

Be my God, Lord. Not only the God of my parents or my church. Mine.

Keep me in the way that I go. Give me bread to eat and clothes to wear and bring me home in peace.

And when the road is harder than I expected, remind me that You never promised an easy road.

You promised to be on it with me.

Everything I have, You gave me first. Teach me to give back, gladly, the way Jacob did at the stone.

In Jesus’ name, amen.
