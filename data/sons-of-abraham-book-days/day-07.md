Yesterday ended with Jacob standing in his mother’s tent, dressed in his brother’s clothes, goat skins on his hands and neck, a hot dish in his hands.

The plan was argued over and settled. The only thing left was to walk in.

Today he walks in.

I want you to feel how close this comes to falling apart. Isaac is blind, but he is not stupid.

He has known these two sons for seventy seven years. He knows how they walk, how they talk, how they smell.

Within a few sentences he is suspicious, and he starts testing. He asks questions. He reaches out his hands.

He pulls his son close.

Every test is a place where the whole thing could collapse, and every test Jacob has to answer with another lie.

Count the lies today. There are more than you think.

And watch the moment where Jacob reaches for the most powerful word he knows to make his father stop asking questions.

He uses God’s name.

Then, at the end, the old man speaks. And the words he says over the son in front of him are not small words.

They are the biggest words a father in this family can say, and they have been waiting since Abraham.

Isaac thinks he is speaking them over Esau.

# The Voice and the Hands

> “The voice is Jacob’s voice, but the hands are the hands of Esau.” (Genesis 27:22)

The meal is ready. The clothes are on. The skins are tied to his hands and neck.

Jacob has everything his mother gave him, and now he goes to his father.

> “And he came unto his father, and said, My father: and he said, Here am I; who art thou, my son?” (Genesis 27:18)

Stop on that question. *Who art thou, my son?* A father with working eyes never has to ask that.

Isaac asks it because he cannot see, and because something is already a little off. Esau left for the field.

Now someone is standing in his tent with food.

The very first thing Isaac says is a test, and Jacob has not even opened his mouth yet.

> “And Jacob said unto his father, I am Esau thy firstborn; I have done according as thou badest me: arise, I pray thee, sit and eat of my venison, that thy soul may bless me.” (Genesis 27:19)

Count them. *I am Esau.* That is one. *Thy firstborn.* He is the second born, so that is two.

*I have done according as thou badest me.* He did not go to the field, he went to the flock, so that is three.

*My venison.* It is goat. Four lies in one breath, and the scene is one verse old.

Notice he ends with his father’s own words. *That thy soul may bless me.*

On Day 6 we heard Isaac say *that my soul may bless thee*, and we heard Rebekah report it to Jacob as *before the LORD*.

So how does Jacob know the soul phrase? I think the answer is simple.

The few lines the Bible records of Rebekah’s report are not the whole conversation, and in any case this is how fathers in that household talked about blessing their sons.

Jacob had heard it his whole life. He knew what to say to sound like the son his father was waiting for.

## The LORD Thy God

> “And Isaac said unto his son, How is it that thou hast found it so quickly, my son? And he said, Because the LORD thy God brought it to me.” (Genesis 27:20)

Here is the first crack. Remember what we said yesterday about hunting. It is not a walk to the flock.

You go out, you look, you track, you wait, you get close, you shoot, you carry it home, you clean it, you cook it.

That takes hours at the least. Esau has barely been gone, and here is dinner.

Isaac does the math and asks the obvious question. *How did you find it so fast?*

And Jacob reaches for the biggest word he has. *Because the LORD thy God brought it to me.*

I want to sit on this, because it is the lowest moment in the scene. Jacob is saying that God did this.

God put the animal in my path. God is the reason the timing is strange.

He takes a blind old man’s faith, the thing Isaac trusts more than anything, and uses it as a tool to shut down the question.

If I say God did it, you will stop asking.

Notice the exact words. *The LORD thy God.* Not my God. Your God.

I do not want to read too much into that, because Jacob certainly knew the God of his father, and in the next chapter God is going to meet him personally.

But in this moment he is leaning on his father’s relationship with God, not his own, to make a lie believable.

He is not swearing an oath here. He does not say, as the LORD lives.

But he is dragging God’s name into the deception and standing it up next to his lie so the lie will look holy.

That is a sin with its own weight, separate from the costume. You can lie to a person.

Jacob is lying about God, to a person who loves God.

## The Voice Is Jacob’s Voice

> “And Isaac said unto Jacob, Come near, I pray thee, that I may feel thee, my son, whether thou be my very son Esau or not.” (Genesis 27:21)

The answer did not satisfy him. *Come near, that I may feel thee.* Very here does not mean extremely.

*My very son Esau* means really my son Esau, truly Esau.

Isaac is saying, I need to find out if you are actually who you say you are.

Does he suspect? I think he does.

The timing is wrong and something in the voice is wrong, and a man who cannot see learns to trust what he can touch.

So he does the one thing Jacob was afraid of. Go back to yesterday. *My father peradventure will feel me.*

That was Jacob’s whole objection, the hole he found in the plot. And now it happens. Isaac reaches out.

> “And Jacob went near unto Isaac his father; and he felt him, and said, The voice is Jacob’s voice, but the hands are the hands of Esau.” (Genesis 27:22)

That is the key verse for today, and it is one of the saddest sentences in Genesis.

Isaac has two witnesses and they disagree. His ears tell him Jacob. His hands tell him Esau.

These twins never sounded alike. Isaac knows that voice.

But his fingers are running over goat hair on the back of a hand, and Esau has been hairy since the day he was born, and the hands are saying what the voice is not.

He is torn. He is not pretending.

He is an old blind man holding his son’s hand and trying to figure out which of his own senses to believe.

## The Disguise Worked

> “And he discerned him not, because his hands were hairy, as his brother Esau’s hands: so he blessed him. And he said, Art thou my very son Esau? And he said, I am.” (Genesis 27:23–24)

Discerned him not means he did not recognize him for who he really was. In plain words, the disguise worked.

Rebekah had thought of the exact test and built the exact answer. The skins did their job.

But look, he asks one more time. *Art thou my very son Esau?*

Even after the hands, even after he has started to bless, the voice is still nagging at him. And Jacob says, *I am.*

Two words. Lie number five. Every time the old man gives him a chance to come clean, he goes deeper.

This is what I want you to see about lying. It is never one. A lie is like a hole in a tent in the rain.

It has to be patched, and the patch has to be patched.

Jacob walked in with one lie, I am Esau, and within six verses he has told it five different ways and pulled God into it.

That is not because Jacob is unusually wicked. It is because that is how lies work. The first one decides the rest.

## The Smell of My Son

> “And he said, Bring it near to me, and I will eat of my son’s venison, that my soul may bless thee. And he brought it near to him, and he did eat: and he brought him wine, and he drank. And his father Isaac said unto him, Come near now, and kiss me, my son.” (Genesis 27:25–26)

Now it slows down, and it gets tender, which makes it worse. Isaac eats.

He eats goat and believes it is wild game, because his wife of nearly a hundred years knows exactly how he likes it.

Jacob brings him wine. And then the father asks for a kiss.

Put yourself in Jacob’s skin. You are standing over your blind father in your brother’s clothes.

He has just eaten the meal you lied about.

And now he wants you to come close and kiss him, the way a son kisses a father before a solemn moment.

You have to lean in. You have to let him hold you. There is no distance left to hide in.

> “And he came near, and kissed him: and he smelled the smell of his raiment, and blessed him, and said, See, the smell of my son is as the smell of a field which the LORD hath blessed:” (Genesis 27:27)

Raiment means clothing. It is not remnant, it is raiment, the garments.

Remember the goodly raiment from yesterday, Esau’s best clothes that Rebekah kept in the house and put on Jacob.

When Isaac pulls his son in to kiss him, he breathes in. And what he smells is Esau.

The fields, the open country, the animals, the sweat of the hunter. The clothes smell like the son he loves.

So look at how the whole disguise works together. The food fooled his taste. The skins fooled his touch.

The clothes fooled his nose.

Three senses satisfied, and the only one left arguing is his hearing, and he has decided to overrule it.

The last doubt goes out of him. *The smell of my son is as the smell of a field which the LORD hath blessed.*

And he begins.

## The Dew of Heaven

> “Therefore God give thee of the dew of heaven, and the fatness of the earth, and plenty of corn and wine:” (Genesis 27:28)

Here is the blessing we talked about on Day 6, the leading blessing, the father naming the next head of the family and praying over him.

Let’s take it piece by piece, because every line is concrete.

*The dew of heaven.* Yes, he means dew, the moisture that settles on the grass in the early morning.

In a dry country like Canaan, that moisture is life. Months can go by without rain.

The dew is what keeps the grazing alive and the crops from burning. Isaac is asking God for water for his son’s land.

*The fatness of the earth.* Not literal fat. Rich, fertile ground that produces. Think about Isaac’s own story.

He planted in a famine year and reaped a hundredfold, because God blessed him.

He knows what fat land looks like and he knows what a hungry year looks like.

He is asking for the good kind for his son.

*Plenty of corn and wine.*

Corn in the King James Bible means grain, wheat and barley, not the yellow corn on the cob we think of. Grain is bread.

It is the household eating. It is feed for the animals and something to trade. And wine is simply fermented grape juice.

I have heard people say ancient people drank wine because they could not drink the water, but that is not quite right.

They drank water too. Wine was vineyards, harvest, something to put on the table when a guest came.

The Psalms say wine *maketh glad the heart of man*. Isaac is asking for a full table and a glad house.

## Let People Serve Thee

> “Let people serve thee, and nations bow down to thee: be lord over thy brethren, and let thy mother’s sons bow down to thee: cursed be every one that curseth thee, and blessed be he that blesseth thee.” (Genesis 27:29)

Now it moves from the land to the people, and this is the part that reaches far past one man’s comfortable life.

*Let people serve thee, and nations bow down to thee.*

Isaac is praying for strength and standing among the peoples around them. Think about what he has just lived through.

On Day 5 the Philistines envied him, stopped up his wells, pushed him out of their land, and then came to his camp asking for a treaty because they could see God was with him.

Isaac knows how much it matters whether your neighbors fear you or push you around.

He is asking that his son’s family will be the one others have to reckon with.

*Be lord over thy brethren, and let thy mother’s sons bow down to thee.* This is the family headship.

The man being blessed will lead, and his brothers will follow.

And here is the irony that the whole chapter has been building toward. Isaac is saying this to Esau, he thinks.

He is telling Esau, you will be lord over Jacob. But the man under his hands is Jacob.

And God had told Rebekah before the boys were born, *the elder shall serve the younger*.

Isaac is pronouncing God’s own word with his own mouth, over the son God chose, while believing he is doing the opposite.

He did not pick Jacob. He never would have. And it still came out of his mouth.

*Cursed be every one that curseth thee, and blessed be he that blesseth thee.*

If you walked with us through The Obedience of Abraham, you have heard this before.

When God first called Abraham, He said, *“I will bless them that bless thee, and curse him that curseth thee” (Genesis 12:3)*.

Isaac is handing that down.

Whoever stands with this son stands with God’s promise, and whoever stands against him is standing against it.

This is the covenant moving to the next generation, exactly like we said on Day 6.

It is not a license to curse people you do not like. It is God’s protection wrapped around the line of the promise.

## Esau Is Still Out There

That is the blessing.

Water, land, bread, wine, standing among the nations, leadership in the family, and God’s own protection.

Everything a patriarch could ask for the son who will carry the family after him. Isaac gave it with his whole soul.

And he gave it to the wrong son, who was the right son, who got it the wrong way.

So let me say what I think today is about. Jacob got what he came for. The disguise passed every test.

The father spoke every word. And none of that makes it right.

The Bible does not say, and Jacob was clever, and God was pleased.

It just shows you a blind man being lied to by his son, and then it keeps going.

God’s promise to Rebekah came true in this room, but not because of the goat skins. It would have come true anyway.

What the goat skins bought was a chain of lies, a father fooled, God’s name used as a cover, and a brother who is about to come home.

Because here is the thing. Esau is still out there.

He is out in the field with a bow, doing exactly what his father asked, and he does not know any of this happened.

Sooner or later he is going to walk into that tent with a plate of real venison and say, Father, I am here.

What happens then? That is tomorrow.

For now, take the reflection questions first, then today’s action, and end your day in prayer.

## What To Watch For In Genesis 27

This is a conversation between a blind father and a son pretending to be someone else.

Read it like a scene, not a summary. Hear every question Isaac asks.

Yesterday Jacob worried his father would feel his skin. Watch whether that fear comes true.

The blessing at the end, verses 27 to 29, is the center of this whole part of the book. Read it twice.

**As you read, notice:**

* 🔢 How many separate lies Jacob tells between verse 18 and verse 24
* ⏱️ What makes Isaac suspicious in verse 20, and how Jacob answers
* ✋ Which of Isaac’s senses he trusts in verse 22, and which one he overrules
* 🌾 The five different things Isaac asks for in verses 28 and 29

And think about this question: what does it cost to keep a lie alive once it has started?

## Reflection Questions

1. **Jacob walked in with one lie and needed five before his father stopped asking. Where in your life has one lie grown a second one?** Trace it back to the first one. That is the one that decided the rest.
2. **Because the LORD thy God brought it to me. Have you ever used God’s name, or God talk, to make something you wanted sound holy?** It can be as small as, I feel like God is telling me, when really you just want it. Be honest about one time.
3. **Isaac heard Jacob’s voice and chose to believe his hands. Where are you overruling what you know because of what you want to be true?** Name the voice you keep hearing and keep ignoring.

## Today's One Thing

**Set It Straight**

Jacob never took the way out his father kept offering him. Today, take yours.

1. Write down one false impression you are letting stand right now. Something you said that was not true, or something you let someone believe because it was easier.
2. Write down the next lie you would need if that person asked a hard question. Look at it. That is the chain starting.
3. Go to that person today, in person or by phone, and set it straight in plain words. No explaining it away. Just the truth and, if it is owed, an apology.

One false impression, one chain spotted, one conversation had. Do it, and check it off.

## Today's Prayer

Father,

You watched a son lie to his blind father and put Your name on the lie. You did not strike him down.

You kept Your promise anyway, and You kept working on him for the rest of his life.

I need that kind of patience, because I have done the same thing in smaller ways more times than I can count.

Forgive me for the lies I told to cover other lies.

For the times I said You wanted something when I was the one who wanted it.

For using Your name to make my plans sound holy.

Make me a person whose voice and hands say the same thing.

When someone gives me a chance to come clean, help me take it the first time, not the fifth.

Give me the courage to say, it was me, before anyone has to feel for the truth.

And thank You that Your blessing does not depend on my cleverness.

You said the younger would lead, and he did, and the goat skins had nothing to do with it.

Teach me to trust what You have said instead of trying to arrange it.

In Jesus’ name, amen.
