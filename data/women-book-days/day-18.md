She was probably a teenager. No army, no title, no wealth. And an angel showed up and asked her to carry the Son of God in her own body.

Mary said yes before she had a single answer to how it would work or what it would cost her.

Think about what that yes actually meant. An unmarried, pregnant girl in a small town where everyone would count the months. A fiance who could have walked away and been fully within his rights. Whispers she would carry for the rest of her life.

And that was only the beginning of the cost. Years later she would stand at the foot of a cross and watch that same son die a criminal’s death in front of her.

Yesterday you met the older woman who welcomed her. Today you meet the young woman who said yes to carrying the hope of the whole world, and paid for that yes all the way to a cross.

**Today we meet the woman God chose to carry His Son, and who trusted Him without understanding it all.**

## An Angel Comes to Nazareth

Mary was a young Jewish girl, most likely still a teenager, from Nazareth, a town so small that people from elsewhere joked about it. She was engaged to a carpenter named Joseph. Her family was poor; we know because later, at Jesus' dedication, they offered the two turtledoves the law allowed only for families who could not afford a lamb. God did not choose a princess or a priest's daughter to carry His Son into the world. He chose a poor teenage girl from a town nobody respected. That is a pattern you have watched for seventeen days now.

The angel Gabriel, fresh from Zacharias, came to her with a greeting that troubled her, *hail, thou that art highly favoured, the Lord is with thee*. Her first reaction was not excitement. It was fear and confusion, and the Bible does not hide it. Gabriel calmed her and told her why he had come: she would conceive and bear a son, Jesus, the Son of the Highest, who would reign on David's throne forever. Mary did not doubt God. But she asked an honest, practical question.

*“Then said Mary unto the angel, How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?” (Luke 1:34)*

A fair question from a girl not yet married. Gabriel answered plainly: the Holy Ghost would come upon her. This would be a miracle, not a normal pregnancy. And then Mary gave the answer that changed everything.

*“Behold the handmaid of the Lord; be it unto me according to thy word.” (Luke 1:38)*

One of the bravest sentences in the entire Bible. She had no idea yet how Joseph would react, what the neighbors would say, or what this child's life would eventually cost her. She said yes anyway. When fear makes you want to say no to something God is clearly asking, Mary felt that fear too. She moved forward regardless.

## A Song, and a Faithful Man’s

## Yes

Gabriel had told Mary that her old relative Elizabeth was six months pregnant, so Mary went to find her, the visit you read from Elizabeth's side yesterday. When Elizabeth's unborn son leaped and Elizabeth called her blessed, Mary answered with a song believers have sung for two thousand years.

*“My soul doth magnify the Lord, And my spirit hath rejoiced in God my Saviour. For he hath regarded the low estate of his handmaiden.” (Luke 1:46–48)*

Hear the second line. Mary calls God **my Saviour**. A woman about to give birth to the Savior of the world still needed saving herself, and she said so plainly. And notice how she describes herself: *the low estate* of His handmaiden. Not important. Not deserving. Poor and overlooked, and God still chose her. Her song, built on Hannah's prayer from five days ago, is about a God who lifts up the humble and fills the hungry. She understood exactly what kind of God had come to her.

But Mary's yes required someone else to have faith too. While she was engaged to Joseph, she was found to be pregnant, and Joseph knew the child was not his. By law he had every right to shame her publicly and end the engagement. Scripture tells us his character first: *being a just man, and not willing to make her a publick example*, he planned to put her away quietly. Then an angel reached him in a dream: fear not to take Mary, for the child is of the Holy Ghost, and He will save His people from their sins. Joseph obeyed immediately and took her as his wife. Mary's obedience could have cost her everything; Joseph chose to trust God over his own reputation, and protected her.

## A Manger, and a Sword

A Roman census forced the couple to travel from Nazareth to Bethlehem, Joseph's ancestral town, the same little town where Ruth gleaned and David was born. Mary made that hard journey great with child, and there she delivered her firstborn.

*“And she brought forth her firstborn son, and wrapped him in swaddling clothes, and laid him in a manger; because there was no room for them in the inn.” (Luke 2:7)*

No palace, no proper room. The King of kings was laid where animals fed, because there was nowhere else for His mother to lay Him. Shepherds came that night with a wild story about angels, and Mary's response was quiet and characteristic: *but Mary kept all these things, and pondered them in her heart*. She did not understand everything happening around her son. She held it and turned it over. That is often exactly what faith looks like in the middle of something you cannot explain.

When they brought Jesus to the temple to be dedicated, a devout old man named Simeon, promised he would see the Messiah before he died, took the baby in his arms and praised God. Then he turned to Mary and said something that must have landed like a stone.

*“(Yea, a sword shall pierce through thy own soul also,) that the thoughts of many hearts may be revealed.” (Luke 2:35)*

A sword through her own soul. Spoken to a new mother holding her newborn. It was true, and it came true. Watching this child's life would one day tear her heart open. Every mother who has loved a child and feared for them can find her own story in that verse. Mary carried that warning quietly for thirty years.

## At the Cross, and Beyond

The Gospels give us glimpses across the years. At a wedding in Cana, when the wine ran out, Mary turned to her grown son, and when He said His hour had not yet come, she simply told the servants her entire philosophy of life in seven words: *whatsoever he saith unto you, do it*. Those are the last recorded words of Mary in Scripture, and they are not about herself at all. They point every listener straight to her son. Jesus turned water into wine, His first miracle, and His disciples believed.

Then comes the hardest scene a mother can be given. Years after Cana, Mary stood near the cross where her son was being executed as a criminal. Simeon's sword had finally arrived, exactly as he had warned her decades before. And even while dying, Jesus looked down and thought of her future.

*“When Jesus therefore saw his mother, and the disciple standing by, whom he loved, he saith unto his mother, Woman, behold thy son! Then saith he to the disciple, Behold thy mother!” (John 19:26–27)*

In the middle of the worst suffering of His life, Jesus made sure His mother would be cared for, and John took her into his own home from that hour. Mary, told decades earlier that her soul would be pierced, watched it happen in the most public and painful way possible, and Jesus still provided for her before He died. And her story does not end at the cross. After the resurrection and ascension, Scripture gives us one last glimpse of Mary, gathered with the apostles in an upper room in Jerusalem, *with one accord in prayer and supplication*. The final picture of Mary is not grief. It is a woman still praying, still faithful, still with the believers, years after that first impossible yes.

Here is where her day lands on you. God often chooses the person nobody would expect; He did not need Mary's status, only her willing heart. Saying yes to God can cost you before it blesses you, and obedience is not a promise of an easy road, only of His presence on the hard one. You can trust God without understanding everything; twice Scripture says Mary pondered rather than fully grasped, and trusted before she understood. And faithful obedience often looks small and quiet, like a girl saying be it unto me, or a mother telling servants to do whatever He says, and then stepping back and pointing people to Him.

Tomorrow we move into Jesus' own ministry, to a woman He went out of His way to meet, a woman most of her town avoided, sitting at a well at noon. The Samaritan woman. But today is not finished yet. Take the reflection questions first, then today's action, and end your day in prayer.

## Reflection Questions

Take a few minutes and answer these honestly.

**Is there a yes I am refusing until I know the cost?**

1. Mary said be it unto me with almost no answers and every reason to be afraid. What is God asking of me that I keep stalling on until the risk feels safe? What would yes sound like tonight?

**Can I trust God in the parts I do not understand?**

2. Mary kept things in her heart and pondered them. She trusted before she grasped. What am I demanding to understand before I will obey? Could I ponder it and trust Him instead?

**Does my faith point to me or to Him?**

3. Mary’s last recorded words were whatsoever he saith unto you, do it. She pointed away from herself. In the places where I have any influence, am I drawing people to myself or to Jesus?

## Today's Action

## Whatsoever He Saith

Mary’s last recorded words were these: whatsoever he saith unto you, do it. Live one day by them.

**1. Write down the one thing you already know God is asking of you and have been slow to obey.**

**2. Do it this week, without first requiring the full picture, the way Mary said yes with the picture still blank.**

**3. Then point one person toward Jesus instead of yourself, in a real, specific way.**

One known yes, one obedient step, one person pointed to Him. Do it, and check it off.

## Today's Prayer

*Father,*

*You chose a poor teenage girl from a town nobody respected to carry the hope of the world, and she said yes before she knew a single thing it would cost her. You still work that way, through the overlooked and the unready. Work that way in me.*

*Give me Mary’s yes, Lord. I confess I keep stalling on what You ask until the risk feels manageable. Teach me to say be it unto me according to Your word while the picture is still blank.*

**Help me trust You in the parts I cannot understand. Let me ponder what I cannot explain and keep walking with You anyway.**

*Thank You that even a sword through the soul was not outside Your care. From the cross, in His worst hour, Your Son still made sure His mother was held. You do not forget the people I love, even in my hardest moment.*

*Make my faith point away from me, the way hers did. Whatsoever You say, help me do, and help me send everyone I can toward You instead of toward myself.*

*And let my life, like hers, end in that upper room: still praying, still faithful, still among Your people, long after the first yes. You were faithful to carry her all the way there. Carry me too.*

**In Jesus’ name, amen.**
