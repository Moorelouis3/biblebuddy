For hundreds of years, one word followed her name everywhere she went. Preachers said it. Paintings showed it. Stories simply assumed it. The word was prostitute.

There is just one problem. The Bible never once calls Mary Magdalene a prostitute. Not one verse. Not one word.

Think about carrying a reputation you never actually earned. People deciding who you are before they ever hear your side, repeating it until it hardens into fact, even though it was never true. That is what happened to her. Centuries ago a sermon mixed her up with a different, unnamed woman, and the mistake got repeated for over a thousand years.

The real Mary Magdalene is far more remarkable than the myth. She was a woman Jesus healed from a terrible darkness. She stood at the cross when almost all of His own disciples had run.

And she was the very first person in all of human history to see the risen Jesus with her own eyes.

**Today we clear away the myth and meet the woman the resurrection could not have been told without.**

## Delivered, Then Devoted

First, clear the myth, because it has done her a real injustice. The Bible nowhere calls Mary Magdalene a prostitute. That idea comes from a sermon preached centuries after her life, which conflated her with the unnamed sinful woman of Luke 7 and with Mary of Bethany, whom you will meet tomorrow. They are three different women. The label stuck anyway, for over a thousand years, which is its own lesson about how long a false reputation can outlive the truth.

Here is what Scripture actually says. Her name comes from Magdala, a fishing town on the Sea of Galilee; Magdalene simply means of Magdala. And before she followed Jesus, something was terribly wrong in her life.

*“And certain women, which had been healed of evil spirits and infirmities, Mary called Magdalene, out of whom went seven devils.” (Luke 8:2)*

*Out of whom went seven devils.* That is the one detail the Bible gives us about her past, and it is not a scandal. It is an affliction. Seven, in Scripture, often pictures something total, complete. Whatever gripped her had a total hold, the kind of suffering that likely cost her a normal life. And Jesus set her free from all of it.

Watch what she did next. She did not slip back into an ordinary life once she felt better. She stayed close to Jesus and gave from what she had. Luke says she and other women *ministered unto him of their substance*, funding His ministry with their own resources. The woman history later slandered was in truth one of the earliest financial supporters of Jesus' work on earth, and from here her name appears in all four Gospels, more often than most of the twelve apostles.

## The Women Who Did Not Run

Fast forward roughly three years. Jesus had been arrested, beaten, and nailed to a cross outside Jerusalem. Look at who was there, and who was not. Nearly all the twelve had scattered. Peter had denied even knowing Him, three times. But the women stayed, and all the Gospel writers name her among them.

*“Now there stood by the cross of Jesus his mother, and his mother’s sister, Mary the wife of Cleophas, and Mary Magdalene.” (John 19:25)*

Three Gospel writers, writing at different times for different readers, all place Mary Magdalene at the cross. That kind of agreement matters, and so does the danger of it. Standing near a man executed by Rome for treason was not safe. Soldiers were right there, and anyone identified as a follower risked becoming a target too. She had nothing to gain and everything to lose by standing at that cross, and she stood there anyway. That is the quiet courage that has run through this whole book: not the courage of armies, but of staying when it would be safer to leave.

She watched Him die. She watched Him buried in a borrowed tomb as the Sabbath fell. And then she waited through the longest, darkest day of her life, the Saturday when it was all over and nothing could be done.

## The Empty Tomb and the

## Gardener

Very early on Sunday, while it was still dark, Mary Magdalene went to the tomb. She went first, before the sun was fully up, before anyone else arrived. And she found the heavy stone rolled away. Her first thought was not resurrection. It was robbery.

*“Then she runneth, and cometh to Simon Peter... and saith unto them, They have taken away the LORD out of the sepulchre, and we know not where they have laid him.” (John 20:2)*

*We know not where they have laid him.* She assumed grave robbers, or the authorities hiding the body. Resurrection was not even on her list of possibilities. Peter and John ran to see, found the empty grave clothes, and went back home, still not understanding. Mary did not go home. Everyone else left. She stayed at the tomb, weeping.

*“And when she had thus said, she turned herself back, and saw Jesus standing, and knew not that it was Jesus.” (John 20:14)*

The person she was looking for was standing right in front of her, and she did not recognize Him. Maybe her eyes were full of tears, maybe it was the early light, but mostly her grief had narrowed her whole world down to one thing: finding a body to grieve over. She was so certain He was dead that even seeing Him alive did not register. She thought He was the gardener and offered, impossibly, to carry the body away herself. That is how much she loved Him.

Then Jesus said one word.

*“Jesus saith unto her, Mary. She turned herself, and saith unto him, Rabboni; which is to say, Master.” (John 20:16)*

He did not need a speech to reveal Himself. He said her name. And the instant she heard it, everything changed. She answered with one word of her own, *Rabboni*, my Master, my Teacher. Sometimes the truest recognition is not seeing a face. It is hearing your own name spoken by the one voice you know better than any other. The Good Shepherd calls His own by name, and they know His voice.

## The First to Preach the

## Resurrection

Then Jesus gave her a job.

*“Go to my brethren, and say unto them, I ascend unto my Father, and your Father; and to my God, and your God.” (John 20:17)*

The very first thing the risen Jesus did was send a woman to tell His disciples that He was alive. He called them *brethren*, family language, and said His Father was now their Father too. This was no small errand. She was being trusted with news that would reshape human history. And she went.

*“Mary Magdalene came and told the disciples that she had seen the LORD, and that he had spoken these things unto her.” (John 20:18)*

This is the first recorded appearance of the risen Jesus to any human being in all of Scripture, and it happened to her. Not to a king. Not to a priest. Not even to His own closest apostles. To a woman once broken by seven devils. And consider what that meant in her world: a woman's testimony was not even accepted as valid legal evidence in a court of that day. Yet the risen Son of God chose a woman's word to carry the most important news ever told. That choice tells you exactly how God works, and you have watched Him work this way for twenty days now. He is not looking for the impressive resume. He is looking for someone who will go and say what they saw.

Here is where her day lands on you. Being delivered does not erase your past; it redirects it. Mary never denied what she had been through, and the same woman freed from darkness became the one who funded the ministry and preached the first Easter sermon. Faithfulness often just means staying when everyone else leaves. Grief can blind you to what God is already doing, so keep looking, even through tears, because the One you are weeping for may be the one standing right there. And Jesus calls you by name before He gives you a job; relationship comes first, assignment second. The woman the world slandered for centuries was the first to say the words that changed everything: I have seen the Lord.

## Reflection Questions

Take a few minutes and answer these honestly.

**What false reputation have I accepted as the truth about**

1. **me?** For centuries people called Mary something the Bible never called her, and it stuck. What label have I believed about myself that God never actually gave me?

**Am I staying, or am I among the ones who left?**

2. Nearly all the disciples fled. The women stayed at the cross, at real risk. Where is Jesus asking me to stay right now, in a place it would be easier and safer to leave?

**Is grief blinding me to what God is already doing?**

3. Mary stood feet from the risen Jesus and mistook Him for the gardener. What might God already be doing right in front of me that my sorrow has kept me from seeing?

## Today's Action

## Hear Your Name

Everything turned for Mary the moment Jesus said her name. Let Him say yours.

**1. Sit quietly and write down the false label you have been carrying as if it were your name.**

**2. Across it, write the name God actually calls you: His own, redeemed, sent. Read both out loud.**

**3. Then do what Mary did: tell one person your one true sentence, I have seen the Lord, what He has done in your life.**

One false label crossed out, one true name read aloud, one testimony spoken. Do it, and check it off.
