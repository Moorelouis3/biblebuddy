Every city has somebody people talk about in whispers. In Jericho, that woman was Rahab. She ran a house built right into the city wall, and Scripture tells you plainly what she was: a harlot. The Bible does not soften the word, and it does not let the word be the end of her either.

Maybe you know what it is like to carry a label into every room. A past everyone remembers. A reputation that arrives before you do. You wonder, maybe without ever saying it out loud, whether God could really use someone like you.

Then one evening, two strangers knocked on Rahab's door, and the woman with the worst reputation in Jericho made the bravest confession of faith in the whole book of Joshua.

By the time her story is finished, she is named in the family line of Jesus Christ, and the New Testament holds her up twice, by name, as the proof of what real faith looks like.

**Today we meet the outsider with a past, and the God who hung her rescue on a scarlet cord.**

## Before You Read

Listen to Rahab's speech in verses 9 to 11. She has never worshipped the LORD in her life, and she is about to out-believe most of Israel.

Notice what news reached her, and how old that news was. Faith came to her by hearing.

Watch what she bargains for. It is not just her own skin.

## The House in the Wall

Forty years after the Red Sea, the wilderness generation was gone, Moses was buried, and Israel stood at the Jordan under a new leader named Joshua. Across the river waited Jericho, a fortified city with walls thick enough that houses were built into them. Joshua sent two men to spy out the city, and of all the doors in Jericho, they ended up at Rahab's.

*“And Joshua the son of Nun sent out of Shittim two men to spy secretly, saying, Go view the land, even Jericho. And they went, and came into an harlot’s house, named Rahab, and lodged there.” (Joshua 2:1)*

An inn built into the wall was a natural place for strangers to pass unnoticed, and a natural place for word to travel too, because word of the spies reached the king of Jericho by nightfall. His men came straight to Rahab's door with an order: bring out the men who came to you.

Rahab had already made her choice. She had hidden the spies on her roof under drying stalks of flax, and she sent the king's men chasing shadows down the Jordan road. Yes, she lied to do it, and Scripture records the lie without comment, the same way it records Abraham's and Jacob's. What the rest of the Bible praises in Rahab is not her method. It is the faith underneath it, and the courage to act on that faith with her life on the line. Hiding God's people in that city was treason. She did it anyway, for a God she had never once worshipped.

Why would she? Climb up to the roof and listen to her explain it herself.

## Your God Is God

Once the danger passed, Rahab went up to the roof and gave the two spies the most remarkable speech in the book.

*“I know that the LORD hath given you the land... For we have heard how the LORD dried up the water of the Red sea for you, when ye came out of Egypt... for the LORD your God, he is God in heaven above, and in earth beneath.” (Joshua 2:9–11)*

Weigh what she is working with. She never saw the Red Sea part. That happened **forty years earlier**, before most of Jericho was born. She never saw a plague or heard a prophet. All she had was secondhand news, the same news everyone in Jericho had, the news that made the whole city's heart melt. The whole city heard and trembled. **One woman heard and believed.**

That is the anatomy of faith, laid bare in a harlot's house. Faith is not measured by how much you have seen. It is measured by what you do with what you have heard. And the pagan woman on the wall says a sentence most of Israel's own kings would later fail to live by: the LORD your God, He is God in heaven above and in earth beneath.

Then notice the very next thing real faith did. It reached past her.

*“Now therefore, I pray you, swear unto me by the LORD... that ye will save alive my father, and my mother, and my brethren, and my sisters, and all that they have, and deliver our lives from death.” (Joshua 2:12–13)*

Her father, her mother, her brothers, her sisters, everyone belonging to them. The woman her town had used and whispered about spent her one bargaining chip on her whole family. Faith that is real tends to pull other people toward the rescue with it.

## The Scarlet Cord

The spies swore the oath and gave her one instruction to hold onto when the attack came.

*“Behold, when we come into the land, thou shalt bind this line of scarlet thread in the window which thou didst let us down by: and thou shalt bring thy father, and thy mother, and thy brethren, and all thy father’s household, home unto thee.” (Joshua 2:18)*

A scarlet cord in a window. That was the whole system. Gather your family inside, tie the thread, and every life inside the marked house would be spared when judgment fell on the city. Rahab's answer was pure obedience: *according unto your words, so be it*. And she bound the scarlet line in the window.

Then came the waiting, and do not skip past it. Days of silence. Then Israel crossed the Jordan, and for six days the strangest army in history marched around her city once a day, blowing trumpets, saying nothing. Rahab's whole life hung on a thread, literally, tied to the window of a condemned building, on the word of two strangers and the God they served. She kept it tied. Yesterday you watched a scarlet thread tied on a newborn's wrist in Judah's family. Here is the other one, holding a family to life on a wall in Jericho.

On the seventh day the walls fell flat, and the city with them. One section of one wall had a reason to stand long enough, and Joshua kept the oath to the letter.

*“And Joshua saved Rahab the harlot alive, and her father’s household, and all that she had; and she dwelleth in Israel even unto this day.” (Joshua 6:25)* Everyone in the marked house lived. And notice the Bible still calls her *Rahab the harlot* in the very verse that saves her. Scripture never hides her past to make the rescue look cleaner. It shows you both at once, on purpose, so you cannot miss the point. **She dwelleth in Israel.** The outsider from the doomed city became a permanent part of God's own people.

## The Harlot in the Family Line

If her story ended there, it would already preach. It does not end there. Over a thousand years later, the New Testament brings her up three separate times, and each one raises the stakes.

The writer of Hebrews, listing the giants of faith, puts her beside Abraham, Moses, and Noah: *By faith the harlot Rahab perished not with them that believed not, when she had received the spies with peace*. Of all Israel's history, the example chosen to stand with the patriarchs is a Canaanite innkeeper from a fallen city. James reaches for her too, asking whether her faith was not proven by what she *did*, hiding the messengers, sending them out safely. Real faith believes, and real faith moves. Rahab is Scripture's own case study that the two were never separate things.

And then there is Matthew's opening page.

*“And Salmon begat Booz of Rachab; and Booz begat Obed of Ruth; and Obed begat Jesse.” (Matthew 1:5)*

Rachab is Rahab through Greek spelling, and Booz is Boaz. Read what that line is actually saying. Rahab married into Israel and became the mother of **Boaz**, the kind hearted landowner of the book of Ruth, three days ahead of us in this study. The man famous for welcoming a foreign woman into Israel with grace was raised by one. Her grandson's grandson was King David, and down that line, in Bethlehem, came Jesus Christ. The woman with Jericho's worst reputation is a named grandmother of the Messiah.

Here is where her day lands on you. The label your city gave you is not the last word God gets to write about your life. Rahab's past was real, and the Bible never hid it. It just refused to let the past outvote her faith. God is not asking you for a spotless record. He is asking for what He got from her: one honest, brave act of trust in what you have heard about Him, and a cord tied where you cannot untie it quietly.

Tomorrow Israel is in the land, and in trouble again, and the leader who rises to meet it holds court under a palm tree. Her name is Deborah. But today is not finished yet. Take the reflection questions first, then today's action, and end your day in prayer.

## Reflection Questions

Take a few minutes and answer these honestly.

**What label have I started believing is God’s label for me?**

1. Jericho called her the harlot. Heaven called her a woman of faith, and both names sit in the same verse. What does my city call me, and have I confused that with what God calls me?

**What have I heard about God that I have never acted on?**

2. Rahab believed forty year old news and bet her life on it. The rest of the city heard the same news and only trembled. What do I already know about God that is still waiting for my scarlet cord?

**Who am I pulling through the window with me?**

3. Rahab spent her one bargaining chip on her whole family. Who is inside my marked house, and who have I not even invited in yet?

## Today's Action

## Tie the Cord

Rahab’s faith became visible: a scarlet line in a window that anyone could see. Make yours visible this week.

**1. Choose one act of faith you have been keeping theoretical: a conversation, a commitment, a public step.**

**2. Tie the cord: do the visible version of it this week, where it can be seen and not quietly untied.**

**3. Tell one person in your family what you did and why, the way Rahab gathered hers into the house.**

One act, made visible, with one person told. Do it, and check it off.

## Today's Prayer

*Father,*

*You are the God of heaven above and earth beneath. A woman in Jericho knew that from nothing but secondhand news, and she bet her life and her family on it. I have heard so much more than she ever heard. Give me her kind of believing.*

*You know my labels, Lord. The ones my city gave me, the ones I have started answering to. Thank You that You wrote the harlot into the book of the faithful and into the family line of Your Son, and that You never once hid her past to do it. You are not embarrassed by where I have been.*

**Be bigger than my past, Lord, the way You were bigger than hers.**

*Thank You that faith does not require a front row seat to Your miracles. It requires acting on what I have heard. Show me the cord You are asking me to tie, the visible step I keep postponing, and give me the nerve to tie it where I cannot quietly take it back.*

*And make my faith reach past me, the way hers did. Bring my whole household into the marked house. Give me the boldness to gather them, and the patience to wait out the silent days while the trumpets circle.*

*You kept every word of the oath to Rahab. You will keep every word to me.*

**In Jesus’ name, amen.**
