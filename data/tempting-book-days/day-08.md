Picture the strangest moment of John's life.

He has spent years in the desert getting ready for one job: prepare the people for the One who is coming.

He has preached it until his voice is raw. *Somebody greater than me is on the way. I am not worthy to untie His shoes.*

And then one day, out of the crowd of sinners lining up to be washed clean, the One Himself steps forward.

Jesus walks down the bank, into the water, and waits His turn.

John freezes.

This is backwards. The sinless One is standing in the sinners' line, asking the servant to baptize the Master.

**John actually tries to stop Him.**

It is a short reading with a question burning in the middle of it: why would Jesus, who never sinned once, insist on a baptism meant for people turning from sin?

The answer tells you what kind of Savior He is. And it hands us a lesson about obedience that most of us would rather skip.

# Into the Water

Set the scene at the Jordan River.

Crowds are gathered along the banks. People are confessing their sins out loud, wading in, getting dunked under the water, coming up dripping and crying and praising God for a fresh start.

It is raw and loud and real. Nobody in that line is pretending to have it together. **That is the whole point of the line.**

Then Jesus steps onto the riverbank, and gets in it.

John knows exactly who He is. This is not just his cousin, not some random man from Nazareth. This is the Lamb of God. The Messiah. The One that every sermon John ever preached was pointing at.

And John does the only thing that makes sense to him:

> "But John forbad him, saying, I have need to be baptized of thee, and comest thou to me?" (Matthew 3:14)

You can hear it in his voice. *This is backwards. I am the sinner here. I need what You have, and You are coming to me?*

And Jesus answers:

> "And Jesus answering said unto him, Suffer it to be so now: for thus it becometh us to fulfil all righteousness. Then he suffered him." (Matthew 3:15)

*Suffer* is an old word that means allow. Let it happen, John. Permit it now.

And then the reason: *for thus it becometh us to fulfil all righteousness.*

In plain words: this is right, this is part of the assignment, and it has to happen.

**This was the plan.**

## Why a Sinless Man Took a Sinner's Bath

Pull the camera back, because the answer is bigger than that river.

This was Jesus accepting His purpose. Stepping fully into the mission the Father sent Him for: to die for the sins of the world.

In the Old Testament, people had to bring sacrifices to cover their sins. They would bring a spotless lamb, lay their hands on its head as a way of transferring their guilt, and the lamb would be killed in their place.

It was a temporary covering, and it was a lesson drilled in year after year: **sin costs something, and an innocent one pays it.**

Every one of those altars was pointing forward at something bigger.

Jesus was the final sacrifice. The Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world, once and for all. No more daily, weekly, yearly sacrifices needed. No more blood of bulls and goats.

Jesus was it.

So when He stepped into that water, He was not performing an empty ritual. He was identifying fully with the people He came to save.

Standing in the sinners' line on purpose, shoulder to shoulder with everyone who needed washing. Even though He was sinless, He was saying: *I will stand in line with them. I will stand in their place. I will take on what they deserve.*

That is the kind of Savior He is. **He does not shout instructions from the bank. He gets in the water with us.**

## The Layer That Steps on Our Toes

And there is one more layer.

He was also showing us how to live.

Look at the order of events in His life. Before Jesus went into the wilderness to face the devil, before He preached a single sermon, before He healed anyone or cast out anything, He submitted Himself to the will of the Father.

He got baptized to show complete obedience.

* 💧 Obedience came before ministry
* 🙇 Surrender came before power
* 🏜️ The water came before the wilderness, and the wilderness came before the mission

That is what following God actually looks like: **total surrender, first.**

Not obedience after the blessing arrives. Not surrender once the plan makes sense.

Jesus did not negotiate His way around the step He technically did not need for Himself. He stepped into it, for us and in front of us.

Most of us have a step like that. Some act of obedience we have been standing at the edge of for months, maybe years.

We know what God is asking. We just keep finding reasons the timing is wrong.

Jesus did not skip the step of obedience, and He did not delay it. He stepped right in.

## What To Watch For In Matthew 3

* 🚶 Who travels to whom — Jesus comes to John
* 🙋 Why John says the baptism should be the other way around
* ✔️ The words Jesus uses: *fulfil all righteousness*
* 🕊️ What opens, what descends, and what is spoken

You don't have to answer all of those right now. Just notice them as you read.

## The Bigger Takeaway

Heaven's response to that obedience is tomorrow's whole story.

Today, the question is about your step. The water you have been standing at the edge of, checking the temperature with your toe.

**Obedience does not wait for the plan to make sense.**

## Today's One Thing

**Do the First Piece**

Full obedience can feel like too much for one day. So do not do all of it today. Do the first piece.

1. Name the one act of obedience you have been postponing.
2. Break off the first piece: the text that starts the conversation, the appointment booked, the first honest sentence.
3. Do that piece today, and tell one person you trust that you did it.

Telling someone is not for show. It builds a witness, and a witness makes the second piece harder to skip.

## Today's Prayer

Father,

Thank You for a Savior who gets in the water. Who stood in the sinners' line on purpose, with my name on His list.

I confess I like obedience best when it is comfortable and makes sense to me. I have been standing at the edge of something You asked of me, checking the temperature with my toe.

Today I step in.

Give me the obedience that comes before the blessing, the surrender that comes before the power. Whatever You are asking, help me do the first piece of it today.

And thank You that when I step into the water, I am only following footprints that are already there.

In Jesus' name, amen.
