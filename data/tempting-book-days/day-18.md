Fourteen hundred years before the devil took Jesus to that rooftop, a thirsty crowd stood in a dry camp called Rephidim and put God on trial.

They had watched a sea split for them. They had eaten bread that fell out of the sky that very week.

And standing in front of a rock that was about to pour out water, they folded their arms and demanded proof: **is the LORD among us, or not?**

The place got a name, *Massah*, which means testing.

And when Moses gave his farewell speech a generation later, he pointed back at it: *you shall not tempt the LORD your God, as you tempted Him in Massah.*

That is the exact verse Jesus stood on when He refused to jump.

Today we read the third *it is written*, and the story underneath it. And at the end, we will watch the devil leave, and catch the four chilling words Luke attaches to his exit.

**The showdown ends today. What it reveals does not.**

# Proof or Trust

Start with the old word, because the verse turns on it.

When Jesus says *thou shalt not tempt the Lord thy God*, He is not using *tempt* the way we have used it in this study.

In the old language it means **to put to the test.** To make demands of. To put someone on trial and require them to perform.

*Do not put God on trial.* That is the command.

And it was written because of a specific afternoon in the desert:

> "And the people thirsted there for water; and the people murmured against Moses, and said, Wherefore is this that thou hast brought us up out of Egypt, to kill us and our children and our cattle with thirst?" (Exodus 17:3)

Rephidim. No water.

The people are thirsty, hot, and scared, and instead of crying out to God in faith, they turn on Moses so hard that he tells the LORD they are almost ready to stone him.

God, in pure grace, tells Moses to strike the rock, and water gushes out for the whole camp.

But the place keeps the name of what happened there:

> "And he called the name of the place Massah, and Meribah, because of the chiding of the children of Israel, and because they tempted the LORD, saying, Is the LORD among us, or not?" (Exodus 17:7)

*Massah* means testing.

And there is the question they asked, with a split sea in their memory and manna in their stomachs: **is the LORD among us, or not?**

They demanded proof, on their terms, on their schedule, from the God who had done nothing but prove Himself since Egypt.

That is what it means to test God: *if You are really with us, You better show up the way we say, right now.*

A generation later, Moses turned that disaster into a command:

> "Ye shall not tempt the LORD your God, as ye tempted him in Massah." (Deuteronomy 6:16)

And fourteen hundred years after that, on the pinnacle of the temple, Jesus pulled out that exact verse and ended the showdown with it.

The devil said *jump and make God prove it.* Jesus said: **we do not put my Father on trial.**

## Two Postures

Hear the difference, because one of them is running your prayer life and it is worth finding out which.

**Testing says:** *if You really love me, God, You will do this for me. If You are really good, You will fix this exactly how I say, by Friday. Prove Yourself, then I will trust You.*

That is not faith. That is pride holding an audition for God.

**Trust says:** *You have already proven Yourself. The sea is already split. The list of what You have carried me through is already written. So even while I wait, even while I am thirsty, I will not put You on trial.*

Jesus did not need to force His Father's hand to prove who He was. He did not twist promises to justify something reckless.

He simply trusted. **That is how He defeated the pride of life.**

## For a Season

And with that third sword stroke, it was over:

> "And when the devil had ended all the temptation, he departed from him for a season." (Luke 4:13)

Read the end of that verse again slowly.

*He departed from him for a season.*

**For a season. Not forever.** The devil left to wait for a better moment.

And the Bible shows us exactly when he found one.

Three years later, outside Jerusalem, with Jesus nailed to a cross at the weakest hour of His life, listen to what the crowd shouted up at Him:

> "And saying, Thou that destroyest the temple, and buildest it in three days, save thyself. If thou be the Son of God, come down from the cross." (Matthew 27:40)

*If thou be the Son of God.*

The exact same opening line from the wilderness, word for word, hurled at Him one last time at the worst possible moment.

*Come down. Save Yourself. Prove it.*

And the Man who would not jump off the temple to prove anything would not come down off the cross either.

Not because He could not. **Because you and I were the reason He was up there.**

He stayed, and the staying saved us.

## What To Watch For In Today's Reading

* 🪨 What God tells Moses to do with the rod

* ❓ The question: *is the LORD among us, or not?*

You don't have to answer all of those right now. Just notice them as you read.

## The Bigger Takeaway

The showdown ends with a victory and a warning.

**The victory:** every place Israel fell, every place we fall, Jesus stood. He succeeded where they failed, trusted where they tested, worshiped where they wandered, and He did it all so He could lead not just Israel but all of us into the real Promised Land, life with God forever.

**The warning:** temptation defeated once is not temptation gone. It leaves for a season and watches for your weakest hour.

Which is why the last three days of this study are not a victory lap. They are your armory.

## Today's One Thing

**Rewrite the Demand**

Somewhere in most of us is a quiet ultimatum we have handed God. Today it gets rewritten.

1. Write down the demand you have been making: *"If You are real, God, then ______."*
2. Under it, rewrite it as trust: *"Even if not, I will ______."*
3. Tonight, pray the second sentence out loud, and throw the first one away. Physically. Crumple it.

Trading the trial for trust is not giving up on the request. It is giving up on the audition.

He never needed one.

## Today's Prayer

Father,

Thank You for the Son who would not jump, and would not come down. Who refused to put You on trial at the pinnacle, and refused to save Himself at the cross, because saving me was the mission.

I confess my Massah moments. I have stood on a pile of Your faithfulness and asked if You were even with me. I have handed You ultimatums and called them prayers.

I put down the tests. I pick up the trust.

You have nothing left to prove to me. The sea is already split. The cross already stood.

And since the enemy only left for a season, keep me dressed and ready for the hour he picks to come back.

In Jesus' name, amen.
