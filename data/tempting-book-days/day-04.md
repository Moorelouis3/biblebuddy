A fight is terrifying when your opponent feels unpredictable.

You never know where the next hit is coming from, so you flinch at everything. You stay tense. You lose before the fight even starts.

But something changes the moment you discover your opponent only has one combination.

The same setup. The same swings. The same order, every single time.

Suddenly you are not flinching anymore. **You are watching for the pattern.**

Here is the good news nobody tells you about the devil: he fights with a system.

Temptation almost never jumps you out of nowhere, even when it feels that way. It walks toward you in five steps, in the same order, every time.

He has run this exact sequence on the whole human race.

Today the Bible lays the whole sequence out in the open. If you can spot these steps while they are happening, you are already halfway to beating them.

**Time to study the combo.**

# Five Steps Down

Walk down them with me slowly, because somewhere on this staircase is the exact spot where he has your number.

## Step One: The Thought

Temptation almost always starts as a whisper. A seed dropped in your mind.

The devil does not have to shove sin down your throat. He just has to plant a suggestion.

*Did God really say you should not do that? It is just one time. Nobody is going to find out. You deserve it.*

**The battle always starts in your head.**

## Step Two: The Desire

If you do not shut the thought down right there, it grows into wanting.

Now it is not just an idea passing through. It is something your heart has started reaching for.

You picture it. You replay it. You let it settle in and make itself at home.

The devil does not even have to be loud about this part. He just gives the seed time to take root.

## Step Three: The Justification

This is where you start building the excuses.

*Well, I have had a long day. I am stressed. God knows my heart. At least I am not doing what they are doing. I will stop after this one last time.*

You start making it sound reasonable. You give yourself permission.

And the devil is right there nodding along, helping you explain why it is really not that big of a deal.

## Step Four: The Action

Once you have justified it, you do the thing.

You click the site. You call the person. You lose your temper. You spend money you do not have.

The thought that started as a whisper three steps ago is now something that actually happened.

## Step Five: The Pattern

If it does not stop there, if you let it keep cycling, it stops being a choice and becomes a habit. An addiction. Your go to way of coping.

The devil barely has to work anymore, because you are running his play on autopilot.

Then the bill arrives. **Sin always costs something.**

It might cost your peace. Your reputation. The trust of someone you love. Your closeness with God. It might cost you years stuck in shame, guilt, and self hate.

The enemy shows up at step one acting like your friend, handing you what you think you want. By step five, he is laughing at the damage.

# James Wrote the Staircase Down

Here is what should get your attention. James wrote this exact staircase down two thousand years ago:

> "But every man is tempted, when he is drawn away of his own lust, and enticed. Then when lust hath conceived, it bringeth forth sin: and sin, when it is finished, bringeth forth death." (James 1:14–15)

Look at the words.

**Drawn away and enticed.** *Enticed* is a fishing word. It means baited, like a hook dressed up to look like a meal. That is steps one and two: the thought and the desire, dangling in front of you, looking harmless.

**"When lust hath conceived."** Conception means something took hold and started growing. That is step three, the justification, where you stop fighting the desire and start feeding it.

**"It bringeth forth sin."** The action. Step four.

**"Sin, when it is finished, bringeth forth death."** The pattern, fully grown, and the price tag on it. Step five.

## Watch It Happen to David

If you want to see all five steps play out in one Bible story, look at King David.

The Bible says he stayed home one spring when kings normally went out to battle. Guard down, alone on the roof. Then:

> "And it came to pass in an eveningtide, that David arose from off his bed, and walked upon the roof of the king's house: and from the roof he saw a woman washing herself; and the woman was very beautiful to look upon." (2 Samuel 11:2)

There is **step one**. He saw. Just a look, just a thought.

But he did not cut it off. He kept watching, and the look became wanting. **Step two.**

Then he sent servants to find out who she was, and the wanting became a plan he could explain to himself. **Step three.**

Then he took her. **Step four.**

And when she turned up pregnant, the cover up began, and it swallowed an innocent man's life. The bill came due for years. **Step five.**

One uncut thought, on one rooftop, from the man the Bible calls a man after God's own heart.

If the staircase worked on David, it will absolutely work on us.

## Where God Tells You To Fight

Which is exactly why the Bible aims us at one specific step:

> "Casting down imaginations, and every high thing that exalteth itself against the knowledge of God, and bringing into captivity every thought to the obedience of Christ;" (2 Corinthians 10:5)

Casting down imaginations. Taking every thought captive.

Do you see where God aims us? **Step one. The thought.**

The earliest step is the cheapest place to win. A seed is easy to pull up. A tree with five years of roots is not.

The earlier you shut it down, the easier it is. Once you let it grow, every step down the staircase gets steeper, and the climb back gets harder.

## What To Watch For In James 1

* 🏅 What the one who endures temptation receives
* 🚫 Where James says temptation does **not** come from — verse 13 clears something up
* 🪝 What draws a man away, and what entices him
* 🌱 What lust brings forth, and what sin brings forth when it is finished

You don't have to answer all of those right now. Just notice them as you read.

## The Bigger Takeaway

Temptation is not weather. It is a staircase, and it has five steps.

The enemy does not need to be creative, because he does not need to be. He just needs you to keep stopping the fight at the same spot you always do.

So here is the real question of the day, and it takes some honesty:

**Where on the staircase do you usually stop fighting?**

That is where the enemy has your number. And that is exactly where we are going digging right now.

## Today's One Thing

**Find the Step**

You cannot guard a door you have never located. Today you find yours.

1. Write the five steps down the side of a page: thought, desire, justification, action, pattern.
2. Circle the step where you usually stop fighting.
3. Next to it, write your most used justification sentence, word for word.

Tonight, when that sentence shows up in your head, you will recognize it.

And a recognized lie is a lie losing its power.
