The devil does not quit after one miss. **He changes the angle.**

The first swing went at the body: *You are starving, so eat.*

Jesus shut it down with the written Word, and the tempter did not slink away to think it over.

He came right back with a completely different weapon.

If hunger will not break You, maybe ambition will.

In one moment, he pulls Jesus up high and spreads every kingdom of the world out in front of Him. Every throne. Every crown. Every nation Jesus came to redeem, glittering in a single view.

And then the offer: **all of it, right now, for one bow.**

Today is the second temptation. The lust of the eyes at maximum power.

And the reason this one deserves your full attention is simple: this is the temptation of the shortcut.

And there is not a person reading this study who does not have one being dangled in front of them right now.

# The Shortcut

Here is the scene, and it is enormous.

> "And the devil, taking him up into an high mountain, shewed unto him all the kingdoms of the world in a moment of time. And the devil said unto him, All this power will I give thee, and the glory of them: for that is delivered unto me; and to whomsoever I will I give it. If thou therefore wilt worship me, all shall be thine." (Luke 4:5–7)

All the kingdoms of the world, in a moment of time.

Rome and its legions. Every empire that ever was or would be. Power, glory, thrones, crowns, all of it flashed in front of a Man who had spent thirty years in a carpenter shop and forty days in a desert.

## Was the Offer Even Real?

That is the first question people ask. Could the devil actually deliver?

Listen to how Jesus talks about him elsewhere. He calls him *"the prince of this world"* (John 14:30). Paul calls him *"the god of this world"* (2 Corinthians 4:4).

Ever since the fall, the enemy has held real, ugly influence over the systems of this world.

The shine he was dangling was not a hologram. That is exactly what made this a true temptation and not a joke.

**The devil does not waste breath offering things that have no pull.**

## What He Was Really Selling

But look closely, because this is the heart of the day.

Jesus came to earth *for* the kingdoms. That was the mission: to defeat sin and death, to redeem people from every tribe and nation, to bring them under God's rule.

**The kingdoms were always going to be His.**

The Father's road to them ran through suffering, betrayal, a bloody cross, and an empty tomb.

And the devil was offering a way around all of that.

*Skip the wilderness. Skip the rejection. Skip the pain and humiliation. I will hand it all to You right now. No cross needed. Just worship me.*

The crown without the cross. The destination without the road.

That is the shortcut, and notice something: **the devil was not offering Jesus a different prize. He was offering the right prize by the wrong road.**

That is almost always how the lust of the eyes works. It rarely tempts you with something evil.

It tempts you with something good, early, sideways, on terms God never set.

## The Same Play On Us

He runs this exact play on us.

It is the devil dangling things in front of your eyes — money, status, relationships, opportunities — and whispering:

*Take it now. Why wait on God? Why trust His timing? Why go through all that struggle when I can hand it to you faster? All you have to do is compromise just a little.*

He is good at it, too, because he has a partner on the inside: **comparison.**

He makes us jealous of other people's lives. We look at what someone else has — their marriage, their body, their following, their success — and start thinking: *I want that. I deserve that. I will do whatever it takes to get it.*

That is the lust of the eyes. Wanting more because of what you see, instead of trusting that God knows exactly what is good for you, when it is good for you, and how it is going to come.

## Why Jesus Did Not Bite

Tomorrow we will stand over His exact answer and where it came from. But catch the shape of His refusal today.

He was not tempted by the shiny kingdoms, because He knew that was not the way.

He was not about to trade worshiping His Father for ruling the world on the devil's terms.

He would not take a shortcut that skipped the cross, because **the cross was the whole point.**

A world handed over by Satan would still be a world lost in its sins.

The shortcut did not just skip the suffering. **It skipped the saving.**

## Every Shortcut Has a Hidden Altar

Here is the truth to carry out of this day.

The devil's price is never really the thing you cut. **It is the bow.**

Compromise in little ways, worship in little ways, bend your knee to something other than God, and the thing you wanted arrives with a new king attached.

Nobody takes the deal thinking they are switching masters. That is always what the deal was.

## What To Watch For In Luke 4

* 🏔️ Where the devil takes Him, and what he shows Him
* 👑 What the devil claims is his to give
* 🙇 What he asks for in exchange — notice the price tag in verse 7
* ⚔️ The command Jesus answers with

You don't have to answer all of those right now. Just notice them as you read.

## The Bigger Takeaway

The question is not whether you want things. Wanting is human, and the things are often good.

**The question is whether you will take God's road to them or the dangled one.**

His road usually costs more and takes longer. It is also the only one where you arrive still free.

## Today's One Thing

**Choose the Long Road on Paper**

Shortcuts win in the moment because the moment is all they show you. Beat the moment by deciding early.

1. Write down one shortcut you could realistically take this month that God would not bless.
2. Under it, write out what the long road is instead: the honest version, with the waiting included.
3. Circle the long road, sign your name next to it, and date it. Decide now, before the dangling starts.

A choice made in the quiet is twice as strong in the moment. That is the whole trick.

## Today's Prayer

Father,

Thank You for a Savior who saw every kingdom in the world dangled in front of Him and did not blink. Who chose the cross road because the shortcut skipped the saving.

You know what the enemy dangles in front of me. You know whose life I compare mine to, and what I have started believing I deserve.

Guard my eyes. I want Your road more than his shortcuts.

Teach me to want what You want for me, on Your schedule, by Your route.

And when the glitter flashes and the whisper says take it now, remind me what every shortcut hides: an altar, and a bow.

I will wait for what comes from Your hand.

In Jesus' name, amen.
