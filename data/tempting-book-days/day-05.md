Here is a question worth sitting with.

If nobody told you where to be today, where would somebody find you?

Not where your schedule puts you. Not where your boss or your family expects you.

**Where you drift when you are free.**

Because where a person drifts tells you what their heart is anchored to.

The Bible gives us exactly one story from the childhood of Jesus. One.

Out of thirty years of growing up, learning to walk, working in a shop, living in a small town, a single scene made it into Scripture.

That alone should make us lean in. If God preserved only one childhood moment, that moment must tell us something He really wants us to know.

It is the story of a twelve year old boy, a panicked mother, a three day search through a crowded city, and an answer so surprising that His own parents could not understand it.

And buried inside it is the secret of why, years later, the devil could not knock Jesus over.

# About My Father's Business

Let me paint the scene for you, because this story deserves it.

Jesus grew up in Nazareth, a small town up north in Galilee. Every year, His family packed up and traveled to Jerusalem for the Passover festival, like faithful Jewish families everywhere.

And Passover was not just a holiday on a calendar. It was the yearly retelling of the night God rescued His people out of Egypt.

Every family would remember the story: a spotless lamb, its blood painted on the doorposts, and death passing over every house that was covered by it.

Passover said the same thing every spring: **God saves, and He saves through the lamb.**

Hold that thought, because the twelve year old boy walking up to the lamb festival *is* the Lamb.

Nobody on that road knew it yet. Heaven did.

## How You Lose the Son of God

Now, about that road. People did not travel like we do, one family in one car. They moved in giant caravans.

Jesus had brothers and sisters, plus aunts, uncles, cousins, and neighbors from Nazareth. By the time they hit the road it could have been fifty, sixty, maybe a hundred people trekking the eighty miles up to Jerusalem, four or five days on foot.

Kids chasing each other. Animals slowing the line. People stopping to eat and set up camp.

It was not strange at all for a kid to be running around with cousins, out of sight of his parents for hours.

Which is exactly how the scariest week of Mary's life began.

The festival ends. The caravan heads home. Mary and Joseph figure Jesus is somewhere in the crowd, hanging with His cousins.

They travel a whole day like that. Then evening comes, camp goes up, and they start looking.

Not with the cousins. Not with the neighbors. **Nowhere.**

Imagine their stomachs dropping. They have lost the Son of God in the big city.

They turn around, retrace every step, and search. It takes three days.

> "And it came to pass, that after three days they found him in the temple, sitting in the midst of the doctors, both hearing them, and asking them questions. And all that heard him were astonished at his understanding and answers." (Luke 2:46–47)

The *doctors* here are not physicians. The word means the teachers of the law, the smartest rabbis in Israel, the scholars who knew the Scriptures better than anyone alive.

And there is a twelve year old sitting in the middle of them, listening, asking questions, and answering theirs, while grown scholars look at each other astonished.

## Notice Where He Drifted

Not lost. Not scared. Not hiding out.

He was in the temple, the one place in the world where God's presence was supposed to dwell.

**When nobody told Jesus where to be, that is where He drifted.**

Then comes the moment every parent understands. Mary does exactly what any mom would do. Half relieved, half upset: *Son, why have You done this to us? We have been worried sick looking for You.*

And His answer is what blows me away:

> "And he said unto them, How is it that ye sought me? wist ye not that I must be about my Father's business?" (Luke 2:49)

*Wist* is an old word that means knew. **Did you not know I had to be about My Father's business?**

Stop and let that land.

He is twelve. No miracles yet. No disciples. No voice from heaven yet.

And He already knows exactly whose He is and what His life is for. Not Joseph's business. His Father's business.

## Why This Matters for Temptation

Before the baptism. Before the wilderness. Before the devil ever opened his mouth. Jesus was already rooted in His identity and His purpose.

That is why this story belongs in a study about temptation.

Because years later, when the devil finally showed up in the desert, he aimed his attacks at exactly this spot.

**"If thou be the Son of God."**

He went straight for identity. And he hit solid rock, because that question had been settled in Jesus since childhood.

Most people do it backwards. They wait until temptation is staring them down to figure out who they are.

They drift through life with no settled sense of whose they are or what their life is for, and when the enemy comes knocking, they are easy to knock over.

A person with no anchor goes wherever the pull is strongest.

But a person who knows whose they are has already won half the fight before it starts. **The bait has less to offer somebody who already knows why they are here.**

## What To Watch For In Luke 2

* 🕍 Why the family travels to Jerusalem every year
* 🚶 How Jesus goes missing without anyone noticing at first
* 📖 Where they find Him, and what He is doing there
* 🏠 What He says about His Father's business

You don't have to answer all of those right now. Just notice them as you read.

## The Bigger Takeaway

So we end where we started, and now you see why the question matters.

When nobody is telling you where to be, where would somebody find you?

Jesus was found in His Father's house, about His Father's business, at twelve.

**Settle whose you are before the test comes.**

That is the lesson of the temple.

## Today's One Thing

**Write the Sentence**

Identity that lives only in your head stays fuzzy. Today it goes on paper.

1. Write this sentence: *"I belong to God, and my life is about ______."*
2. Finish it honestly. Short is fine. True is required.
3. Keep the paper. Later in this study we are going to build on it.

If you cannot finish the sentence yet, write the sentence anyway and leave the blank open before God.

An honest blank is a better start than a borrowed answer.

## Today's Prayer

Father,

Thank You for the boy in the temple. Twelve years old, and He already knew whose He was and what His life was for.

I confess I have spent years drifting. Letting the strongest pull decide where I end up, and figuring out who I am only after the test is already on top of me.

Settle me before the storm, not during it.

Root my identity in You. Make Your house the place I drift to when nobody is watching. Make Your business the business my life is about.

And when the enemy comes asking who I think I am, let him find the question already answered.

In Jesus' name, amen.
