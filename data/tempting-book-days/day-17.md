For his last swing, the devil changes weapons.

He has tried hunger. He has tried glory. Jesus answered both with Scripture, the sword coming out faster than the trap could close.

So for the final attack, the devil does something that should make every believer sit up straight.

**He picks up the sword himself.**

*For it is written*, says the devil. And then he quotes the Bible. Accurately, word for word, from one of the most beloved psalms ever sung.

He takes Jesus to the holiest place in the land, stands Him on the highest edge of it, and wraps the dare in Scripture.

Because the scariest thing you will learn today is that the enemy knows your Bible. **Possibly better than you do.**

And the way Jesus handles a twisted verse is a skill you cannot live without.

# The Edge of the Roof

Look where the devil stages his last attempt:

> "And he brought him to Jerusalem, and set him on a pinnacle of the temple, and said unto him, If thou be the Son of God, cast thyself down from hence: For it is written, He shall give his angels charge over thee, to keep thee: And in their hands they shall bear thee up, lest at any time thou dash thy foot against a stone." (Luke 4:9–11)

The pinnacle of the temple. The very top of the most sacred place on earth for the Jews. The center of worship, where sacrifices were made and God's presence was supposed to dwell.

Historians describe that drop at around four hundred feet down into the Kidron Valley. Standing there was like standing on the roof edge of a skyscraper, looking straight down.

And listen to the new weapon. **For it is written.**

The devil has watched Jesus win twice with Scripture, so now he tries the sword himself. He quotes Psalm 91, the promise that God shall give His angels charge over thee, that they shall bear thee up in their hands.

**He quoted it accurately.** That is what people miss.

The words were right. The use was poison.

## Read the Address on the Promise

Go read where that promise lives. Psalm 91 opens with an address label:

> "He that dwelleth in the secret place of the most High shall abide under the shadow of the Almighty." (Psalm 91:1)

*He that dwelleth in the secret place of the most High.*

The whole psalm belongs to the person who lives close to God, abides under His shadow, walks in His ways.

To that person, God promises protection on the road God has them walking: the terror by night, the arrow by day, the snare, the pestilence.

**It is a soldier's psalm for a soldier on assignment.**

It is not insurance for stunts.

The devil cut the promise loose from its address and tried to redeliver it to a jump off a roof.

Protection on God's path became permission to test God off it. Same words. Opposite meaning.

## The Pride of Life With Its Mask Off

Look at what the dare was really made of.

It was Satan saying: *if You are really God's Son, You can do whatever You want. Put Yourself in any situation, no matter how foolish or dangerous, and God will have to save You. Test Him. Prove how chosen You are. Jump, and when the angels catch You in front of the whole temple crowd, everyone will know.*

That is pride of life thinking:

* 🦸 Living like you are invincible
* 📜 Like God owes you protection no matter how reckless you are
* 🧹 Like you can run your life on your own terms and God will clean up the mess

It says, *look how special I am. I will jump, and God will catch me, because He has to.*

Jesus was not having it:

> "And Jesus answering said unto him, It is said, Thou shalt not tempt the Lord thy God." (Luke 4:12)

He knew you do not prove faith by putting yourself in foolish situations just to see if God will bail you out.

That is not faith. **That is arrogance wearing faith's clothes.**

He stayed humble, trusted His Father's plan, and refused to force His Father's hand.

## Jumping Slowly Is Still Jumping

Nobody reading this is likely to climb a church roof and jump to prove God loves them.

But people take reckless spiritual risks all the time.

They live in ways they know God warned against: chasing the sin, ignoring the wisdom, diving into situations they were never meant to be in, and then expect God to rescue them without consequence.

*I can live however I want, and God will still have to bless me and keep me safe.*

That is jumping. It is just slower.

## The Second Lesson, and It Is Urgent

The devil knows the Bible. He can quote it accurately and aim it at your destruction.

Twisted Scripture is one of his oldest tools, and it did not retire when Jesus left the desert.

It shows up today as verses pulled out of context to excuse sin, to promise what God never promised, to pressure you into what God never asked.

Which means knowing verses is not enough. **You have to know them in context.**

You have to know the address on the promise, not just the promise.

A verse without its context is a sword anyone can pick up, including the enemy. A verse with its context only cuts one way.

That is why Jesus could stand on that pinnacle unhurried. He knew Psalm 91 was true, and He knew what it was true *about*.

The twist had no room to work.

## What To Watch For In Today's Reading

* 🏛️ Where the devil takes Jesus for the final temptation
* 📖 The words *for it is written*, in the devil's mouth
* 🏷️ Who Psalm 91 says the promises belong to
* ⚔️ The answer Jesus gives

You don't have to answer all of those right now. Just notice them as you read.

## The Bigger Takeaway

Two questions walk out of this day with you.

**Where have you been jumping and billing heaven for the landing?**

**And is your Bible knowledge deep enough to catch a twist?**

## Today's One Thing

**Read the Address**

A promise without its address can be delivered anywhere, including where it was never meant to go. Protect your favorite one.

1. Take the one verse you lean on most.
2. Read the entire chapter it lives in, today.
3. Write one line underneath your verse: what it actually promises, and who it is addressed to.

Now that promise is twist proof in your hands. Do this to every verse you love and the devil loses a weapon.
