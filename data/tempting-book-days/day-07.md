Before the most famous baptism in history, there was a birth announcement.

It was delivered by an angel, to an old priest, in a cloud of incense, inside the temple of God.

The promise was impossible: a son, for a couple far too old to have one.

And the old priest stood in front of the angel and did not believe a word of it.

That unbelieving priest became the father of the boldest believer of his generation. His son grew up to be a wild man in camel hair, eating locusts in the desert, with a voice that shook the whole country awake.

His name was John. And before Jesus ever stepped into the Jordan River, God spent years building this one man for a single job: **get the people ready.**

Today we read more of the Bible than usual, because John's story starts long before the river, and every piece of it matters. An angel, a silenced father, a baby leaping in the womb, and a prophecy written seven hundred years in advance.

God does not improvise. Today you get to watch how far ahead He plans.

# The Voice in the Wilderness

John did not just pop up out of nowhere, preaching by a river. His story starts in the temple, years earlier, with an old priest named Zacharias.

Zacharias and his wife Elisabeth were faithful people, and they were childless, and they were old.

In that world, that combination carried a quiet ache and a public shame. They had prayed for a child for what must have been decades. **The prayer had outlived their hope.**

Then one day Zacharias is chosen to burn incense in the temple, a once in a lifetime honor. And standing beside the altar, he finds an angel of the Lord waiting for him.

> "But the angel said unto him, Fear not, Zacharias: for thy prayer is heard; and thy wife Elisabeth shall bear thee a son, and thou shalt call his name John." (Luke 1:13)

*Thy prayer is heard.*

Think how long that prayer had been sitting in heaven, sounding unanswered.

And the angel keeps going: this boy will be great in the sight of the Lord, filled with the Holy Ghost, and he will turn many in Israel back to their God. He will go before the Lord Himself, to make ready a people prepared for Him.

## The Priest Who Did Not Believe

It is one of the greatest birth announcements ever given.

And Zacharias, standing in front of an angel, in the temple, with the incense still rising, asks for proof. *I am old. My wife is old. How can I know this is real?*

So the angel gives him a sign he did not ask for: **silence.**

Zacharias walks out of that temple unable to speak, and he stays mute for nine whole months, until the day the baby is named. The first thing out of his mouth when his voice returns is praise.

Here is what I love about that.

God's plan did not depend on the priest's faith being perfect. The promise marched right on through his doubt.

God is gracious like that. **He can work with a believer who wobbles.**

## Two Babies, One Mission

Then comes the moment that gives me chills every time.

Mary, pregnant with Jesus, comes to visit Elisabeth, pregnant with John. The Bible says that the second Mary's greeting reached Elisabeth's ears, the baby leaped in her womb, and Elisabeth was filled with the Holy Ghost.

Before either boy was born, John was already responding to Jesus. Already jumping at the sound of His presence.

Two unborn children, and the mission was already in motion.

And this plan was older than both mothers. Seven hundred years before that meeting, the prophet Isaiah had already written John's job description:

> "The voice of him that crieth in the wilderness, Prepare ye the way of the LORD, make straight in the desert a highway for our God." (Isaiah 40:3)

When Luke introduces John's preaching, he quotes this exact prophecy.

**God does not scramble.** He writes His plans centuries before He runs them, and then He raises up exactly the people He promised.

Both births announced by angels. Both boys prophesied in the Old Testament. Both carrying a clear mission before they ever took their first breath.

Jesus came to save the world. John came to prepare the way for Him.

## The Verse That Is Easy To Skip

But between the announcement and the river, there is a verse most people read right past:

> "And the child grew, and waxed strong in spirit, and was in the deserts till the day of his shewing unto Israel." (Luke 1:80)

*Waxed* is an old word for grew. John grew, got strong in spirit, and lived in the deserts until the day of his *shewing*, his public unveiling, to Israel.

Just like Jesus spent hidden years in Nazareth, John spent hidden years in the wilderness.

Out there in the desert, living on locusts and wild honey, dressed in camel hair, separated from the comforts of normal life, away from the noise of the village.

That was his training ground. That was where God shaped him, filled him with boldness, and stripped away everything that would get in the way of his purpose.

**A man with nothing to lose and nothing to protect is a dangerous preacher.**

## God's Classroom

God has always done this with His people.

* 🐑 **Moses** spent forty years in the wilderness tending sheep before God called him back to lead Israel out of Egypt.
* 🏃 **David** ran through the wilderness hiding from Saul before he ever wore the crown.
* ✉️ **Paul**, after meeting Jesus on the Damascus road, spent years away in Arabia before his ministry took off.

The wilderness is God's classroom.

So by the time Jesus walked up to the Jordan River, John was already locked into his calling.

He was out there preaching repentance, telling people to turn from their sins, baptizing them in the river as a sign they were being washed clean and starting fresh.

Baptism was powerful because it was an outward sign of an inward change. A way of saying publicly: *I am done living for myself. I am turning back to God.*

That was John's whole mission. Get people ready for Jesus.

And when Jesus finally stepped onto the scene, John did not flinch, did not hesitate, did not wonder. He pointed straight at Him and told the crowd exactly who was walking toward them.

Years in God's classroom will do that. **When your moment comes, you recognize it.**

## What To Watch For In Today's Reading

* 👼 What the angel promises about the son who is coming
* 🤰 What John does in the womb when Mary walks in
* 🏜️ Where John spends the years before his ministry
* 🛣️ What John preaches at the Jordan, and whose way he prepares

You don't have to answer all of those right now. Just notice them as you read.

## The Bigger Takeaway

John's story shows us the same thing we keep seeing over and over.

**The wilderness is not punishment. It is preparation.**

God uses it to shape you, so that when your moment comes, you are already ready, already strong in spirit, already doing what He made you to do.

So the question for you today is the John question: is your wilderness making you bitter, or making you bold?

## Today's One Thing

**Seven Days Stripped**

John's strength grew in a life stripped of extras. Taste a small piece of that.

1. Pick one comfort or distraction you reach for daily: a feed, a show, a snack habit, background noise.
2. Set it aside for the next seven days, starting today.
3. Mark today as day one somewhere you can see it, and check off each day as it passes.

Every time you reach for it and find it gone, let that empty moment point you to God.

That is the desert doing its work.

## Today's Prayer

Father,

Thank You for John. For announcing him by an angel, writing his mission seven hundred years early, and shaping him in the desert until he was ready.

Thank You that You plan that far ahead for Your people, and that my story is not improvised either.

Make me strong in spirit in my desert, ready for my shewing.

Strip away the comforts I hide behind and the noise I numb myself with. Fill the empty space with boldness, the way You filled John.

And let my life do what his did: prepare the way, and point people straight at Jesus.

In Jesus' name, amen.
