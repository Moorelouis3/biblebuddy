Yesterday God spoke. Today Abram moves.

Read those two sentences again, because the whole book lives between them. There is no argument in that space.

No negotiation. No chapter of Abram asking for details, demanding a map, or listing his reasons why the timing is bad.

God said go. And the very next thing Genesis records is a man going.

We have spent seven days walking through the world Abram came from.

The flood, the nations, the tower, the family line, the house of Terah, the stalled journey, the call.

All of it was setup.

Today, the man this book is named after finally does the thing he is remembered for. He obeys.

And watch closely how he does it, because obedience in this book is not going to mean perfection.

We will watch this same man stumble more than once before we are done.

Obedience means something simpler and harder than perfection. It means that when God says go, you go.

**Today the obedience of Abraham begins.**

# The Space Between

> “So Abram departed, as the LORD had spoken unto him.” (Genesis 12:4)

Yesterday ended with God speaking. Watch how today begins:

> “So Abram departed, as the LORD had spoken unto him; and Lot went with him: and Abram was seventy and five years old when he departed out of Haran.” (Genesis 12:4)

Do not rush past the first word. So. God said *get thee out*, and the text answers, *so Abram departed*.

Command, then movement. Nothing in between.

That space between God’s voice and Abram’s feet, or really the lack of it, is where the obedience of Abraham begins.

Not on a mountain years from now. Right here, in one little word.

And think about everything Abram did not have when he started walking:

* 🧭 No destination. God still had not named the land
* ❓ No answer for how a man with a barren wife becomes a great nation
* 🌁 No guarantee of what was waiting down the road
* 🚪 No plan for ever coming back

He did not get his questions answered first. He got up. The understanding was going to have to catch up on the road.

Now look at the number in that verse. Abram was seventy and five years old.

This is not a twenty year old with a backpack and nothing to lose.

This is a settled, married, wealthy man with a whole household depending on him.

The older we get, the heavier moving gets, and Abram had seventy five years of weight. He went anyway.

Whatever you have been telling yourself about being too old, too settled, or too far into your life to obey God, Abram heard the same voice at seventy five and packed.

## Into the Land of Canaan

And look who is in the caravan:

> “And Abram took Sarai his wife, and Lot his brother’s son, and all their substance that they had gathered, and the souls that they had gotten in Haran; and they went forth to go into the land of Canaan; and into the land of Canaan they came.” (Genesis 12:5)

Sarai is there. And Lot is there. Remember who Lot is. His father Haran died back in Ur, in front of his own father.

Nahor, Abram’s other brother, stays behind in Mesopotamia.

But the fatherless nephew we have been watching since Day 6 goes with his uncle into Canaan. Keep watching these two.

Their story together is just getting started.

**Substance** is an old word for everything they owned. And notice where they got it.

The text says the substance was gathered, and the souls, meaning the servants and workers joined to the household, were gotten in Haran.

You do not gather possessions and people overnight. However many years it was, Haran had become a real home.

And that matters, because leaving a rest stop is easy. Leaving a home you built is obedience.

And then the quiet line that finishes an old story: *into the land of Canaan they came*.

Terah set out for Canaan and settled halfway. His son finishes the journey his father parked.

The stalled trip from Day 7 finally arrives.

## A Full Country

Now, before we follow Abram through the land, understand what Canaan was.

Canaan was not one city with a gate you walked through.

It was a whole region, filled with different peoples, settlements, and small city states.

Walled towns, farmland, trade roads, and local kings.

So when the text says Abram passed through the land, picture a stranger leading his family and flocks through country where every town already belongs to somebody else.

> “And Abram passed through the land unto the place of Sichem, unto the plain of Moreh. And the Canaanite was then in the land.” (Genesis 12:6)

Sichem, also spelled Shechem, is one particular place inside that bigger land.

And then comes a sentence you could easily skim past, and should not. The Canaanite was then in the land.

Think about what that means. We know exactly who the Canaanites are, because we traced them on Day 3. Canaan’s people.

And they are here, living in the very land God is about to promise away.

God did not lead Abram to an empty field with a welcome sign.

He led him to a full country and promised him something that, humanly speaking, was already taken.

Abram’s faith is going to have to live next to that fact for the rest of his life.

## An Altar Unto the LORD

And right there, God appears:

> “And the LORD appeared unto Abram, and said, Unto thy seed will I give this land: and there builded he an altar unto the LORD, who appeared unto him.” (Genesis 12:7)

**Seed** is the Bible’s word for descendants.

Your children, and their children, and the whole line that comes after you. So hear what God just did.

Back in Haran, the call was *a land that I will shew thee*. No name, no location.

Now that Abram is standing in Canaan, God sharpens it: this land. This one. The one under your feet.

Catch the order of that, because it is the heart of this whole day. Abram did not get the map and then obey.

He obeyed, and then he got shown the map. Some things God will only show you after you start walking.

If Abram had stayed in Haran waiting for the details, he would have died there like his father, still holding his questions.

And look at the first thing Abram builds in the promised land. Not a house. Not a wall. An altar.

An **altar** is a place set apart for worship and sacrifice to God, usually built up from stones or earth.

It was how a man marked a spot and said, God met me here, and I answered.

If that sounds familiar, it should. Go back to the flood for a second.

When Noah stepped off the ark onto the washed earth, the first thing the Bible records him building was an altar to the LORD.

Now Abram enters the land God showed him, and the first thing he builds is an altar.

Two men, two new beginnings, one identical response. When God keeps His word, the men in this book build altars.

Worship is what obedience does when it arrives.

## The Altar Stays

> “And he removed from thence unto a mountain on the east of Bethel, and pitched his tent, having Bethel on the west, and Hai on the east: and there he builded an altar unto the LORD, and called upon the name of the LORD.” (Genesis 12:8)

There is a quiet picture hiding in that verse. Abram pitches a tent, and builds an altar. The tent is temporary.

You fold it up and carry it to the next camp. The altar stays.

Abram owns nothing here yet, and he lives like a traveler passing through. But everywhere he stops, worship gets built.

You could trace this man’s path across Canaan by the altars he left behind. That is worth asking about your own path.

If somebody traced yours, what would they find marking the places you have been?

Then verse 9 keeps him moving, going on still toward the south, down through the length of the land.

He does not own one square foot of it.

He is walking the whole promise on foot, meeting it the only way he can right now. By faith.

One more thing before we land this day. Why does Genesis keep naming places? Sichem. Moreh. Bethel. Hai.

The south country. This is not filler, and it is not a geography lecture. Remember who Moses wrote Genesis down for.

Israel. And Israel knew these names the way you know the towns around where you grew up.

Imagine hearing this story as an Israelite. Shechem? I know Shechem. Bethel? I know Bethel.

They were hearing that centuries before Israel ever possessed this land, their ancestor walked through it while the Canaanite still held it, and God stood there and said, *unto thy seed*.

When the Bible starts dropping place names, slow down. It is almost always planting something it plans to harvest later.

And notice something about how we read today, because it is bigger than this chapter. We did not rush.

We stopped at a two letter word. We followed Lot backward to his father’s grave.

We asked why the writer kept naming towns. We connected an altar in Canaan to an altar after the flood.

That is not just how we studied Genesis 12. That is how you study the Bible.

## God Said Go

So here is where the day lands. Obedience is not a feeling, and it is not perfection.

It is the shrinking of the space between God’s voice and your feet.

Abram heard, and Abram moved, at seventy five, with a full house, toward a land he had never seen, already occupied by somebody else.

And God met him inside the obedience, not before it.

God said go. He went.

Now it is your turn. Take the reflection questions first, then today’s action, and end your day in prayer.

## What To Watch For In Genesis 12

Yesterday God told Abram to go.

Watch how fast the going happens, and what the text does not put between the command and the obedience.

Notice how old Abram is when he leaves. It is probably not the age you would guess.

Count how many altars get built in these six verses, and notice what Abram never builds.

**As you read, notice:**

* 👣 The first word of verse 4, and what it answers
* 🐪 Who goes with Abram, and what they bring
* 🏰 Who is already living in the land God promised
* 🔥 What Abram builds at Sichem and near Bethel

And think about this question: **How much did Abram know before he started walking?**

## Reflection Questions

1. **What has God already told you to do that you are still thinking about?** The space between His voice and your feet is where obedience lives or dies. Be honest about how wide yours is right now.
2. **Abram was seventy five, settled, and wealthy when he uprooted his whole life. What has too late or too settled talked you out of?** The most famous act of obedience in Genesis was done by an old man with a full household. Your age is not the obstacle you think it is.
3. **Abram left altars everywhere he went, and never built himself a house. What does your trail look like?** If somebody traced your path through the last few years, would they find marks of worship, or just things you collected?

## Today's One Thing

**Close the Space**

Between God’s command and Abram’s feet there was one word: so. Today, shrink your space down to one word too.

1. Write down the one thing God has already spoken to you about that you have been sitting on.
2. Write today’s date next to it. This is the day the thinking about it ends.
3. Take the first physical step before you sleep tonight. The call, the apology, the signup, the first box packed.

One thing, one date, one step. Write it, do it, and check it off.

## Today's Prayer

Father,

You spoke, and Abram moved. I want my life to read like that verse.

But You know the space between Your voice and my feet.

You know the things You have already told me that I am still thinking about, still praying about, still waiting to feel ready for.

I have called my delay wisdom. I have called it timing. Today I am calling it what it is.

**Forgive me for the distance between hearing You and obeying You.**

Give me a heart that moves. Not a perfect heart, because Abram did not have one of those either.

Just a heart that gets up when You speak, even when the questions are not answered yet.

Teach me that some things You only show after I start walking. Abram did not see the land until he left Haran.

Help me stop demanding the whole map as the price of my first step.

And make my life leave altars, Father.

Not just things I gathered, but places where anybody who traces my path can tell that You met me there, and I answered.

I am not too old for this. I am not too settled for this.

Abram was seventy five, and his greatest days were all still in front of him.

So here I am. You said go.

**In Jesus’ name, amen.**
