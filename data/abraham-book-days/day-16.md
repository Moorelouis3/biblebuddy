The meal is over.

The three visitors stand up from under the tree, brush themselves off, and look down the valley toward Sodom.

And Abraham, still playing the host, walks them out to the road to see them off.

Somewhere on that road, God decides to tell Abraham what He is about to do.

Think about what that means. The God of heaven is about to judge a city, and He stops to let a man in on it.

Not because Abraham gets a vote.

Because Abraham has been chosen to raise a family that will teach the world what justice looks like, and God wants him to see justice up close before he teaches it.

And Abraham does not nod politely. He has a nephew in that city.

He steps closer, and he asks God the hardest question a human being can ask.

If You destroy the whole place, what happens to the good people inside it? Is that right?

Then he keeps asking. Fifty. Forty five. Forty. Thirty. Twenty. Ten.

Six times he comes back, and six times God says yes.

**Today a man argues with God, and God lets him.**

# Shall Not the Judge Do Right

> “That be far from thee… Shall not the Judge of all the earth do right?” (Genesis 18:25)

Yesterday ended with Sarah behind the tent door and a promise with a date on it.

Today picks up the second the meal is finished:

> “And the men rose up from thence, and looked toward Sodom: and Abraham went with them to bring them on the way.” (Genesis 18:16)

They stand up and look toward Sodom. That is where they are headed. And catch what Abraham does. He walks with them.

In his world, a good host did not just feed you and wave from the tent.

He walked you out to the road and got you started on your way. Abraham is still serving, all the way to the last step.

But hold on to that word Sodom. Abraham knows exactly who lives there. Lot.

The nephew he raised, the nephew he split the land with, the nephew he rode into battle to rescue on Day 11.

Three visitors who know things no stranger should know are now walking toward his family.

## Shall I Hide from Abraham

And on that road, God does something He did not have to do:

> “And the LORD said, Shall I hide from Abraham that thing which I do; Seeing that Abraham shall surely become a great and mighty nation, and all the nations of the earth shall be blessed in him?” (Genesis 18:17–18)

God asks Himself a question out loud. Should I hide this from Abraham? And the answer is no, for a reason.

Abraham is not a bystander.

He is the man God is building a nation from, and through him every nation on earth is going to be blessed.

We first heard that line back on Day 7, when God called him out of Haran.

God is repeating it now because what He is about to do with Sodom is connected to what He is doing with Abraham.

And do not skip past *all the nations of the earth shall be blessed in him*, because the New Testament tells us where that line ends up.

Paul says in Galatians that this promise was the gospel preached ahead of time to Abraham, and that the blessing reaches all the nations through Jesus Christ, who came from Abraham’s family line.

The man walking three strangers to the road is standing at the start of a story that runs all the way to the cross.

> “For I know him, that he will command his children and his household after him, and they shall keep the way of the LORD, to do justice and judgment; that the LORD may bring upon Abraham that which he hath spoken of him.” (Genesis 18:19)

Here is the reason God lets Abraham in. *For I know him*. That is relationship language.

God has chosen this man, walked with him for twenty four years, and knows what He has made him for.

And what has He made him for?

To *command his children and his household after him* to *keep the way of the LORD*, and to *do justice and judgment*.

**Justice** means doing right by people, treating them the way they ought to be treated.

**Judgment** here means making right decisions, the kind of decisions a good judge makes.

Abraham’s whole job is to raise a family that does right and decides right, and to pass that down for generations.

So think about what God is doing.

The man who has to teach justice to his children is about to watch God do justice to a city.

God is not just giving Abraham information.

He is giving him a front row seat to how the Judge of the whole earth handles wickedness, so that when Abraham teaches his household about right and wrong, he teaches it from what he saw, not from what he guessed.

## I Will Go Down Now

> “And the LORD said, Because the cry of Sodom and Gomorrah is great, and because their sin is very grievous; I will go down now, and see whether they have done altogether according to the cry of it, which is come unto me; and if not, I will know.” (Genesis 18:20–21)

Two things come up from Sodom. A **cry**, and a sin.

The cry is an outcry, the sound people make when they are being wronged and nobody is helping them.

Think of a city where people are getting hurt and their cries are going up, and the One who hears them is God.

And the sin is **grievous**, which means heavy. Severe. Not a few bad days. A weight.

Then God says, *I will go down now, and see*. Have you heard that before? Go back to Day 4.

At the tower of Babel, *the LORD came down to see the city and the tower* (Genesis 11:5). Same move.

Before God acts on a city, He goes down and looks.

Hear what God is really showing us. He is not acting on a rumor. He is not hitting a city because somebody complained.

He goes and sees for Himself whether the wrong matches the cry. Let Me look at this Myself.

That is what a careful judge does. God already knows everything, so this is not God catching up.

This is God letting us watch how He judges, so that nobody can ever say He was careless.

## Abraham Drew Near

> “And the men turned their faces from thence, and went toward Sodom: but Abraham stood yet before the LORD.” (Genesis 18:22)

The two men head down toward Sodom.

Tomorrow we will see them walk through the city gate, and the Bible will call them angels. But the LORD stays.

And Abraham stays with Him. *Stood yet before the LORD*. He does not go back to the tent.

He has just heard that God is about to do something to the city where Lot lives, and he is not leaving until he says what he has to say.

> “And Abraham drew near, and said, Wilt thou also destroy the righteous with the wicked?” (Genesis 18:23)

*Drew near*. He steps closer. Picture a man taking a step toward God to ask a question he knows is dangerous.

And here it is. Will You destroy the righteous with the wicked? The **righteous** are people who do right before God.

The **wicked** are people who do evil. Abraham is not saying Sodom is innocent. He heard the cry.

He is asking something sharper. If there are good people mixed in with the bad, do they burn too?

That is a real question. People have been asking it for as long as there have been people.

And look where Abraham brings it. Not to his tent to stew on it. Not to his friends to complain about it.

He brings it straight to God, standing in front of Him.

> “Peradventure there be fifty righteous within the city: wilt thou also destroy and not spare the place for the fifty righteous that are therein? That be far from thee to do after this manner, to slay the righteous with the wicked: and that the righteous should be as the wicked, that be far from thee: Shall not the Judge of all the earth do right?” (Genesis 18:24–25)

**Peradventure** means suppose. What if. Suppose there are fifty good people in there. He has not counted.

Fifty is where he opens. Then he says *that be far from thee*, which means that is not like You.

That would be beneath You. And then the sentence this whole chapter is remembered for.

*Shall not the Judge of all the earth do right?*

Hear what Abraham is doing. He is not telling God that God is wrong. He is appealing to who God is.

You are the Judge of everything. Judges do right. So do right.

He is holding God to God’s own character, and he is doing it to His face.

And remember who is in that city. This is not a debate for Abraham. Lot is down there.

The man he already risked his life to rescue once is living inside the place God just said He is going to look at.

Every number Abraham is about to say has his nephew’s face on it, even though he never says Lot’s name.

He asks for the city.

## Dust and Ashes

> “And the LORD said, If I find in Sodom fifty righteous within the city, then I will spare all the place for their sakes.” (Genesis 18:26)

Catch how big God’s answer is. Abraham asked whether the righteous would be destroyed with the wicked.

God answers with something larger.

If I find fifty, I will **spare**, which means hold back from destroying, *all the place*. The whole city.

For their sakes. Not just rescue the fifty and torch the rest.

Fifty righteous people would be enough for God to spare everybody around them.

A few people doing right could mean mercy for a whole city of people doing wrong.

That is how much weight God puts on the righteous.

> “And Abraham answered and said, Behold now, I have taken upon me to speak unto the Lord, which am but dust and ashes: Peradventure there shall lack five of the fifty righteous: wilt thou destroy all the city for lack of five? And he said, If I find there forty and five, I will not destroy it.” (Genesis 18:27–28)

Now watch how Abraham talks when he comes back the second time.

*I have taken upon me to speak unto the Lord, which am but dust and ashes*. He knows exactly who he is talking to.

**Dust and ashes** is Abraham saying, I am small, I am going to die, I am nothing next to You. And he asks anyway.

That is the posture to learn from this chapter. Bold enough to ask. Humble enough to remember whom you are asking.

And look at how clever his question is. What if five are missing? Would You destroy a whole city over five people?

He is not jumping to forty five. He is making it about the five.

And God says, if I find forty five, I will not destroy it.

> “And he spake unto him yet again, and said, Peradventure there shall be forty found there. And he said, I will not do it for forty’s sake. And he said unto him, Oh let not the Lord be angry, and I will speak: Peradventure there shall thirty be found there. And he said, I will not do it, if I find thirty there.” (Genesis 18:29–30)

Forty. Yes. Thirty. Yes. And hear Abraham’s voice getting more careful.

*Oh let not the Lord be angry, and I will speak*. Please do not be angry with me, but I have to ask again.

He is pushing, and he knows he is pushing, and he keeps going.

> “And he said, Behold now, I have taken upon me to speak unto the Lord: Peradventure there shall be twenty found there. And he said, I will not destroy it for twenty’s sake. And he said, Oh let not the Lord be angry, and I will speak yet but this once: Peradventure ten shall be found there. And he said, I will not destroy it for ten’s sake.” (Genesis 18:31–32)

Twenty. Yes. And then, *I will speak yet but this once*. One more time. Ten.

And God says, for ten, I will not destroy it.

Six times Abraham comes back. Fifty, forty five, forty, thirty, twenty, ten.

And six times God says yes without a single argument.

Stop and let that land, because a lot of people read this chapter wrong.

They picture a reluctant God being talked down by a clever man, like Abraham is haggling at a market and finally wears Him out.

That is backwards. God is not being talked into mercy.

He said yes to fifty before Abraham ever opened his mouth a second time. He says yes to every number.

The mercy was already in Him. Abraham is not changing God’s mind. He is finding out how far God’s mercy already goes.

And do not turn the numbers into a formula.

This is not a rule that says every town needs ten good people or it burns, and it is not a prayer trick where you count down until God gives in.

It is one conversation between God and one man about one city, and what it shows is a God who is willing, at every step, to spare the many for the sake of the few.

## Abraham Returned unto His Place

Abraham stops at ten. God says yes to ten. Then the conversation ends:

> “And the LORD went his way, as soon as he had left communing with Abraham: and Abraham returned unto his place.” (Genesis 18:33)

**Communing** means talking together. When God finished talking with Abraham, He left. And Abraham went home.

That is it. No answer about Sodom. No report on how many righteous people God found.

Abraham asked everything he had to ask, heard yes six times, and then had to walk back to his tent and leave the outcome with God.

That is what **intercession** is. It is a big church word for a simple thing.

Speaking to God on behalf of somebody else.

Abraham stood between God and a city full of people who did not know he was standing there, and he pleaded for them.

Then he let go. He did not follow the angels to Sodom. He did not try to fix it himself.

He asked, and he trusted the Judge.

Tomorrow we are going to find out that there were not ten. But hold on to this for later in the week.

When the smoke is rising, the Bible is going to say that *God remembered Abraham*, and brought Lot out.

The conversation on the road did not save the city. It was still heard.

## Every Answer Was Mercy

So look at the whole day.

God chose to tell a man what He was about to do, because that man was going to teach his family justice.

The man did not stay quiet. He stepped closer and asked the hardest question there is. Is this right?

And God did not strike him for asking. He answered him, six times, and every answer was mercy.

That is permission. You are allowed to bring God your hardest questions, the ones about fairness and suffering and why.

Bring them like Abraham did, close, honest, and remembering you are dust and ashes. It is also a responsibility.

Abraham did not ask for himself. He asked for a city of people who were not asking for themselves.

Somebody in your life is living in a Sodom right now, and nobody is standing in the gap for them.

And it is trust. When the talking is done, you go back to your place and leave it with the Judge of all the earth.

He does right.

He did right with Sodom, and tomorrow you are going to see exactly what that looked like for the one family living inside it.

Tomorrow, two angels walk into Sodom at evening, and the first man they meet is sitting at the city gate.

His name is Lot. But today is not finished yet.

Take the reflection questions first, then today’s action, and end your day in prayer.

## What To Watch For In Genesis 18

In verse 19, God explains why He is telling Abraham any of this. Read that verse twice.

It is the key to the whole conversation.

Watch God go down to see for Himself. We have seen Him do this once before, at a tower.

Count the numbers as Abraham brings them down. Listen to how his tone changes as the numbers get smaller.

**As you read, notice:**

* 📖 What God says Abraham will teach his children
* 😢 What has come up to God from Sodom
* ❓ The question Abraham asks about the Judge of all the earth
* 🤝 What God promises to do for the sake of a few righteous people

And think about this question: **Why would God let a man question Him like this?**

## Reflection Questions

1. **Abraham refused to write off a whole city because of its worst people. Who have you written off with the crowd they belong to?** God was willing to spare a city of wicked people for ten righteous ones. Ask Him to show you who you have stopped seeing as a person.
2. **What is the hard question about God, fairness, or suffering that you have been carrying around instead of bringing to Him?** Abraham stepped closer and asked it to God’s face, as dust and ashes. Ask yours the same way tonight.
3. **Who is living in a Sodom right now with nobody standing between them and the consequences?** Abraham interceded for people who did not know he was praying. Name the person. That is where today’s action starts.

## Today's One Thing

**Stand in the Gap**

Abraham stood before the LORD and pleaded for people who were not pleading for themselves. Do it for one person.

1. Write down the name of one person you know who is living in a destructive place or a destructive pattern right now.
2. Pray for them by name today, out loud, and be specific. Not bless them. Ask God for the exact thing they need.
3. Set a reminder on your phone to pray for them again tomorrow, and the next day. Six times, like Abraham.

One name, one specific prayer, one reminder set. Do it, and check it off.

## Today's Prayer

Father,

You are the Judge of all the earth, and You do right.

Before I ask You anything tonight, I want to say that I believe that, even on the days when I cannot see it.

Thank You that You let Abraham draw near. Thank You that You did not strike him for asking whether You were being fair.

You answered him, six times, and every answer was mercy. So I am going to draw near too.

**Here is my hard question, Lord. I am dust and ashes, and I am asking anyway.**

You know the question I have carried without saying it. The one about fairness, about suffering, about why.

I am bringing it to You instead of to everybody else. Do not be angry with me.

Help me hear Your answer, even if it is not the one I wanted.

And give me Abraham’s heart for people who are not praying for themselves.

There is somebody in my life living in a Sodom right now. I have written them off with the crowd. Forgive me for that.

Put their name in my mouth, and keep it there.

When I have said everything I have to say, teach me to go back to my place like Abraham did, and leave the outcome with You.

You remembered him. You will remember me.

**In Jesus’ name, amen.**
