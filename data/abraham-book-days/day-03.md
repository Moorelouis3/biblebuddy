Eight people walked off the ark. That was it. That was the entire human race. No other families. No other cities.

No other nations. One family, and an empty planet.

Try to imagine how quiet that world was.

You could walk for a year in any direction and never meet a stranger, because there were no strangers.

Everybody alive on the planet ate dinner in the same camp. That is where today begins.

Today we are reading Genesis chapter 10. It looks like one of those pages people skip.

It is a long list of names you cannot pronounce.

But do not skip it, because something enormous is hiding inside those names.

**The empty world is filling back up.**

Today we watch God’s command come true, name by name.

And buried in the middle of the list is a name that is about to change everything.

# The World Fills Back Up

> “These are the families of the sons of Noah… and by these were the nations divided in the earth after the flood.” (Genesis 10:32)

Genesis 10 opens like this:

> “Now these are the generations of the sons of Noah, Shem, Ham, and Japheth: and unto them were sons born after the flood.” (Genesis 10:1)

And then the names start. Thirty two verses of sons and grandsons and families.

Honestly, this is the kind of chapter most people flip past. It reads like a list.

But this list is one of the most important pages in the Bible.

Teachers call it the **Table of Nations**, because it traces how the families of Noah’s three sons became the nations of the earth.

The way the list is usually counted, seventy nations come out of this one chapter.

Now think back to Day 1. The first thing God said to this family was be fruitful, multiply, and replenish the earth.

Fill it back up. Genesis 10 is the receipt. It is God’s command coming true, name by name, family by family.

## The Isles of the Gentiles

The chapter walks through the three sons one at a time. Japheth first, then Ham, then Shem.

Watch what each section is doing.

Japheth’s section is short. His sons and grandsons spread out, and the Bible sums up his whole family in one line:

> “By these were the isles of the Gentiles divided in their lands; every one after his tongue, after their families, in their nations.” (Genesis 10:5)

Lands. Tongues. Families. Nations. Keep those words in your pocket, especially tongues.

**Tongues** means languages, and that word is going to raise a question before this day is over.

Some people try to match every name in this chapter to a modern country.

The Bible does not play that game, and the plain reading is enough.

Japheth’s family became peoples, and the peoples spread into their lands.

## The Land of Canaan

Then comes Ham’s family, and now we start recognizing names.

One of Ham’s sons is Canaan. We met him yesterday. He is the son Noah named in the curse.

And now Genesis shows us the family that comes from him, the Canaanites, and lists the peoples and borders that grew out of his line.

Do not skim past that name, because the land of Canaan is about to sit at the center of this whole book.

When Abraham enters the story, the land God sends him to is Canaan.

Hundreds of years later, when Israel comes out of Egypt, the land they are promised is Canaan.

The Bible is quietly setting the stage.

So who are the **Canaanites**? They are a people group, the same way the Israelites are a people group.

Israelites means the people who came from Israel. Canaanites means the people who came from Canaan, Ham’s son.

His family grew into tribes and cities and settled the land that ended up carrying his name, the land of Canaan.

So from now on, whenever you see the word Canaanites in your Bible, you will know exactly who they are.

Those are Canaan’s people.

## A Mighty Hunter Before the LORD

And then, in the middle of Ham’s list, the chapter suddenly slows down. Every other name gets a word or two.

One man gets his own story.

> “And Cush begat Nimrod: he began to be a mighty one in the earth. He was a mighty hunter before the LORD… And the beginning of his kingdom was Babel, and Erech, and Accad, and Calneh, in the land of Shinar.” (Genesis 10:8–10)

His name is Nimrod, and he is the first man in the Bible to be called mighty.

He is a hunter, a builder of kingdoms, a man with power.

People have piled up legends about Nimrod over the centuries, but we will stay with what the text actually says, because what it says is enough.

Look at the first city in his kingdom. Babel, in the land of Shinar.

Remember that name. Genesis drops it here almost in passing. Tomorrow, we are going there.

## The Road to Abraham

The chapter saves Shem for last, and that is not an accident.

Because Genesis is about to do something with Shem’s family that it does not do with anybody else’s.

From this point on, the Bible slowly stops following every nation on earth and starts narrowing.

Noah, then three sons, then many nations, and then one particular family line. Shem’s line.

And that line is going to lead to a man named Abram.

We are not there yet. Genesis 11 will walk us down that family tree step by step.

But this is where the road to Abraham begins. It begins in a list.

## After Their Tongues

Now, before you close this chapter, there is one more thing you need to see, and it is the most important thing in it.

Genesis 10 keeps repeating the same phrase over the families of all three sons:

* 👪 After their families
* 💬 After their tongues
* 🌄 In their lands
* 🌍 In their nations

That should stop you. Wait a minute. Every person in this chapter came from the same eight people. One family. One boat.

One language. So how did one family end up scattered across different lands, speaking different languages?

Genesis 10 never explains it. It just shows you the end result.

That is because Genesis is telling this part of the story in a special way.

Chapter 10 pulls the camera all the way back and shows you the big picture first, where everybody ended up.

Then chapter 11 zooms back in on one event that explains how it happened.

So do not read these chapters like chapter 10 finished and then Babel came later.

Babel is the reason chapter 10 looks the way it does.

## Bigger Than One Lifetime

So what does a chapter full of names give us? We do not need to force some giant lesson onto a list of families.

We just need to see what is really here.

Noah stood on empty ground with three sons and a command. He never saw most of what is in this chapter.

He could not have imagined it.

One command from God, fill the earth, worked itself out through children, and grandchildren, and great grandchildren, through families and cities and nations, long after Noah was gone.

That is how God works. Bigger than one lifetime. The people in this chapter are not heroes and prophets.

They are ordinary families having children and raising them. And God built nations out of that.

Eight people stepped onto an empty earth. Genesis 10 leaves us looking at families, cities, lands, and nations.

God’s command to fill the earth was becoming reality.

But there is something strange buried in this chapter. These families are not just living in different places.

They are speaking different languages. And they all came from the same man.

So what happened?

To understand that, Genesis takes us backward. To a plain in the land of Shinar.

To a city, and a tower, and a name we have already seen.

Babel.

We will go there tomorrow. But today is not finished yet.

Before you close the book, sit with the reflection questions, take today’s action, and end your day in prayer.

## What To Watch For In Genesis 10

This chapter is a long list of names. Do not let that scare you off. You do not need to pronounce them.

You need to see what the list is doing.

On Day 1, God told Noah’s family to fill the earth. This chapter shows it happening.

One man in the middle gets more attention than everybody else. Slow down when you reach him.

**As you read, notice:**

* 👥 The three sons the whole chapter is built around
* 🔁 The words that keep repeating: families, tongues, lands, nations
* 🏹 The man called a mighty hunter, and the first city in his kingdom
* 📍 The name Canaan, and the family that comes from him

And think about this question: **How did one family become all of this?**

## Reflection Questions

1. **Noah never saw the nations that came from his obedience. Can you trust God with things you will not live to see?** He walked off the boat and obeyed. The nations came later. Obedience plants what other people harvest.
2. **Genesis 10 is built out of ordinary families just living. What could God be building through your ordinary life?** Nobody in this chapter knew they were becoming a nation. They raised their children. God did the building.
3. **What parts of the Bible have you been skipping?** This chapter looks like a list until you slow down and find the story inside it. Skipped pages hold things like this.

## Today's One Thing
