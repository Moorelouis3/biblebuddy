Yesterday we left an old servant standing by a well at sunset, five hundred miles from home, ten camels kneeling behind him, praying a very specific prayer.

God, let the girl who offers to water my camels be the one.

He has sworn an oath with his hand under Abraham’s thigh. He has crossed the desert.

He has done everything a man can do, and now the future of the promise is hanging on whether God answers a prayer at a well in a foreign town.

Here is what I love about today. The answer does not come after a long wait. It comes while he is still talking.

A girl walks up with a jar on her shoulder.

You already know who she is, because the Bible told you her name three days ago. The servant does not know yet.

So he stands there, quiet, and watches a young woman work, and tries to figure out whether what he is looking at is an answer.

**Today God answers a prayer before it is finished.**

# Rebekah at the Well

> “I being in the way, the LORD led me to the house of my master’s brethren.” (Genesis 24:27)

Yesterday ended mid prayer. Here is the very next verse:

> “And it came to pass, before he had done speaking, that, behold, Rebekah came out, who was born to Bethuel, son of Milcah, the wife of Nahor, Abraham’s brother, with her pitcher upon her shoulder.” (Genesis 24:15)

*Before he had done speaking*. He is still praying. The amen is not out of his mouth yet, and here she comes.

God did not make this man wait a week at the well. The answer was already walking down the path while he was asking.

And look at what the Bible does for you, the reader. It tells you her whole family on the spot.

Rebekah, daughter of Bethuel, son of Milcah and Nahor, Abraham’s brother.

That is the exact family from the list on Day 25. You know, right now, that this is the girl. The servant does not.

He sees a young woman with a water jar on her shoulder.

That gap, between what you know and what he knows, is the tension of the whole scene.

> “And the damsel was very fair to look upon, a virgin, neither had any man known her: and she went down to the well, and filled her pitcher, and came up.” (Genesis 24:16)

The Bible gives you two things about her and then shows you a third. *Very fair to look upon* means beautiful.

*A virgin, neither had any man known her* is the Bible saying plainly, twice, that she has never been with a man.

**Known** is the old way of saying slept with.

That matters for the story because this is a bride for the son of the promise, an unmarried young woman of Abraham’s own family.

But the third thing is the one to watch. She goes down to the well, fills her jar, and comes back up.

This well is the kind you walk down into, steps cut to the water.

She is doing the evening chore, hauling a heavy jar of water up out of the ground and setting it on her shoulder.

Ordinary work. Watch what she does with it.

## For Thy Camels Also

> “And the servant ran to meet her, and said, Let me, I pray thee, drink a little water of thy pitcher. And she said, Drink, my lord: and she hasted, and let down her pitcher upon her hand, and gave him drink.” (Genesis 24:17–18)

He runs to her. He asks the exact question from his prayer. *I pray thee* means please. May I have a little water?

And she answers, *Drink, my lord*, which is a respectful sir, and she **hasted**. She hurries.

She swings the jar down off her shoulder onto her hand and gives a stranger a drink. No hesitation.

Now the moment everything hangs on. The first half of the sign is done. He asked. She gave.

Will she say the second half?

> “And when she had done giving him drink, she said, I will draw water for thy camels also, until they have done drinking. And she hasted, and emptied her pitcher into the trough, and ran again unto the well to draw water, and drew for all his camels.” (Genesis 24:19–20)

She says it. Word for word, the thing he prayed for. I will water your camels too, until they are done.

And then feel the size of what she just volunteered. Ten camels.

Camels that have just crossed hundreds of miles of desert.

A thirsty camel can drink a staggering amount, and every bit of it has to come up out of that well in her one jar.

She empties it into the **trough**, the long basin the animals drink from, and runs back down for more. And again.

And again. Until all ten have had enough. The Bible does not count the trips, so I will not either.

Just picture a young woman running up and down well steps with a full water jar for ten camels while a stranger stands there and watches.

That is not politeness. That is hard, sweaty, generous work for somebody she has never met.

Look at the words. Hasted. Ran. Hasted. Ran. Her feet tell you everything.

The servant asked God to show him a woman’s heart through her hands, and Rebekah is showing him with both hands and both feet.

## Held His Peace

> “And the man wondering at her held his peace, to wit whether the LORD had made his journey prosperous or not.” (Genesis 24:21)

And what does the man do? *Wondering at her* means watching her in amazement. *Held his peace* means he kept quiet.

*To wit* means to find out.

He stood there, amazed, and said nothing, trying to figure out whether what he was watching was God’s answer.

Imagine the discipline that takes. He just prayed for exactly this. It is happening in front of his eyes.

Every part of him must have wanted to shout. And he keeps his mouth shut and watches.

Because the sign is not the whole answer. He still does not know whose daughter this is.

A generous girl from the wrong family is not the bride. So he waits, and lets her finish, and does not get ahead of God.

> “And it came to pass, as the camels had done drinking, that the man took a golden earring of half a shekel weight, and two bracelets for her hands of ten shekels weight of gold.” (Genesis 24:22)

When the last camel is done, he brings out gifts.

The King James says **earring**, but later in the chapter he says he put it on her nose.

It is a nose ring, a normal piece of jewelry for a woman in that world.

Half a shekel of gold, around five or six grams, a little nugget of gold.

And two bracelets for her wrists, ten shekels of gold between them, which is about a hundred and fifteen grams, roughly a quarter of a pound of gold on her arms.

Do not read those numbers like the silver prices from Day 24. Same word, shekel, because it is a weight.

Different metal entirely. Four hundred shekels of silver bought a field.

Ten shekels of gold is a quarter pound of gold, and gold was worth far more than silver by weight.

These are not trinkets. A stranger just hung a small fortune on a girl at a well.

That is Abraham’s wealth talking, and it is saying, the man who sent me is serious.

## Whose Daughter Art Thou

> “And said, Whose daughter art thou? tell me, I pray thee: is there room in thy father’s house for us to lodge in? And she said unto him, I am the daughter of Bethuel the son of Milcah, which she bare unto Nahor. She said moreover unto him, We have both straw and provender enough, and room to lodge in.” (Genesis 24:23–25)

Now the two questions. Whose daughter are you? And is there room at your father’s house for us to stay the night?

And she answers the first one with the exact words you have been waiting for. Bethuel. Milcah. Nahor.

The servant has never met these people. He only had Abraham’s instructions. Go to my family.

And the first generous girl at the well turns out to be Abraham’s brother’s granddaughter.

The sign and the family line just locked together.

Then look at her second answer, because it is the camel thing all over again. He asked for a place to sleep.

She says yes to that, and adds **straw and provender**, which means bedding and feed for the animals, enough for all ten camels and the men with him.

She was not asked for that. She just sees a traveling party and takes care of all of it. That is who she is.

More than was asked, every time.

## The LORD Led Me

> “And the man bowed down his head, and worshipped the LORD. And he said, Blessed be the LORD God of my master Abraham, who hath not left destitute my master of his mercy and his truth: I being in the way, the LORD led me to the house of my master’s brethren.” (Genesis 24:26–27)

The man who held his peace finally lets go.

Right there at the well, in front of the girl and the camels, he bows his head and worships. Yesterday he prayed.

Today he gives thanks. That is the right shape for a life. Ask first. Then, when God moves, stop and say so.

And hear what he says. *Who hath not left destitute my master of his mercy and his truth*.

Destitute here does not mean Abraham went broke. It means God has not left Abraham without His mercy and His truth.

His mercy is His loyal love, the kindness the servant asked for yesterday.

His truth is His faithfulness, His keeping of His word.

God did not withhold either one from Abraham, even on an errand five hundred miles from Abraham’s tent.

Then the sentence I would put over this whole day. *I being in the way, the LORD led me*.

While I was on the road, God was leading me. Remember what Abraham said on Day 25. He shall send his angel before thee.

The servant never saw an angel. He saw a girl with a jar. But standing at that well, he knows somebody got there first.

He walked, and God led. Both at the same time.

> “And the damsel ran, and told them of her mother’s house these things.” (Genesis 24:28)

And Rebekah runs again. This time home, to her mother’s household, to tell them what just happened at the well.

*Her mother’s house* just means her family, the women’s side of the household where a girl would run with news like this.

It does not mean her father is gone.

Picture a girl bursting into the tents with gold on her nose and her wrists and a story about a stranger and ten camels.

## Hold Your Peace Until You Know

So let’s take stock of where we are, because the servant would.

He prayed for a sign, and God gave it before he finished the sentence.

He asked for a woman whose character showed through her hands, and she watered ten camels and offered to feed them.

He needed her to be from Abraham’s family, and she is Nahor’s granddaughter. He has found her.

But hold on. Finding her is not the same as bringing her home.

There is a family in those tents who has never heard of Isaac.

There is a brother and a father who have to agree to send their girl five hundred miles away to marry a man she has never seen.

And there is Rebekah herself. Remember what Abraham said. If she is not willing, you are released.

Nobody is going to force her. She has to choose.

So the servant has his answer, and he still does not have his bride. God led him to the house.

What happens inside the house is tomorrow.

Here is what to take from the well tonight. God was answering while the man was still asking.

God showed a woman’s heart through her work.

And the man who had every reason to shout kept quiet until he was sure, then bowed his head and thanked God out loud.

Pray specific. Watch for character. Hold your peace until you know. Then worship.

Tomorrow, Rebekah’s brother comes running out to the well, and he has noticed the gold. But today is not finished yet.

Take the reflection questions first, then today’s action, and end your day in prayer.

## What To Watch For In Genesis 24

Look at the first four words of verse 15. Timing is the whole point.

You know Rebekah’s family from Day 25. The servant does not. Watch him find out.

Count how many times Rebekah hurries or runs. Her feet tell you who she is.

**As you read, notice:**

* 🐪 What Rebekah offers that the servant did not ask for
* 🤫 What the servant does while she works
* ❓ The two questions he asks her after the camels are done
* 🙇 What the servant does the moment he hears her father’s name

And think about this question: **What does the way you work say about who you are?**

## Reflection Questions

1. **When has God answered you before you finished asking, and did you notice at the time?** The girl was already on the path while the servant was still praying. Look back for the answers that were walking toward you.
2. **Rebekah did more than was asked, twice, for a stranger. What does the way you work when nobody important is watching say about your heart?** The servant judged her by her hands and feet. People are judging you the same way.
3. **The servant held his peace until he was sure, then worshiped out loud. Which one do you struggle with, the waiting or the thanking?** Ask first, then watch, then stop and give God credit. Check which step you skip.

## Today's One Thing

**Water the Camels**

Rebekah was asked for a drink and gave ten camels their fill. Do more than you were asked today.

1. Pick one task somebody asks of you today. A small favor, a chore, a request at work.
2. Do it, and then do the next thing they did not ask for but obviously need. Water the camels. Offer the straw and feed.
3. Before bed, go back to the prayer you wrote down yesterday, and write one thing God has already done about it, even a small thing.

One task, one extra, one answer noticed. Do it, and check it off.

## Today's Prayer

Father,

You answer before I finish asking. The girl was already on the path. The ram was already in the thicket.

The well was already there. Thank You that Your answers are usually walking toward me while I am still talking.

Make me a Rebekah. Quick to serve. Quick to do more than I was asked. Generous with strangers who can do nothing for me.

Let my hands and my feet say who I am before my mouth gets the chance.

**And when You move, let me hold my peace until I am sure, and then let me worship out loud.**

I have been in the way, Lord, on roads I did not choose, to places I did not understand.

Lead me there the way You led that servant. Go ahead of me. Get there first.

Thank You that You have not left me destitute of Your mercy or Your truth. Not once. Not on any road.

**In Jesus’ name, amen.**
