The sun is up. Lot just walked through the gate of a little town called Zoar with his wife and his two daughters, and the angel said nothing could happen until he got there.

He is there.

Today is only six verses. But they are six of the heaviest verses in the whole Bible, and I do not want to rush through them. Before the fire falls, I want to back up and look at what is actually about to be destroyed. Not just two names you have heard in a thousand sermons. A whole region. Five cities. People who ate breakfast that morning. Fields somebody planted. A valley the Bible once compared to the garden of the LORD.

And I want to be honest about what was wrong with it, because most people only know half of that answer.

Then we are going to stand next to Abraham on the hill where he asked God for ten, and watch the smoke come up.

**Today judgment comes, and so does mercy.**

## What To Watch For In Genesis 19

Before you open to Genesis 19, flip back to Genesis 14:2 and read the names of the five kings. Four of those cities are about to disappear.

Verse 25 lists everything that was destroyed. Read the list slowly. It is bigger than two cities.

The last verse tells you why Lot got out. It is not the reason you would expect.

**As you read, notice:**

* What falls from heaven, and where it comes from
* What Lot’s wife does, and what happens to her
* Where Abraham is standing when he sees the smoke
* Whom God remembered when He brought Lot out

And think about this question: **What was Sodom actually guilty of?**

> “God remembered Abraham, and sent Lot out of the midst of the overthrow.” (Genesis 19:29)

# Fire on the Plain

Let’s go back to Genesis 14, because Sodom and Gomorrah are not alone in this.

> “That these made war with Bera king of Sodom, and with Birsha king of Gomorrah, Shinab king of Admah, and Shemeber king of Zeboiim, and the king of Bela, which is Zoar.” (Genesis 14:2)

Five cities. Sodom, Gomorrah, Admah, Zeboiim, and Bela, which is Zoar. We met them on Day 11. These are the five kings who banded together and rebelled against the four kings from the east. They lost that war, and that is how Lot got captured and Abraham had to go get him. So when you hear Sodom and Gomorrah, do not picture two cities sitting by themselves in the desert. Picture a cluster of five towns in one valley, close enough to fight the same war on the same day.

The Bible calls them the **cities of the plain**. The plain is the flat, low country around the Jordan River and the Dead Sea. And this was not wasteland. Remember what Lot saw when he picked it on Day 10:

> “And Lot lifted up his eyes, and beheld all the plain of Jordan, that it was well watered every where, before the LORD destroyed Sodom and Gomorrah, even as the garden of the LORD, like the land of Egypt, as thou comest unto Zoar.” (Genesis 13:10)

Well watered everywhere. Like the garden of the LORD. Like Egypt, where the Nile makes everything green. This was the best looking land in the whole region, with fields and springs and five towns full of people living off of it. And notice the little phrase tucked in there. *Before the LORD destroyed Sodom and Gomorrah*. The Bible is telling you that the valley you are reading about does not look like that anymore.

Where were they? Somewhere around the Dead Sea, which is the lowest spot on the face of the earth, so salty nothing lives in it. Archaeologists have dug up real ruins from Abraham’s general time period at the south end of the Dead Sea, old walled towns that were burned and abandoned, and some think those are the cities of the plain. Others point to a site north of the Dead Sea. The ruins are real. The name tags are not. Nobody has found a sign that says Sodom. What we know for sure is that there was a living valley there once, and the Bible says God ended it.

And it was not just two cities that ended. Listen to Moses looking back on it hundreds of years later:

> “And that the whole land thereof is brimstone, and salt, and burning, that it is not sown, nor beareth, nor any grass groweth therein, like the overthrow of Sodom, and Gomorrah, Admah, and Zeboim, which the LORD overthrew in his anger, and in his wrath.” (Deuteronomy 29:23)

Four names. Sodom, Gomorrah, Admah, and Zeboiim. All four overthrown. Four of the five cities from Genesis 14 went up in the same fire. The fifth one, Bela, is the little town that got renamed Zoar yesterday, and it survived for exactly one reason. Lot asked. One scared man’s request on the road at dawn is why there were five cities before the fire and one after.

Now, what was going on down there? Here is where most people only know half the story. Ask somebody what Sodom’s sin was, and they will say sexual sin. And they are not wrong. We watched the whole city, old and young, surround Lot’s house demanding to rape two strangers. The New Testament says it plainly:

> “Even as Sodom and Gomorrha, and the cities about them in like manner, giving themselves over to fornication, and going after strange flesh, are set forth for an example, suffering the vengeance of eternal fire.” (Jude 7)

**Fornication** is sex outside of what God designed. *Strange flesh* is sex that goes after what it was never meant to have. And Jude says it was not just Sodom. *The cities about them in like manner*. The whole valley lived like that. So yes, sexual sin and sexual violence are in the charge sheet, and the mob at Lot’s door proved it.

But hundreds of years later, God sent the prophet Ezekiel to tell Jerusalem that they were acting like Sodom, and listen to what He put first:

> “Behold, this was the iniquity of thy sister Sodom, pride, fulness of bread, and abundance of idleness was in her and in her daughters, neither did she strengthen the hand of the poor and needy. And they were haughty, and committed abomination before me: therefore I took them away as I saw good.” (Ezekiel 16:49–50)

Read that list. **Pride**. *Fulness of bread*, which means they had more than enough to eat. *Abundance of idleness*, which means they were comfortable and bored, with time on their hands and nothing they had to work for. And *neither did she strengthen the hand of the poor and needy*. They had plenty and they did not help anybody. Then *haughty*, which is pride again, looking down on people. And then **abomination**, the thing God finds disgusting, which is where the mob comes in.

Put it in everyday terms. Sodom was a rich valley full of people who were full, proud, lazy, and cold. Full fridge, full calendar of nothing, and a poor man at the gate they stepped over on the way to dinner. The sexual violence was the ugliest flower on the plant. The root was pride and comfort and not caring about anybody but themselves. That is a sobering list, because a lot of us would never join a mob, and a lot of us know exactly what fulness of bread and abundance of idleness feel like.

And remember the cry. Yesterday God said the cry of Sodom had come up to Him. Somebody down there was getting hurt and crying out. The poor and needy nobody strengthened. The strangers who slept in the square. The cry went up, God came down and looked, and now here we are.

> “Then the LORD rained upon Sodom and upon Gomorrah brimstone and fire from the LORD out of heaven.” (Genesis 19:24)

**Brimstone** is an old word for sulfur. It is a yellow rock that burns, and when it burns it gives off a blue flame and a smell like rotten eggs that gets in your throat. So picture burning sulfur and fire coming down out of the sky. Not a storm. Not something the people down there had ever seen. *From the LORD out of heaven*. The Bible is clear about where it came from. This was not weather. This was God.

> “And he overthrew those cities, and all the plain, and all the inhabitants of the cities, and that which grew upon the ground.” (Genesis 19:25)

Read the list slowly, because it is four things, not one. *Those cities*. The towns themselves, the walls, the houses, Lot’s house, the gate he sat in. *All the plain*. The whole green valley that looked like the garden of the LORD. *All the inhabitants*. Every person in four cities, old and young. *And that which grew upon the ground*. The crops, the grass, the trees. The land itself.

Think about how much that is. A region. Every person in four cities. Every farm. The ground so ruined that Moses could still point at it generations later and say nothing grows there. **Overthrew** means turned upside down. God turned the whole valley over, and it never turned back.

This is not the first time we have seen God judge in this book, and I want you to hear that. God put Adam and Eve out of the garden. He marked Cain and sent him wandering. He flooded the whole earth in Noah’s day. He scattered the people at Babel. He struck Pharaoh’s house because of Sarai back on Day 9. God has been judging sin since Genesis 3. Sodom is not God losing His temper out of nowhere. It is the same God who has always taken sin seriously, taking it seriously again.

And the one that lines up closest with Sodom is the flood. Jesus Himself put them side by side:

> “Likewise also as it was in the days of Lot; they did eat, they drank, they bought, they sold, they planted, they builded; But the same day that Lot went out of Sodom it rained fire and brimstone from heaven, and destroyed them all.” (Luke 17:28–29)

Listen to Jesus describe Sodom’s last morning. Eating, drinking, buying, selling, planting, building. Normal life. Nobody was looking up. Same as the days of Noah. People were getting married and going to work right up until the rain started. That is the connection. Judgment did not come in the middle of a riot. It came on an ordinary day while people were making ordinary plans, and the only people who got out were the ones God pulled out first. Noah had an ark. Lot had two angels holding his hand.

Then, a few verses later, Jesus says three words. *Remember Lot’s wife*. So let’s remember her:

> “But his wife looked back from behind him, and she became a pillar of salt.” (Genesis 19:26)

The command yesterday was clear. *Look not behind thee*. And she looked. *From behind him* means she was trailing behind Lot, and she turned around to see what was happening to the city they had just left. And she became a pillar of salt. Right there on the road. A standing block of salt in a valley that is still full of salt to this day.

Do not read that as a woman getting zapped for being curious. Think about what she was looking at. Her house. Her neighbors. The two sons in law who stayed. Years of life. Everything she had built was behind her, and when it started to burn, she could not keep her eyes forward. Her body left Sodom. Her heart was still back there.

That is why Jesus says remember her. When God pulls you out of something, the danger is not just the thing behind you. It is you turning back toward it. Longing for it. Grieving it like it was good. You can be outside the city with your feet and still be inside it with your eyes. Lot’s wife made it out of Sodom and still died looking at it.

> “And Abraham gat up early in the morning to the place where he stood before the LORD: And he looked toward Sodom and Gomorrah, and toward all the land of the plain, and beheld, and, lo, the smoke of the country went up as the smoke of a furnace.” (Genesis 19:27–28)

Now the camera goes back up the hill. Abraham gets up early and goes back to the exact spot where he stood before the LORD the day before. The place where he asked, fifty? Forty five? Forty? Ten? He walks back there and looks down.

And the whole valley is smoke. *As the smoke of a furnace*, thick and black, pouring up like a kiln. Imagine standing where he stood. He did not get a report. He did not get a yes or no. He got up early, walked to the spot where he had pleaded for that city, and watched it burn. There were not ten.

Picture what is going through him. Lot is down there. His nephew, the boy he raised, the man he rescued. As far as Abraham can see from that hill, Lot is in that smoke. He asked God for the city, and the city is gone.

And then the last verse of today tells you what Abraham could not see from the hill:

> “And it came to pass, when God destroyed the cities of the plain, that God remembered Abraham, and sent Lot out of the midst of the overthrow, when he overthrew the cities in the which Lot dwelt.” (Genesis 19:29)

God remembered Abraham. Not Lot. Abraham.

Stop on that, because it is the whole point of the last three days. When God pulled Lot out of Sodom, the Bible does not say it was because Lot was righteous, or because Lot asked, or because Lot deserved it. It says God remembered Abraham. The old man on the hill who stood before the LORD and said, shall not the Judge of all the earth do right? That conversation did not save the city. There were not ten. But it was heard, and God answered it in a way Abraham never asked for and could not see. He sent Lot out of the middle of the overthrow.

So judgment and mercy are standing in the same valley this morning. Four cities overthrown, and one man walked out because somebody prayed for him. The fire was real. The cry had gone up, God had come down and looked, and He did exactly what the Judge of all the earth does. But inside that judgment, there was a hand on Lot’s wrist, and the reason for that hand was a prayer on a hill.

That is still how God works. He takes sin seriously, all of it, the pride and the full fridge and the ignored poor man and the mob at the door. He does not wink at any of it. And at the same time, He remembers the people who stand before Him and plead for others. Your prayers for somebody in a Sodom are not wasted just because the Sodom burns. God remembers. He sends people out of the middle of things.

Tomorrow, Lot is afraid again, and he ends up in a cave in the very mountains he refused to run to. What happens in that cave is one of the hardest stories in Genesis. But today is not finished yet. Take the reflection questions first, then today’s action, and end your day in prayer.

## Reflection Questions

1. **Ezekiel put pride, fullness of bread, and idleness ahead of everything else on Sodom’s list. Which of those three is closest to home for you?** Most of us would never join the mob. Be honest about the full fridge, the empty calendar, and the poor man you stepped over this week.
2. **What has God pulled you out of that you still keep turning around to look at?** Lot’s wife left Sodom with her feet and stayed with her eyes. Remember her. Where is your heart still standing in a city God burned?
3. **Abraham prayed for a city, and God answered by saving one man he never named. Where might God be answering your prayers in a way you cannot see from your hill?** He remembered Abraham. He remembers you. Keep standing in that spot.

## Today's One Thing

**Strengthen a Hand**

Sodom had plenty and did not strengthen the hand of the poor and needy. Be the opposite of Sodom today.

1. Write down the name of one person near you who is poor or in need right now. Not a cause. A person.
2. Today, strengthen their hand with something that costs you. Groceries, gas money, a bill, your afternoon. Do it before the sun goes down.
3. Then go back to the spot where you pray for the person from Day 16, and pray for them again. God remembers.

One hand strengthened, one prayer repeated. Do it, and check it off.

## Today's Prayer

Father,

You are the Judge of all the earth, and today I watched You do right. I do not take that lightly. You take sin seriously, and I want to take it as seriously as You do.

I am not in the mob, Lord, but I know the rest of Sodom’s list. I know pride. I know a full table and a bored heart. I know what it is to step over somebody in need because I was comfortable. Forgive me for every part of Sodom that lives in me.

**Do not let me look back at what You have burned, and do not let me stop praying for the people still in it.**

I remember Lot’s wife. Keep my eyes forward. When You pull me out of something, help me leave it with my heart and not just my feet.

And thank You that You remembered Abraham. Thank You that a prayer on a hill reached into the middle of the fire and pulled one man out. I have people in Sodom, Father. I am standing in my spot for them. Remember me, and send them out.

Make me the opposite of that valley. Humble instead of proud. Generous instead of full. Awake instead of idle. And strengthen my hand so I can strengthen somebody else’s.

**In Jesus’ name, amen.**
