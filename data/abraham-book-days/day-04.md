Yesterday ended with a question. If every nation on earth came from the same man, how did his family end up scattered across the world, speaking different languages?

Today Genesis answers it. The camera zooms back in. One plain. One people. One language. And one giant project.

They are about to build the biggest thing human hands have ever built. And they are about to do it for all the wrong reasons.

This is one of the most famous stories in the Bible, and one of the most misunderstood. People remember the tower. They forget why it was built, what God actually thought of it, and what it has to do with how you and I live right now. Because the truth is, Babel never really ended. We just build with different bricks.

**Today we are reading about Babel.**

## What To Watch For In Genesis 11

Yesterday left us with a question about the languages. This passage is the answer.

Listen to what the builders say they want. They tell you their reason out loud.

Watch what God does when He wants to see the tower. Notice where He has to go.

**As you read, notice:**

* How many languages the whole earth starts with
* The two things the builders want to build
* What the builders say they are afraid of
* What the LORD does to their language

And think about this question: **Why does this tower bother God?**

> “Let us build us a city and a tower, whose top may reach unto heaven; and let us make us a name, lest we be scattered abroad upon the face of the whole earth.” (Genesis 11:4)

# The Tower and the Name

The chapter opens on a world we can barely imagine:

> “And the whole earth was of one language, and of one speech.” (Genesis 11:1)

One language. Everybody on earth could talk to everybody else. And as the people moved east, they found a wide open plain in a land called Shinar, and the text says they dwelt there.

We saw the name Shinar yesterday. It is Nimrod territory, the land where his kingdom began. And that little phrase, they dwelt there, is the first crack in the story.

Because think back to Day 1. God’s command to this family was clear. Be fruitful, multiply, fill the earth. Spread out. And instead of spreading out, they found one comfortable spot and settled in it, all together.

Then they start talking:

> “And they said, Go to, let us build us a city and a tower, whose top may reach unto heaven; and let us make us a name, lest we be scattered abroad upon the face of the whole earth.” (Genesis 11:4)

**Go to** is an old way of saying come on, let us do this. So listen to the plan:

* A city, so they can stay together
* A tower that reaches the sky
* A name for themselves

And catch that last line, because they say the quiet part out loud. Lest we be scattered. They know God wants the earth filled. Scattering is the one thing God asked for. And it is the one thing they are building against.

Now, about that tower. When the Bible says its top may reach unto heaven, that does not necessarily mean they believed they could build stairs into God’s throne room. In their language, the sky itself was called heaven. So this means they wanted to build something taller than anything human eyes had ever seen. A tower that touched the clouds.

Think of a skyscraper. That is what this is. The first skyscraper. And think about why cities still race to build the tallest building in the world today. It is not for the extra office space. It is so the whole world knows their name. A building that big is a legacy. People will come from everywhere to see it, and everyone who sees it will know who built it.

That is what let us make us a name means. They wanted to be known. They wanted to be remembered. They wanted the world looking up at what they built, saying those people are great.

And here is the thing. Wanting to build is not the sin. Wanting your life to matter is not the sin. The question is who you are building for. This tower was a name built without God, for the world’s approval. Not one brick of it was for Him. It was a monument to themselves, built by people hiding from the very thing God told them to do.

We still build like that. We just use different bricks:

* Followers
* Titles
* Money
* Reputation
* A name people recognize

None of those things are evil by themselves. But when the whole project is us, made by us, for the approval of the world, we are standing on that same plain in Shinar.

And hold on to this word name, because it is about to matter. A few chapters from now, God is going to walk up to one ordinary man and say, *I will make thy name great*. The people of Babel tried to make their own name. God is going to show us a man who was given one. The difference between those two things is where this whole book is going.

Now watch what God does about the tower. This might be the most quietly funny verse in Genesis:

> “And the LORD came down to see the city and the tower, which the children of men builded.” (Genesis 11:5)

Read that again slowly. The tallest thing human hands had ever built. A tower with its top in the heavens. The pride of the whole human race.

And God had to come down to see it.

From where God sits, the greatest project mankind ever attempted was so small He came down to look at it. They were building up, up, up, straining for the sky. And heaven was not even close.

Sit with that picture for a minute, because it is not just about towers. It is about everything that looks huge from down here.

Your problems work the same way. From where you stand, they tower over you. The bill. The diagnosis. The broken relationship. The thing you cannot fix. It fills your whole sky. But from where God sits, the biggest thing you will ever face is small enough that He has to come down to look at it. That does not mean your problems are not real. It means they are not bigger than Him. Nothing is. The same God who looked down on Babel looks down on the mountain in your life, and He is not impressed by its size.

So God ends the project. And notice how He does it. No fire. No flood. He just touches their words:

> “So the LORD scattered them abroad from thence upon the face of all the earth: and they left off to build the city. Therefore is the name of it called Babel; because the LORD did there confound the language of all the earth.” (Genesis 11:8–9)

**Confound** means to mix up. One morning the builders opened their mouths and could not understand each other anymore. And a project that big dies the moment people cannot agree. The bricks did not fail. The words did.

So the people split up by language, walked away from their half built tower, and spread out across the earth. And there is the answer to yesterday’s question. The nations of Genesis 10, in their lands, after their tongues. Babel is where the tongues came from.

And do not miss what actually happened. God said fill the earth. They said no, we will stay together and build. And they ended up filling the earth anyway. God’s plan did not need their cooperation. His will was going to happen either way. The only thing their disobedience changed was how it happened.

That is worth remembering, because this whole book is about obedience. God’s plan is coming true either way. The question is never whether God’s will happens. The question is whether you get the blessing of being part of it willingly, or whether it rolls right over your tower.

They wanted a name, and they got one. Babel. It means confusion. That is what building without God leaves behind.

And Genesis is done with the whole world now. Starting tomorrow, the Bible stops following all the nations and walks down one single family line, name by name, generation by generation, until it stops at one man. The man God will give a name to.

We will meet him soon. But today is not finished yet. Take the reflection questions first, then today’s action, and end your day in prayer.

## Reflection Questions

1. **Whose approval are you building for?** The builders said let us make us a name. Be honest about whose name your hardest work is really making.
2. **Is there something God told you to do that you have been settling instead of obeying?** They knew the command was to spread out, and they built a city to stay put. Naming your Shinar is the first step to leaving it.
3. **What problem in your life looks like a tower right now?** God had to come down just to see the biggest thing people ever built. Your tower is not big to Him either.

## Today's One Thing

**Check Whose Name It Is**

The builders told the truth about their motive: let us make us a name. Today, tell the truth about yours.

1. Write down the three things you have worked hardest on lately.
2. Next to each one, write who it is really for: God, other people’s approval, or your own name.
3. Pick one of them and give it to God today. Say out loud: this one is for Your name, not mine.

Three things, three honest labels, one handed over. Write it, say it, and check it off.

## Today's Prayer

Father,

You saw the tower at Babel, and You see the ones I build too.

I want to be honest with You. I have spent real time and real strength making a name for myself. Chasing approval. Wanting people to look at what I built and know who I am. Some of my towers took years.

**Forgive me for building without You.**

I do not want a name I made up for myself. I want the name You give. So change what I am building for. Let my work, my family, my gifts, and my days build Your kingdom first, and let You be the One who decides what my name is worth.

And Father, You know the towers I did not build. The problems standing over me right now that fill up my whole sky. I confess that I stare up at them like they are bigger than You.

Remind me where You sit. Remind me that You had to come down just to look at the greatest thing people ever made. Nothing I am facing is tall from where You are.

So I am handing You the tallest one today. You know which one it is. I trust You with it.

Thank You that Your plan does not depend on my perfection, and thank You for wanting me in it anyway.

**In Jesus’ name, amen.**
