Life has gotten good at Beersheba.

The well is secured. The king is a friend. The tree is growing. And the little boy who was weaned at the big feast has grown up into a young man who can carry a load of firewood up a mountain. Picture him at about thirteen, old enough to work beside his father, young enough to still call him My father in that voice sons have. Abraham has spent a decade pouring everything he has into this boy. Every lesson. Every story. Every hope he ever had for the future.

Then God speaks again. And this time He does not ask Abraham to leave a country, or wait on a promise, or send a son away with food and water. He asks him to take Isaac up a mountain and offer him as a burnt offering.

I have read this chapter more times than I can count, and it still stops me. The son of the promise. The one God said the whole future runs through. And God says, give him to Me.

What Abraham does with that command, and what God does at the top of that mountain, is the center of this whole book.

**Today God asks for everything, and then He provides.**

## What To Watch For In Genesis 22

The very first verse tells you something Abraham does not know. Read the whole chapter remembering that he never heard that sentence.

Count how many times somebody says Here am I. Watch who says it, and to whom.

Verse 5 contains a promise Abraham makes to his servants. Read it carefully. It tells you what he believed.

**As you read, notice:**

* How long the journey takes before Abraham sees the place
* What Isaac carries, and what Abraham carries
* The question Isaac asks, and the answer his father gives
* What is caught in the thicket, and what Abraham names the place

And think about this question: **What is the one thing you would not give God if He asked?**

> “My son, God will provide himself a lamb for a burnt offering: so they went both of them together.” (Genesis 22:8)

# The Test on the Mountain

Yesterday ended with Abraham under his tree at Beersheba, calling on the everlasting God. Here is how today opens:

> “And it came to pass after these things, that God did tempt Abraham, and said unto him, Abraham: and he said, Behold, here I am.” (Genesis 22:1)

*After these things*. After the treaty, the well, the tree, the long peaceful stay. Time has passed. The Bible does not say how much, but Isaac has gone from a toddler to a boy who can carry wood, so picture years, not days. A good stretch of quiet. And then God speaks.

Now the word that trips everybody up. The King James says God did **tempt** Abraham. In the old English, tempt did not mean what it means now. It meant test. Put to the proof. God is not trying to get Abraham to sin. He is about to find out, in action, what this man’s trust is made of.

And catch something. You, the reader, just got told it is a test. Abraham did not. God says his name, and Abraham answers, *Behold, here I am*. I am here. I am listening. That is the right way to answer when God calls. Go back to the garden, where God called Adam and Adam was hiding in the trees. Abraham is not hiding. He is standing up, saying, here I am, before he knows what is coming. Hold on to that answer, because you are going to hear it two more times today.

> “And he said, Take now thy son, thine only son Isaac, whom thou lovest, and get thee into the land of Moriah; and offer him there for a burnt offering upon one of the mountains which I will tell thee of.” (Genesis 22:2)

Read how God says it. Thy son. Thine only son. Isaac. Whom thou lovest. Four steps, each one closer, like God is making absolutely sure there is no mistake about who He means. Your son. The only one of the promise. The one named Laughter. The one you love.

And then the command. Offer him for a **burnt offering**. Let me say plainly what that means, because the whole chapter depends on it. A sacrifice is something given to God in worship. In a burnt offering, an animal was killed, laid on an altar, and burned up completely. Nothing kept, nothing eaten. All of it given to God. We have seen it before in this book. Noah built an altar after the flood and offered burnt offerings. Abel brought the best of his flock. Abraham cut animals in half on Day 12. Abraham knows exactly what a burnt offering is. He has done it with animals.

Now God names a person. His son.

And here is the problem that should be screaming in your head. God said the promise runs through Isaac. Day 14. Day 21. *In Isaac shall thy seed be called*. The nations, the stars, the blessing for the whole earth, all of it through this boy. And now God says to kill the boy. How can both be true? Hold that question. Abraham is about to answer it with his feet.

The place is **Moriah**, a region of hills to the north, and God says He will point out which mountain. Hundreds of years later, the Bible tells us Solomon built the temple on Mount Moriah in Jerusalem. So the tradition is that Abraham walked to the hills where Jerusalem would stand. Notice the pattern too. On Day 7, God said go to a land I will show you. Now, go to a mountain I will tell you of. Same God. Same kind of obedience. Walk first, see the place later.

> “And Abraham rose up early in the morning, and saddled his ass, and took two of his young men with him, and Isaac his son, and clave the wood for the burnt offering, and rose up, and went unto the place of which God had told him.” (Genesis 22:3)

There it is again. *Rose up early in the morning*. Two days ago, he rose up early to send Ishmael away. Now he rises up early to take Isaac up a mountain. Both times it is the hardest thing he has ever done. Both times he does not sleep on it. Let that sink into you about this man. His obedience is not slow.

And look at how concrete it is. He saddles the donkey. He takes two young servants to help with the loads and the animal on a multi day trip. He takes Isaac. And he **clave** the wood, which means he split it. Picture that. The old man standing over a log with an axe, splitting the firewood for the fire that is supposed to burn his son. Every swing of that axe is obedience. He is not promising to obey. He is packing for it.

> “Then on the third day Abraham lifted up his eyes, and saw the place afar off.” (Genesis 22:4)

Three days. Not one terrible afternoon. Three days of walking north with Isaac beside him and the firewood on the donkey. Three nights of camp. Imagine the conversations on that road. Isaac asking where they are going. Isaac asking what they are going to do when they get there. What would Abraham say? And imagine what is going through the father’s mind. He has already watched one son walk out of his camp into the desert. Now he is walking the other one toward a mountain. Three days is a long time to carry a command like that.

And then he sees it. The place. Far off.

> “And Abraham said unto his young men, Abide ye here with the ass; and I and the lad will go yonder and worship, and come again to you.” (Genesis 22:5)

Now read this verse like a detective, because it is the most important sentence in the chapter. *Abide ye here* means you two stay here with the donkey. *Yonder* means over there. And then listen. *I and the lad will go yonder and worship, and come again to you*.

We. Will come back. Both of us.

Think about that. If Abraham believed he was walking up that mountain to kill his son and walk down alone, how would he explain that to two servants waiting at the bottom? He does not say I will come back. He says we. Abraham is telling you what he believes before he takes another step. Somehow, both of us are coming down.

The New Testament tells us exactly what was in his head. Listen to Hebrews:

> “By faith Abraham, when he was tried, offered up Isaac: and he that had received the promises offered up his only begotten son, Of whom it was said, That in Isaac shall thy seed be called: Accounting that God was able to raise him up, even from the dead.” (Hebrews 11:17–19)

There is the answer to the problem. Abraham put the two things God said next to each other. The promise runs through Isaac. Offer Isaac. And he reasoned it out. If God says the promise runs through this boy, and God says to offer this boy, then God is going to have to raise this boy from the dead. He did not know how. He just knew who. The God who gave a son to a hundred year old man could give him back from an altar. That is not a man giving up. That is a man trusting God’s ability to keep His word even through death.

> “And Abraham took the wood of the burnt offering, and laid it upon Isaac his son; and he took the fire in his hand, and a knife; and they went both of them together.” (Genesis 22:6)

Now watch the loads. Abraham puts the wood on Isaac. The boy carries the firewood for his own offering up the mountain on his back. Abraham carries the fire, which means live coals in a pot, kept burning from the camp, and the knife. The old man carries the dangerous things. The son carries the heavy thing. And *they went both of them together*. Mark that phrase. It comes twice.

> “And Isaac spake unto Abraham his father, and said, My father: and he said, Here am I, my son. And he said, Behold the fire and the wood: but where is the lamb for a burnt offering?” (Genesis 22:7)

My father. Here am I, my son. Hear it. The same words Abraham said to God at the start of the chapter, he now says to his boy. Here I am. I am listening. And then Isaac asks the question that has been hanging over the whole trip. He is not a baby. He knows how this works. Fire, wood, knife. Where is the lamb?

Picture the father hearing that question from the son carrying the wood.

> “And Abraham said, My son, God will provide himself a lamb for a burnt offering: so they went both of them together.” (Genesis 22:8)

*God will provide himself a lamb*. In plain words, God will provide the lamb He needs for the offering. Abraham does not say, you are the lamb. He does not say, there is no lamb. He says God will provide. That is the sentence this whole mountain is about. And then, for the second time, *they went both of them together*. Father and son, side by side, up the hill. The repetition is the Bible slowing down so you feel it.

> “And they came to the place which God had told him of; and Abraham built an altar there, and laid the wood in order, and bound Isaac his son, and laid him on the altar upon the wood. And Abraham stretched forth his hand, and took the knife to slay his son.” (Genesis 22:9–10)

An **altar** is a raised platform for offerings, built out of stones in a place like this. Abraham builds it. He lays the wood on it in order. He binds Isaac, ties him, and lays him on the wood. And he picks up the knife. **Slay** means kill. Read it plainly. This is no longer packing or walking. His hand is out. The knife is in it. The next motion is the end of Isaac.

The Bible does not tell us one word Isaac said. It does not tell us whether Abraham’s hand shook. It just tells us how far it went. All the way to the knife.

> “And the angel of the LORD called unto him out of heaven, and said, Abraham, Abraham: and he said, Here am I. And he said, Lay not thine hand upon the lad, neither do thou any thing unto him: for now I know that thou fearest God, seeing thou hast not withheld thy son, thine only son from me.” (Genesis 22:11–12)

Abraham, Abraham. Twice, fast, urgent. Stop. And for the third time in the chapter, *Here am I*. He answered God when God said start. He answers God when God says stop. That is what obedience is. It is not just doing the hard thing. It is listening the whole way through, ready to stop the second God says stop.

The messenger is the angel of the LORD, the same kind of messenger who found Hagar at the well on Day 13 and called to her from heaven on Day 21. God keeps sending His word to this family at the exact moment it is needed. Do not put the knife down one second early, and do not let it fall one second late.

And hear why. *Now I know that thou fearest God*. **Fear** here does not mean Abraham was scared of God. It means reverence. God has the final say in his life, and Abraham just proved it with his hands. Proverbs says the fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom. This is what that looks like when it stops being a saying and becomes a man on a mountain with a knife.

Here is what the test was really about. Not whether Abraham loved Isaac. Of course he loved Isaac. The question was whether anything, even the son he waited twenty five years for, sat above God in his life. And the answer is no. Abraham did not withhold his son. He held Isaac with open hands. God was first. Everything else, even the promise, came second to the One who made it.

And notice, God says *thine only son* again. Abraham has two sons. But Isaac is the only son of the covenant, the only one born of Sarah, the only one the promise runs through. That is what God means. Ishmael is not forgotten. Isaac is just the one on the altar.

> “And Abraham lifted up his eyes, and looked, and behold behind him a ram caught in a thicket by his horns: and Abraham went and took the ram, and offered him up for a burnt offering in the stead of his son.” (Genesis 22:13)

He looks up, and there it is. A **ram**, a grown male sheep, caught by its horns in a **thicket**, a tangle of bushes, right behind him. Abraham goes and gets it and offers it *in the stead of his son*. In his son’s place. The ram dies. Isaac lives.

Remember the answer on the way up. God will provide himself a lamb. There is the provision. God did not cancel the offering. He supplied a substitute for it. Something died on that altar that day, and it was not Isaac. Let that picture settle into you, because the whole rest of the Bible is going to come back to it. A substitute, provided by God, dying in the place of the son.

> “And Abraham called the name of that place Jehovahjireh: as it is said to this day, In the mount of the LORD it shall be seen.” (Genesis 22:14)

Abraham names the place. **Jehovah** is the old English way of writing God’s personal name, the one your King James usually prints as LORD in capital letters. **Jireh** comes from the Hebrew word for seeing. When God sees a need, He sees to it. So **Jehovah Jireh** means the LORD will provide. The LORD sees, and the LORD provides. Abraham names a mountain after the thing he learned on it.

And people kept saying it. *In the mount of the LORD it shall be seen*. On God’s mountain, provision shows up. The thing you need will be seen to there. That saying outlived Abraham by a long time.

> “And the angel of the LORD called unto Abraham out of heaven the second time, And said, By myself have I sworn, saith the LORD, for because thou hast done this thing, and hast not withheld thy son, thine only son: That in blessing I will bless thee, and in multiplying I will multiply thy seed as the stars of the heaven, and as the sand which is upon the sea shore; and thy seed shall possess the gate of his enemies; And in thy seed shall all the nations of the earth be blessed; because thou hast obeyed my voice.” (Genesis 22:15–18)

God speaks a second time, and you might ask, why? He already promised all of this. Nations on Day 7. Dust on Day 10. Stars on Day 12. Covenant on Day 14. Why say it again?

Because of one new thing. *By myself have I sworn*. God swears an oath. Not a promise this time. An oath, sworn on His own name. The book of Hebrews explains it. When people swear, they swear by something bigger than themselves. God has nothing bigger than Himself to swear by, so He swears by Himself, so that the people waiting on the promise can be absolutely sure it cannot change. After this mountain, the promise is not just given. It is sworn.

And listen to the language. *In blessing I will bless thee*. *In multiplying I will multiply*. That is Hebrew for I will surely bless, I will greatly multiply. Stars and sand, more than anybody can count. *Possess the gate of his enemies* means his descendants will win. Remember the gate from Day 17, the place where a city’s business and power sat. To hold your enemy’s gate is to hold his city. And then the line from the very beginning. *In thy seed shall all the nations of the earth be blessed*. Day 7, repeated on a mountain, because Abraham obeyed.

Hear what God says at the end. *Because thou hast obeyed my voice*. God did not start loving Abraham on this mountain, and the promise did not start here. But God notices obedience. He names it. A man who did not hold back his son gets to hear God swear by Himself.

> “So Abraham returned unto his young men, and they rose up and went together to Beersheba; and Abraham dwelt at Beersheba.” (Genesis 22:19)

And they go home. Back to the servants, back to the donkey, back down to Beersheba, the well, the tree. The verse only names Abraham, but do not misread that. Isaac walked down that mountain alive. Abraham told the servants we will come again to you, and they did.

So step back and look at this mountain. God asked Abraham for the one thing he could not imagine giving. Abraham got up early, split the wood, walked three days, carried the fire, built the altar, and lifted the knife. He trusted that the God who promised a future through Isaac could keep that promise even through death. And at the last second, God stopped him and provided a ram in the boy’s place.

That is the obedience this whole book is named for. Not perfect obedience. We have watched Abraham lie and scheme and laugh. But when it came down to the one thing, he did not withhold it. He put God above the son he loved, and he found out that God provides.

You have an Isaac. Something you love so much that if God asked for it, you would want to argue. The question of this chapter is not whether you love it. The question is whether God sits above it. And the promise of this chapter is that when you open your hands, you find out He is Jehovah Jireh. He sees, and He provides.

Tomorrow the book turns a hard corner. Sarah, who laughed behind the tent door and laughed again in the nursery, comes to the end of her life. But today is not finished yet. Take the reflection questions first, then today’s action, and end your day in prayer.

## Reflection Questions

1. **What is your Isaac, the thing you love so much that God asking for it would feel impossible?** Abraham loved his son and still put God first. Name the thing, and ask honestly whether God sits above it.
2. **Abraham said we will come again before he knew how. Where do you need to trust God’s ability to keep His promise even when you cannot see the way?** He reasoned that God could raise the dead. Reason the same way about your situation.
3. **When has God provided a ram in the thicket, something you did not see until you looked up?** Write it down. Jehovah Jireh is a name you learn by remembering.

## Today's One Thing

**Climb the Mountain**

Abraham laid his Isaac on the altar with his own hands. Do the same with yours, on paper, today.

1. Write down your Isaac. The one person, dream, possession, or plan you would struggle most to hand over to God.
2. Under it, write this sentence and sign it: God, You are above this. If You ask for it, I will not withhold it.
3. Then write down one ram. One time God provided something you did not see coming. Keep both on the same page.

One Isaac named, one sentence signed, one ram remembered. Do it, and check it off.

## Today's Prayer

Father,

You are Jehovah Jireh. You see, and You provide. You provided a ram on a mountain for a man who did not hold back his son. And one day You provided Your own Son, and nobody stopped the knife. I do not take that lightly tonight.

You know my Isaac. You know the thing I love so much that I would argue with You over it. I am laying it on the altar right now, with my own hands, before You even ask.

**Here am I, Lord. When You say start, and when You say stop.**

Give me Abraham’s kind of trust. The kind that says we will come back before it knows how. The kind that reasons that You can raise the dead, because You have never once failed to keep Your word.

Make me quick to obey like him. Up early. Wood split. Walking before I understand. And make me quick to listen all the way through, so I never put the knife down early or let it fall late.

And when I look up, help me see the ram. You have already provided more than I have noticed. Open my eyes on the mountain.

**In Jesus’ name, amen.**
