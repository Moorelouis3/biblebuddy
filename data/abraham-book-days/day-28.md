Sixty years ago, an old man named Abraham heard God say, get out of your country, away from your family, to a land I will show you.

And he went. That was Day 7. It is the reason this book exists.

Today a young woman in that same old country is going to hear almost the same thing. Not from God in a vision.

From her own family, standing in front of a stranger with ten camels. Will you go with this man?

To a land you have never seen, to marry a man you have never met, with no plan to come home.

She has a few seconds to answer.

I want you to feel the weight of her choice, because the whole promise of this book is about to rest on it.

Abraham said she could refuse. Nobody is dragging her.

If Rebekah says no, the servant goes home empty, and the next generation of the promise has no mother.

**Today a girl says three words that change everything.**

# The Answer

> “And they called Rebekah, and said unto her, Wilt thou go with this man? And she said, I will go.” (Genesis 24:58)

Yesterday ended with the servant’s question hanging over the dinner table. Yes or no? Here is the answer:

> “Then Laban and Bethuel answered and said, The thing proceedeth from the LORD: we cannot speak unto thee bad or good. Behold, Rebekah is before thee, take her, and go, and let her be thy master’s son’s wife, as the LORD hath spoken.” (Genesis 24:50–51)

Laban the brother and Bethuel the father answer together. And listen to how they put it.

*The thing proceedeth from the LORD*. This came from God.

*We cannot speak unto thee bad or good* means it is not ours to argue with, one way or the other.

If God did this, who are we to say no? *Rebekah is before thee*. Here she is. Take her and go.

Now be careful with that. This family just recognized that the LORD was behind the servant’s story.

That does not mean they had thrown out every other god in the house.

Remember from Day 25, Abraham’s relatives back here served other gods, and this same Laban will have household idols years from now.

People can see God’s hand in something and still keep their idols on the shelf. But give them this.

They heard the evidence, they saw God in it, and they did not fight it.

## He Worshipped the LORD

> “And it came to pass, that, when Abraham’s servant heard their words, he worshipped the LORD, bowing himself to the earth.” (Genesis 24:52)

The servant hits the ground. Face down, worshiping. This is the third time in two days. He prayed at the well on Day 25.

He bowed his head and blessed God when he heard whose daughter she was on Day 26.

Now the family says yes and he is on the floor. Trace that line through this man.

Every time God moves, he stops and thanks Him, out loud, in front of whoever is watching.

He does not wait until the whole thing is finished to be grateful. He is grateful at every step.

> “And the servant brought forth jewels of silver, and jewels of gold, and raiment, and gave them to Rebekah: he gave also to her brother and to her mother precious things.” (Genesis 24:53)

Now the gifts come out, and these are on top of the nose ring and bracelets from the well.

Silver jewelry, gold jewelry, and **raiment**, which means clothing, fine garments, for Rebekah.

And precious things for her brother and her mother.

The Bible does not weigh these or describe them, so I will not either. Just understand what is happening.

In that world, a marriage was not only two people.

It was two families, and the gifts honored the family that was giving up a daughter and showed them that the man she was going to could provide.

Notice something about Abraham while the jewelry is going around.

He owns gold and silver and fine clothes, not just sheep and land.

The man who walked with God for sixty years is a very rich man.

Owning wealth did not make him unfaithful, and faith did not make him poor.

Do not turn that into a promise that following God makes everybody rich, because it does not.

But do not swallow the idea that a man of God has to be broke either. Abraham was neither.

He was blessed, and he used it to establish the next generation.

And think about where you have seen wealth move around women in this book before.

Pharaoh loaded Abraham with gifts when he took Sarah. Abimelech paid a thousand shekels when he gave her back.

Those were ugly scenes, the wrong kind of exchange. This is the right kind.

Abraham’s resources going out to build a marriage, not to clean up a lie.

## Hinder Me Not

> “And they did eat and drink, he and the men that were with him, and tarried all night; and they rose up in the morning, and he said, Send me away unto my master.” (Genesis 24:54)

Now, finally, he eats.

The errand is told, the answer is yes, the gifts are given, and the servant and his men sit down to the meal they turned away from last night.

They sleep. And the very next morning, the servant is up and ready to go. *Send me away unto my master*.

Let me take her and leave.

> “And her brother and her mother said, Let the damsel abide with us a few days, at the least ten; after that she shall go.” (Genesis 24:55)

And the family pushes back. Give us some time. A few days. Ten at least. Then she can go.

Put yourself in that tent. Last night a stranger showed up at dinner.

This morning he wants to ride off with your daughter, your sister, to a place five hundred miles away, and you will probably never see her again.

Of course they want ten days. Ten days to pack. Ten days to say goodbye. Ten days to get used to the idea.

That is not them being difficult. That is a family about to lose a girl they love.

> “And he said unto them, Hinder me not, seeing the LORD hath prospered my way; send me away that I may go to my master.” (Genesis 24:56)

*Hinder me not*. Do not hold me up. And hear his reason, because it is not impatience.

*Seeing the LORD hath prospered my way*.

God has made this whole trip work, from the first prayer at the well to your yes at the table.

The servant does not want to sit on a finished answer for ten days. When God has clearly moved, you move.

He has an old master waiting at home who may not have many years left, and he wants to get this done while God is so obviously in it.

## I Will Go

> “And they said, We will call the damsel, and enquire at her mouth. And they called Rebekah, and said unto her, Wilt thou go with this man? And she said, I will go.” (Genesis 24:57–58)

So they do the one thing that settles it. *Enquire at her mouth* means ask her directly. Let her answer for herself.

And they call Rebekah in and ask her the question. Will you go with this man?

Stop and feel this. Go back to Day 25. Abraham built her choice into the oath.

If the woman is not willing, you are released. Nobody was ever going to force Rebekah.

So the whole thing comes down to a young woman standing in front of her family and a stranger, being asked whether she will leave everything she has ever known, right now, today, to marry a man she has never laid eyes on, in a land she has never seen.

*I will go*.

Three words. No ten days. No conditions. No let me think about it.

The same spirit that got Abraham out of Haran sixty years ago just showed up in a girl at a well.

God said go to Abraham, and he went. Her family asked, will you go, and she went.

The promise is going to keep moving because a second person in this family was willing to leave home for something they could not see.

That is the center of today. Not the gold, not the negotiation.

A woman who said yes to a future she could not see because she could see that God was in it.

Nobody should read this as a rule that every marriage has to be arranged by a servant with camels. That was their world.

The thing to carry out of it is her answer. When God is plainly in a thing, I will go.

## The Mother of Thousands of Millions

> “And they sent away Rebekah their sister, and her nurse, and Abraham’s servant, and his men. And they blessed Rebekah, and said unto her, Thou art our sister, be thou the mother of thousands of millions, and let thy seed possess the gate of those which hate them.” (Genesis 24:59–60)

So they send her. And look who goes with her. *Her nurse*. Not a medical nurse.

This is the woman who nursed her as a baby and raised her, the caregiver who had been with her since she was born.

Later in Genesis we learn her name, Deborah, and she stays with Rebekah her whole life. That one detail makes this real.

Rebekah is not riding off alone with strangers. The woman who held her as a baby is going too.

Then the family blesses her. And listen to what they say, because you have heard it before.

*Be thou the mother of thousands of millions*. The Hebrew is thousands of ten thousands, a number too big to count.

And then, *let thy seed possess the gate of those which hate them*. Go back to Day 23.

On the mountain, God swore to Abraham, *thy seed shall possess the gate of his enemies*.

Rebekah’s family, five hundred miles away, who have never heard that oath, speak nearly the same words over her as she leaves.

The blessing God put on Abraham’s line is being spoken onto the woman who is about to carry it.

> “And Rebekah arose, and her damsels, and they rode upon the camels, and followed the man: and the servant took Rebekah, and went his way.” (Genesis 24:61)

And she goes. Her **damsels**, the young women who served her, go with her.

They climb onto the camels, and they ride out behind the servant. Picture the caravan leaving.

Ten camels, the men who came with them, a girl, her nurse, her attendants, and the gifts. Weeks of desert ahead.

And a family standing at the edge of town watching their daughter ride away.

Remember who she is. The same girl who ran up and down well steps to water ten camels for a stranger.

The same hands, the same feet, the same quick yes.

God pointed her out by her character, and her character is what got her on the camel.

## A Promise Over a Home

So step back and look at this day. A family said, this came from the LORD, and did not fight it.

A servant worshiped on the floor and would not wait ten days once God had moved.

And a young woman was asked a question that would cost her everything familiar, and she answered in three words.

I will go.

That is what obedience sounds like when it is not an old man anymore.

It is a girl with gold on her wrists, choosing a promise over a home. Abraham’s story is almost over.

Rebekah’s is just starting, and it starts the same way his did.

Tomorrow, the caravan comes over the last hill at evening, and there is a man standing in a field.

But today is not finished yet. Take the reflection questions first, then today’s action, and end your day in prayer.

## What To Watch For In Genesis 24

The family answers first. Listen to what they say they cannot do.

Then there is a tug of war over timing. Notice who wants to wait and who does not.

Then the question finally goes to Rebekah. Her answer is three words in English.

**As you read, notice:**

* 🙌 Who the family says the whole thing came from
* 🙇 What the servant does the moment he hears yes
* 📅 How many days the family asks for
* 🌟 The blessing they speak over Rebekah as she leaves

And think about this question: **What would it take for you to say I will go?**

## Reflection Questions

1. **Rebekah said I will go to a future she could not see, because she could see God was in it. What is God plainly in that you keep asking for ten more days on?** The servant said, hinder me not. Ask yourself what is hindering you.
2. **The servant worshiped at every step, not just at the end. When God moves in your life, do you thank Him right then, or wait until the whole thing is done?** Three times in two days he stopped and gave God credit. Count yours this week.
3. **Rebekah’s family blessed her as she left instead of holding her back. Who in your life needs you to bless their going, even though you would rather keep them?** They asked for ten days, then sent her with a blessing. Do the second thing.

## Today's One Thing

**Say I Will Go**

Rebekah answered in three words, with no ten day delay. Give God a three word answer today.

1. Write down the one thing God is plainly calling you toward that you have been stalling on with reasons that sound like at least ten days.
2. Under it, write the words I will go, and sign your name and today’s date.
3. Take the first step before the sun goes down. Make the call, send the application, pack the first box, have the conversation.

One calling named, three words signed, one step taken. Do it, and check it off.

## Today's Prayer

Father,

You called an old man out of this same country sixty years before, and he went.

Then You called a young woman out of it, and she went.

Thank You that You keep calling people out of the familiar and into the promise, and thank You that some of them say yes fast.

I want to be one of those. Not a ten days person. An I will go person.

**Here is my three word answer, Lord. I will go.**

You know what You have been calling me toward.

You know the reasons I have been giving for waiting, and how good they sound. Hinder me not.

When You have plainly prospered the way, get me on the camel.

Make me like the servant too. Quick to hit the floor and thank You at every step, not just at the end.

Let me worship on the way, not only when I arrive.

And for the people I love who are being called away from me, give me the grace to bless their going instead of holding them back.

Speak over them what You have spoken, and let me watch them ride away with my blessing in their ears.

**In Jesus’ name, amen.**
