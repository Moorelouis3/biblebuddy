Abraham is about a hundred and forty years old. Sarah has been in the cave at Machpelah for about three years.

Isaac is forty, grown, and still single.

And that is a problem bigger than one lonely son. God promised Abraham descendants like the stars.

Every one of those stars has to come through Isaac. And right now Isaac has no wife.

The promise has a bottleneck, and the old man knows it.

So Abraham does the last big thing he will do in this book.

He calls in the man who runs his entire household, makes him swear the most serious oath a man could swear, and sends him five hundred miles north to find a bride from the family he left behind fifty years ago.

Then the camera follows that servant, not Abraham, across the desert, until he is standing by a well outside a town at evening, with ten camels behind him and no idea what to do next.

And he does the thing every one of us should do when we have no idea what to do next. He prays.

**Today the future of the promise gets handed to a servant and a prayer.**

# The Servant’s Oath and Prayer

> “He shall send his angel before thee, and thou shalt take a wife unto my son from thence.” (Genesis 24:7)

Before we go to Abraham’s tent, we have to go back and pick up five verses we skipped.

Right after the mountain on Day 23, the Bible drops a family list:

> “And it came to pass after these things, that it was told Abraham, saying, Behold, Milcah, she hath also born children unto thy brother Nahor; Huz his firstborn, and Buz his brother, and Kemuel the father of Aram, And Chesed, and Hazo, and Pildash, and Jidlaph, and Bethuel. And Bethuel begat Rebekah: these eight Milcah did bear to Nahor, Abraham’s brother. And his concubine, whose name was Reumah, she bare also Tebah, and Gaham, and Thahash, and Maachah.” (Genesis 22:20–24)

Why is this here? Because of one name. Rebekah. Everything else in the list is there to tell you where she comes from.

So let me untangle the family for you, because it is easy to get crossed up. Back on Day 6, Terah had three sons.

Abraham, Nahor, and Haran. Haran died young in Ur, leaving a son, Lot, and a daughter, Milcah.

Nahor married Milcah, his own niece.

So Nahor and Milcah are Abraham’s brother and Abraham’s niece, married to each other.

They stayed behind in the old country when Abraham left.

Now word reaches Abraham that Nahor and Milcah have eight sons. The last one on the list is Bethuel.

And **begat** means fathered. Bethuel is Rebekah’s father.

So Rebekah is Nahor’s granddaughter, which makes her Abraham’s great niece.

Hold on to that, because in a few verses Abraham is going to say, go to my family, and this is the family.

One more thing in the list. Nahor also has four sons by a **concubine** named Reumah.

A concubine was a recognized partner in a household, a real relationship with real children, but with a lower standing than the main wife.

It was not a secret affair. Think of Sarah and Hagar.

Hagar was given to Abraham as a wife, but she never stopped being Sarah’s servant, and the household treated her children differently.

That is the kind of arrangement this is.

Twelve children in Nahor’s house, eight through the wife, four through the concubine.

Now you know where Rebekah comes from. Let’s go to Abraham.

## Thy Hand Under My Thigh

> “And Abraham was old, and well stricken in age: and the LORD had blessed Abraham in all things.” (Genesis 24:1)

**Well stricken in age** is not an injury. It means far along in years. The Hebrew says he had come into days.

He is about a hundred and forty.

Sarah died at a hundred and twenty seven, when he was a hundred and thirty seven, and Isaac is forty when he marries, which puts us about three years after the cave.

And the Bible sums up his whole life in one line. *The LORD had blessed Abraham in all things*.

Land, herds, silver, gold, a household, a son, a promise sworn on God’s own name. All things.

> “And Abraham said unto his eldest servant of his house, that ruled over all that he had, Put, I pray thee, thy hand under my thigh: And I will make thee swear by the LORD, the God of heaven, and the God of the earth, that thou shalt not take a wife unto my son of the daughters of the Canaanites, among whom I dwell: But thou shalt go unto my country, and to my kindred, and take a wife unto my son Isaac.” (Genesis 24:2–4)

The **eldest servant**, the one who *ruled over all that he had*, is the man who manages everything Abraham owns.

The chief of the household. Not a young errand boy. The most trusted man in the camp.

Back on Day 12, before Isaac was born, Abraham told God that a servant named Eliezer of Damascus was going to inherit everything.

Many people think this is the same man, Eliezer, now old, now sent to find a wife for the son who replaced him as heir.

The chapter never says his name, so hold that loosely.

What it does say is that this man held Abraham’s whole world in his hands.

Then the strangest instruction in the chapter. *Put thy hand under my thigh*.

Abraham has the servant place his hand under his upper thigh, right near where life comes from, and swear.

People have long understood that gesture as swearing on the family line itself, on the descendants God promised, and maybe on the covenant sign of circumcision carried on that part of the body.

It was the most serious oath a man in that household could take.

Abraham’s grandson Jacob does the exact same thing years later, making Joseph put a hand under his thigh and swear to carry his body out of Egypt and bury him with his fathers.

This is how this family swore about the things that mattered most.

And here is what the servant has to swear. Not a Canaanite.

Go to my country, to my own family, and get a wife for Isaac there.

Now, be honest about the obvious question, because I asked it too. Why?

Abraham’s family back in the old country were not exactly a church.

The book of Joshua says plainly that Abraham’s fathers served other gods.

Later in Genesis, Rebekah’s own brother Laban is going to have household idols.

So it is not that the Canaanites were pagans and Abraham’s relatives were pure. Both had other gods in the house.

The difference is what a marriage does.

In that world, when you married into a people, you joined their society, their land, their ways, and their gods.

A Canaanite wife for Isaac would have pulled the promised family straight into the Canaanite world, and the whole point of this family was to be set apart inside that land, not swallowed by it.

Marrying inside the family kept the line, the identity, and the inheritance together.

And look at the one rule Abraham is about to add. The wife comes here. Isaac does not go there. That is the real key.

## Send His Angel Before Thee

> “And the servant said unto him, Peradventure the woman will not be willing to follow me unto this land: must I needs bring thy son again unto the land from whence thou camest? And Abraham said unto him, Beware thou that thou bring not my son thither again.” (Genesis 24:5–6)

Good servants ask good questions before they swear. **Peradventure** means what if. What if the woman says no?

Should I take Isaac up there to her? *Thither* means there. And notice *again* does not mean Isaac had ever been there.

It means back to the place the family came from.

Abraham’s answer is sharp. *Beware*. Watch yourself. Do not take my son back there. Not under any circumstances.

> “The LORD God of heaven, which took me from my father’s house, and from the land of my kindred, and which spake unto me, and that sware unto me, saying, Unto thy seed will I give this land; he shall send his angel before thee, and thou shalt take a wife unto my son from thence. And if the woman will not be willing to follow thee, then thou shalt be clear from this my oath: only bring not my son thither again.” (Genesis 24:7–8)

Listen to Abraham explain why. God took me out of that house and that land. God spoke to me. God swore to me.

*Unto thy seed will I give this land*. This land. Not that one. The whole promise is tied to Isaac living here.

Sending Isaac back north would be walking out of the promise.

Abraham left that country on Day 7 and never looked back, and he is not about to let his son undo it for a wife.

Then the line the whole day turns on. *He shall send his angel before thee*. God will send His messenger ahead of you.

Abraham is a hundred and forty years old, sending a servant across five hundred miles of desert to find one specific woman in a town he has not seen in sixty years.

And he is not nervous, because he knows who is going ahead of the servant.

Think of the wise men following the star to find Jesus. God guides people on journeys He sends them on.

The servant is not going to see an angel walking the road. But he is going to find out that somebody got there first.

And then a mercy inside the oath. If she will not come, *thou shalt be clear from this my oath*. You are released.

Hear what that means. The woman gets a choice.

The servant is not allowed to drag her, trick her, or force her, and he is not allowed to solve the problem by moving Isaac.

If she says no, the servant comes home with a clear conscience.

Abraham trusts God to lead, and he trusts the woman to decide.

## Unto the City of Nahor

> “And the servant put his hand under the thigh of Abraham his master, and sware to him concerning that matter.” (Genesis 24:9)

Feel the weight of that moment.

The future of everything God promised, a nation, a blessing for the whole world, is riding on whether this servant finds a wife for Isaac.

He swears by the LORD, with his hand in the place of the family line, to an old man who will not live to see most of it.

This is not a casual favor.

It is the most important errand anyone in this book has ever been sent on, and he takes it seriously enough to ask hard questions first.

> “And the servant took ten camels of the camels of his master, and departed; for all the goods of his master were in his hand: and he arose, and went to Mesopotamia, unto the city of Nahor.” (Genesis 24:10)

Ten camels.

That is a real caravan, with men to drive it, supplies for weeks, gifts for a bride’s family, and room on the way back for a bride and her people.

*All the goods of his master were in his hand* means he had Abraham’s resources at his disposal.

He was not carrying everything Abraham owned.

He was carrying Abraham’s authority and enough of his wealth to represent him.

And look where he goes. **Mesopotamia** means the land between the rivers, the Tigris and the Euphrates.

Specifically, the Hebrew points to the northern part, up around Haran, where Abraham’s family settled after leaving Ur on Day 6.

Not Ur itself, which was far to the south. The city of Nahor, his brother’s town.

From Hebron to there is about five hundred miles. Weeks on the road with ten camels. This was not a quick trip.

> “And he made his camels to kneel down without the city by a well of water at the time of the evening, even the time that women go out to draw water.” (Genesis 24:11)

He arrives. *Without the city* means outside it. He kneels the camels by the well, and the Bible tells you exactly when.

Evening, when the women come out to draw water. Think about that daily chore. No taps, no pipes.

Every drop of water a household used had to be carried from the well in a jar, and in that town it was the women who did it, in the cool of the evening.

So the servant parks himself at the one spot in town where every young woman is going to walk past before dark.

That is smart. And then he does something smarter.

## Send Me Good Speed

> “And he said, O LORD God of my master Abraham, I pray thee, send me good speed this day, and shew kindness unto my master Abraham.” (Genesis 24:12)

He prays. Hear how he addresses God. *LORD God of my master Abraham*.

He is praying to the LORD Himself, the same God, but he comes in through Abraham.

Your relationship with my master, Your promises to him, that is what I am standing on. He is not praying to Abraham.

He is reminding God whose errand this is.

*Send me good speed* does not mean make me go fast. It means give me success today. Make this work.

And *shew kindness unto my master Abraham*.

That word kindness is the Hebrew word for loyal love, the faithful love God shows to the people He has made promises to.

The servant is asking God to be faithful to Abraham by helping him find this girl.

> “Behold, I stand here by the well of water; and the daughters of the men of the city come out to draw water: And let it come to pass, that the damsel to whom I shall say, Let down thy pitcher, I pray thee, that I may drink; and she shall say, Drink, and I will give thy camels drink also: let the same be she that thou hast appointed for thy servant Isaac; and thereby shall I know that thou hast shewed kindness unto my master.” (Genesis 24:13–14)

Now look at what he asks for, because it is not random. A **damsel** is a young woman. A **pitcher** is a water jar.

Here is my sign, God. I will ask a girl for a drink.

If she gives me one and then says, let me water your camels too, she is the one.

Think about what that sign actually tests. Ten camels.

A camel that has crossed the desert can drink an enormous amount of water, and every bit of it has to come up out of the well in a jar and go into the trough, one trip at a time.

A girl who offers to do that for a stranger is not just being polite. She is generous. She works hard.

She sees a need and takes it on without being asked.

She has the character of a woman who could stand next to the son of the promise.

The servant is not asking God for the prettiest girl at the well.

He is asking God to show him a woman’s heart through her hands.

**Appointed** means chosen. Set apart by God for this. The servant believes God has already picked the woman.

He is asking God to point her out.

## Trust and Prayer

And that is where today stops.

An old servant, far from home, standing by a well at sunset with ten thirsty camels behind him and the whole future of the promise in his hands.

He has sworn the oath. He has made the trip. He has done everything a man can do.

And now he has asked God to do what only God can do.

That is the picture to take with you. When Abraham could not be there, he did not send a plan.

He sent a man and trusted God to send an angel ahead of him.

And when the man got there, he did not start interviewing girls.

He stood still and prayed, specifically, for God to show him.

Trust and prayer, in that order, is how this family finds its way when God has not spoken out loud.

Tomorrow, before the servant even finishes praying, a girl walks up to the well with a jar on her shoulder.

But today is not finished yet. Take the reflection questions first, then today’s action, and end your day in prayer.

## What To Watch For In Genesis 24

Start with the five verses at the end of Genesis 22.

They are a family list, and one name in it is the whole reason we are reading them. Find the girl.

Then read Genesis 24:1–14. Watch what Abraham will and will not allow his servant to do.

The servant’s prayer at the well is very specific. Notice exactly what he asks God for.

**As you read, notice:**

* ✋ Where Abraham puts the servant’s hand, and why
* 🚫 The one thing Abraham says never to do with Isaac
* 👼 What Abraham says will go ahead of the servant
* 💧 The sign the servant asks for

And think about this question: **How do you make a decision this big when God has not spoken out loud?**

## Reflection Questions

1. **Abraham refused to let Isaac go back to the old country, even for a wife. What is the old country you keep thinking about going back to?** God took Abraham out of there. Beware of walking your future back into what He called you out of.
2. **The servant asked God for a sign that showed a woman’s character, not her looks. What are you actually looking for in the people you let close?** Ask God to show you hearts through hands.
3. **When you face a decision God has not spoken out loud about, do you start interviewing, or do you stand still and pray first?** The servant did all he could, then prayed before he moved. Check your order.

## Today's One Thing

**Pray Before You Move**

The servant stood by the well and asked God for something specific before he spoke to anyone.

Do that with one decision today.

1. Write down one decision you are facing where God has not spoken out loud. A job, a relationship, a move, a purchase.
2. Before you take one more step on it, pray the servant’s way. Name God as the God of His promises to you, and ask Him for one specific thing that would show you His leading.
3. Write down what you asked for and the date, so you can look back and see what He did.

One decision, one specific prayer, one date written. Do it, and check it off.

## Today's Prayer

Father,

You are the LORD God of my fathers. You made promises to people before me, and You keep them in my life.

Like the servant at the well, I am standing on what You said to someone else and trusting it for me.

Send Your angel before me. I have errands in front of me that I do not know how to finish.

Go ahead of me into every room, every town, every conversation, and get there first.

**Send me good speed this day, and show kindness to the people I am carrying.**

Keep me out of the old country.

Do not let me walk my future back into the places You called me out of, even for something I want badly.

And teach me to pray the way this servant prayed. Specific. Standing still. Before I move.

Show me the hearts of people through their hands, and give me the patience to wait by the well until You point.

Thank You that You had already chosen before he ever asked. You have gone ahead of me too.

**In Jesus’ name, amen.**
