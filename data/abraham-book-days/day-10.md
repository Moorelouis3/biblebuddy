They made it out of Egypt. Alive. Together. Fed. Richer than they have ever been.

If you stopped reading yesterday and looked at the outcome sheet, you might have called the whole trip a win.

Today Genesis starts collecting the bill.

Because caravans do not just carry sheep and silver. They carry consequences.

Some of what walked up out of Egypt with this family is about to split it in half, and some of it planted seeds nobody will see sprout for years.

But today is not only about consequences. It is also about the way back.

Because the first thing Abram does on the other side of his worst chapter might be the wisest thing we have watched him do yet.

He goes back to the last place he got it right.

**Today we find out what crossed the border.**

# What Came Up Out of Egypt

> “Unto the place of the altar, which he had made there at the first: and there Abram called on the name of the LORD.” (Genesis 13:4)

The chapter opens with the caravan climbing home:

> “And Abram went up out of Egypt, he, and his wife, and all that he had, and Lot with him, into the south. And Abram was very rich in cattle, in silver, and in gold.” (Genesis 13:1–2)

Everybody made it. Abram, Sarai, Lot, the whole household. And notice the new detail the text adds. Abram was very rich.

Cattle, silver, gold. Some of that he brought from Haran.

But a good piece of it has Pharaoh’s fingerprints on it, gifts given for her sake, back when Egypt thought Sarai was an available woman.

Keep your eye on that wealth. It looks like a blessing. It is about to act like something else.

## The Place of the Altar

But first, watch what Abram does, because this is the part to copy:

> “And he went on his journeys from the south even to Bethel, unto the place where his tent had been at the beginning, between Bethel and Hai; unto the place of the altar, which he had made there at the first: and there Abram called on the name of the LORD.” (Genesis 13:3–4)

Read where he goes. Not somewhere new. Somewhere old.

He retraces his own steps all the way back to Bethel, to the exact spot where his tent used to sit, to the altar he built back when his obedience was fresh.

The place of the altar, which he had made there at the first.

And there, the text says, Abram called on the name of the LORD.

Now think about what was missing from the entire Egypt story. No altar. No calling on the LORD.

Not one recorded word to God between the famine and the palace. The whole disaster played out inside that silence.

And the first thing Abram does on the other side of it is walk back to the last place he ever heard from heaven, and start talking again.

That is what the way back looks like. After a fall, you do not need a new secret. You need your old altar.

Go back to the last place you got it right, and call on His name.

And notice what God does not do here. There is no lecture. No punishment scene. No probation period.

The altar was still standing right where Abram left it. It always is.

## Their Substance Was Great

But Egypt was not done with this family, because look what flares up next:

> “And the land was not able to bear them, that they might dwell together: for their substance was great, so that they could not dwell together. And there was a strife between the herdmen of Abram’s cattle and the herdmen of Lot’s cattle: and the Canaanite and the Perizzite dwelled then in the land.” (Genesis 13:6–7)

**Strife** means fighting. Quarreling. Two sets of herdsmen, one family, arguing over grass and wells.

And what is the fight actually over? Look at the verse. Their substance was great.

The problem is not that they have too little. The problem is that they have too much.

The herds got so big the land could not hold them side by side.

Now trace where a chunk of those herds came from. Sheep and oxen from Pharaoh’s hand.

The wealth that walked up out of Egypt is the very thing tearing the family apart.

What looked like the plan working is now the reason uncle and nephew cannot live on the same hillside.

That is the first consequence to cross the border:

* 🐑 Herds too big for the land to carry them together
* 😠 A family fight in front of watching neighbors
* 👀 Eyes that now knew exactly what Egypt looked like
* 📖 And maybe more that we will meet later in this book

And do not skip the last line of that verse. The Canaanite and the Perizzite dwelled then in the land.

The neighbors are watching.

The one family on earth carrying God’s promise is squabbling over pasture in front of the very peoples God said they would one day bless.

Some things have not changed in four thousand years. The world still watches how the people of God fight.

## Let There Be No Strife

Now watch Abram, because the man at Bethel is not the man from Egypt:

> “And Abram said unto Lot, Let there be no strife, I pray thee, between me and thee, and between my herdmen and thy herdmen; for we be brethren. Is not the whole land before thee? separate thyself, I pray thee, from me: if thou wilt take the left hand, then I will go to the right; or if thou depart to the right hand, then I will go to the left.” (Genesis 13:8–9)

Measure the change. In Egypt, fear made Abram protect himself first, whatever it cost the person next to him.

At Bethel, fresh from the altar, the same man hands his younger nephew first pick of the entire land and volunteers to take whatever is left.

He is the elder. He is the one holding God’s promise. By every custom of that world, the choice was his.

He gives it away.

That is what a man looks like after he has been back to the altar. Faith does not need first pick.

Faith can let go of its rights, because faith knows Who is really holding the land.

## Like the Land of Egypt

Then Lot chooses:

> “And Lot lifted up his eyes, and beheld all the plain of Jordan, that it was well watered every where, before the LORD destroyed Sodom and Gomorrah, even as the garden of the LORD, like the land of Egypt, as thou comest unto Zoar. Then Lot chose him all the plain of Jordan.” (Genesis 13:10–11)

Slow down and catch the comparison, because it is the key to this whole day.

The plain looked like the garden of the LORD. And it looked like the land of Egypt.

Where did Lot learn what Egypt looked like? He walked through it. He was in that caravan too.

Lot stood in the greenest, richest, best fed country in the world, and it trained his eyes.

Now, standing on a ridge in Canaan, he sees a well watered plain and his heart says, that looks like Egypt.

And he goes toward it.

That is the quietest consequence of the whole trip, and maybe the biggest. Egypt did not just load their saddlebags.

It shaped their appetites. Some consequences do not follow you home carrying a sign.

They follow you home inside your own eyes, in what you now find beautiful, in what you now cannot stop wanting.

The family left Egypt. Egypt did not entirely leave the family.

And notice the two warnings the text drops around Lot’s beautiful choice.

First, the storyteller leans in: before the LORD destroyed Sodom and Gomorrah.

The reader gets told what Lot cannot see, that this postcard of a valley is standing on a trapdoor. Then:

> “Abram dwelled in the land of Canaan, and Lot dwelled in the cities of the plain, and pitched his tent toward Sodom. But the men of Sodom were wicked and sinners before the LORD exceedingly.” (Genesis 13:12–13)

Lot chose by sight. And choosing by sight walked him one tent peg at a time toward Sodom.

Do not make him a monster for it. He picked what looked best, and most of us would have done the same.

That is exactly what should bother us. Eyes that Egypt has trained will always pick the plain.

They cannot see the trapdoor from the ridge.

## Lift Up Now Thine Eyes

And then, the moment Lot’s dust settles on the eastern road, God speaks:

> “And the LORD said unto Abram, after that Lot was separated from him, Lift up now thine eyes, and look from the place where thou art northward, and southward, and eastward, and westward: for all the land which thou seest, to thee will I give it, and to thy seed for ever. And I will make thy seed as the dust of the earth.” (Genesis 13:14–16)

Catch the echo, because Moses put it there on purpose.

A few verses ago, Lot lifted up his eyes and grabbed the best land he could see.

Now God says to Abram, *lift up now thine eyes*. Same motion, opposite direction.

Lot lifted his eyes to take. Abram lifts his eyes to receive. One man saw what he could get.

The other man was shown what he would be given.

And look at the size of the gift. Every direction.

North, south, east, west, including the beautiful plain Lot just picked. For ever.

And a family line like the dust of the earth, promised to a man whose wife still cannot have children.

God did not shrink the promise after Egypt. He widened it. Abram’s worst chapter did not cost him one square foot.

Then God says something wonderfully physical: *Arise, walk through the land in the length of it and in the breadth of it; for I will give it unto thee* (Genesis 13:17).

Get up and put footprints on the promise. Do not just look at it from the ridge. Walk it.

> “Then Abram removed his tent, and came and dwelt in the plain of Mamre, which is in Hebron, and built there an altar unto the LORD.” (Genesis 13:18)

Look at how the chapter is framed.

It opens with Abram returning to an old altar, and it closes with him building a new one.

Egypt sits in the middle of this story like a hole in the trail, the one stretch of road with no altars on it.

The detour is over now. The man is marking the ground with worship again.

## The Way Home

So what crossed the border? Wealth that split a family. Eyes that Egypt had trained.

Consequences that outlived the trip that caused them. But something else crossed the border too.

A man who knew the way back to his altar. And a God whose promise came through Egypt without a scratch on it.

Your Egypt has consequences. This book will not lie to you about that. But the way home is not complicated.

Go back to the last place you got it right. Call on His name.

And listen, because God’s answer to a returned man was not a smaller promise. It was a bigger one.

Tomorrow, war comes to the plain, and Lot finds out what his beautiful choice costs. But today is not finished yet.

Take the reflection questions first, then today’s action, and end your day in prayer.

## What To Watch For In Genesis 13

Watch the first thing Abram does when he gets back to Canaan, and where he goes to do it.

A fight breaks out in this passage. Pay attention to what the fight is actually over, and where that came from.

When Lot makes his choice, notice what the text compares the land to.

**As you read, notice:**

* 🙏 Where Abram goes first, and what he does there
* 🐄 What the herdsmen are fighting over
* 🌿 What the plain of Jordan reminds Lot of
* 👀 What God tells Abram after Lot leaves

And think about this question: **What did they bring back from Egypt?**

## Reflection Questions

1. **What did you bring back from your Egypt?** Some consequences ride home quietly: habits, appetites, things your eyes learned to want. Name what crossed the border with you.
2. **Where is your Bethel, the last place you clearly heard God?** Abram went backward before he could go forward. The old altar was still standing. Yours is too.
3. **Are you choosing by sight like Lot, or receiving by promise like Abram?** Lot lifted his eyes for himself and picked the plain. Abram lifted his eyes at God’s word and received everything. Same eyes, different masters.

## Today's One Thing

**Go Back to Bethel**

After Egypt, Abram went back to the last altar and called on the name of the LORD. Today, make that walk yourself.

1. Write down your Bethel: the last time or place you clearly heard from God. A habit, a decision, a spot, a season.
2. Go back to it today. Physically if you can. If not, do again the thing you were doing back then.
3. Call on His name there, out loud, the way Abram did.

One place remembered, one walk back, one prayer out loud. Do it, and check it off.

## Today's Prayer

Father,

Thank You that the altar was still standing.

After the silence, after Egypt, after everything Abram did and failed to do, the way back to You was still open.

It is still open for me too.

So here I am, walking back to my Bethel. Back to the last place I clearly heard You.

I am calling on Your name again, out loud, like a man who remembers the way home.

**Thank You that You meet returners without a lecture.**

And help me unpack my caravan honestly. I brought things back from my Egypt.

Some of it looks like blessing and acts like trouble, and some of it lives in my own eyes, in what I have learned to want.

Take back what was never supposed to come home.

Retrain my eyes, Father. Lot saw the plain and thought of Egypt, and it walked him toward Sodom one tent peg at a time.

When I lift up my eyes, let it be because You said to, and let me see what You are giving instead of what I could grab.

And thank You that my worst chapter did not shrink Your promise.

You met a returned man with a bigger word, not a smaller one. Walk me through everything You still intend to give.

**In Jesus’ name, amen.**
