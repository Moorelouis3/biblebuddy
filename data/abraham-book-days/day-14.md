Thirteen years.

That is how much time passes between the last verse of yesterday’s chapter and the first verse of today’s.

Thirteen years between one recorded word from God and the next.

Thirteen years of Abram raising the boy born out of the shortcut, teaching him to ride and herd and fight, watching him grow from a baby into a teenager.

Thirteen years of a man quietly deciding that this must be how God meant to keep His promise.

Abram is ninety nine years old now. Sarai is pushing ninety.

He has lived with God’s promise for almost a quarter of a century, and he has a son in his tent who looks a lot like an answer.

And then God speaks again.

And what He says is going to change Abram’s name, Sarai’s name, Abram’s body, and everything Abram thought he understood about how the promise was going to work.

Because you can wait on God, and you can try to help God, and you can even raise a son for thirteen years believing he is the plan.

But you cannot force God’s plan into the shape you picked. Today, God takes the pen back.

**Today God sets the clock.**

# The Almighty Sets the Clock

> “But my covenant will I establish with Isaac, which Sarah shall bear unto thee at this set time in the next year.” (Genesis 17:21)

Yesterday ended with Abram at eighty six, holding a newborn. Look where today begins:

> “And when Abram was ninety years old and nine, the LORD appeared to Abram, and said unto him, I am the Almighty God; walk before me, and be thou perfect.” (Genesis 17:1)

Ninety nine. Do the math. Thirteen years since Ishmael was born.

Twenty four years since Abram left Haran at seventy five with a promise and no map.

For twenty four years he has been the man God promised a nation to, and for thirteen of them he has had one son running around his camp.

Whatever he believed on the night of the stars, it is not hard to imagine that somewhere in those thirteen years, Ishmael quietly became the plan in Abram’s mind.

Of course he did. The boy was right there.

And now God shows up and introduces Himself with a title we have not heard yet. *I am the Almighty God*.

In Hebrew, **El Shaddai**. God Almighty. The God for whom nothing is too hard.

Abram lives in a world crawling with gods, little gods of rain and crops and wombs, each one in charge of one small thing.

The God speaking to him is announcing that He is in charge of everything.

Hold on to that introduction, because in a few verses God is going to promise something so impossible that only an Almighty God could pull it off.

Then the instruction. *Walk before me, and be thou perfect*. Do not let that word scare you.

**Perfect** here does not mean never sin again. The idea behind it is whole. Undivided. Wholehearted.

Live your life in front of Me with nothing held back. Not a demand for a flawless man. A call for a man who is all in.

> “And Abram fell on his face: and God talked with him, saying, As for me, behold, my covenant is with thee, and thou shalt be a father of many nations.” (Genesis 17:3–4)

Abram hits the ground. Face down in the dirt, the way people in his world showed total reverence to a king.

And God starts laying out the covenant again, but this time He gets specific in ways He never has before.

Understand what is happening here. God is not inventing a new promise because the old one did not work out.

He has been saying land and descendants and nations since Day 7. He sealed it with fire between the pieces in Day 12.

Today He is sharpening it. Names. A sign. The exact mother. The son’s name. And a date.

The plan did not change because Abram and Sarai took a shortcut.

God is simply making the plan impossible to misunderstand.

## Father of Many Nations

And the first thing He sharpens is the man’s name:

> “Neither shall thy name any more be called Abram, but thy name shall be Abraham; for a father of many nations have I made thee.” (Genesis 17:5)

Remember Day 7. We said hold on to this man’s name, because God was not finished with it. Here is the payoff.

**Abram** meant exalted father, the name that had been an ache in his life for decades, a childless man called Father every time somebody spoke to him.

Now God gives him **Abraham**, and tells us what it means: father of many nations.

In that world, a name was not a label. It was a statement about who you were and where your story was going.

So God is doing something bold.

He is renaming a ninety nine year old man with one son Father of Many Nations, before a single one of those nations exists.

From now on, every time anybody calls his name across the camp, they will be speaking God’s promise out loud.

The wound became the prophecy. And from here on, this book calls him Abraham.

Then God lays out what this covenant actually contains:

> “And I will make thee exceeding fruitful, and I will make nations of thee, and kings shall come out of thee. And I will establish my covenant between me and thee and thy seed after thee in their generations for an everlasting covenant, to be a God unto thee, and to thy seed after thee. And I will give unto thee, and to thy seed after thee, the land wherein thou art a stranger, all the land of Canaan, for an everlasting possession; and I will be their God.” (Genesis 17:6–8)

Lay it out plainly:

* 🌟 Abraham will have more descendants than he can imagine
* 👑 Whole nations will come from him, and kings
* 🌳 The covenant passes down through his family line, generation after generation, forever
* 🌄 Canaan, the land he is camping in as a foreigner, becomes his family’s permanent possession
* 💛 And the heart of it: I will be their God

That last line is not God saying, I exist. It is relationship language. I am claiming this family as Mine.

They belong to Me, and I belong to them. That is what a covenant is at its core, a God who ties Himself to a people.

Now, a modern reader might hear all this and think, how does any of that help Abraham?

Most of it happens after he is dead. But you have to step into his world to feel it.

There, a man did not think of his life as just the years between his birth and his funeral. His life was his line.

His children, his grandchildren, the name they carried, the land they held.

A promise to Abraham’s descendants was not a consolation prize. It was the deepest hope a man of his time could have.

God was telling him, your story does not end when you do. It is just getting started.

## A Token of the Covenant

Then God attaches a sign to the covenant:

> “Every man child among you shall be circumcised. And ye shall circumcise the flesh of your foreskin; and it shall be a token of the covenant betwixt me and you.” (Genesis 17:10–11)

We met the word **token** on Day 1. It means a sign. Noah’s covenant got a rainbow hung in the clouds.

Abraham’s covenant gets a sign carried on the body.

**Circumcision** is the cutting away of the foreskin, the fold of skin at the tip of the male organ.

Other peoples in the ancient world practiced circumcision too, so Abraham would have known what the word meant.

What is new is the meaning God gives it.

For this family, it becomes the permanent, physical mark of belonging to the covenant.

God says the covenant will be *in your flesh*. Not written on a scroll somewhere.

Carried on the body of every man in the household, every day of his life.

> “And he that is eight days old shall be circumcised among you, every man child in your generations, he that is born in the house, or bought with money of any stranger, which is not of thy seed.” (Genesis 17:12)

Two details. First, the timing. Every baby boy on the eighth day of his life.

God gives no explanation for the number, and the point is simply obedience to the timing He set. Second, the reach.

Not just Abraham’s sons. Every male in the household, including the servants born there and the ones bought with money.

Yes, bought. The text does not hide that Abraham’s camp included people who had been purchased.

And God brings every one of them inside the sign of the covenant.

> “And the uncircumcised man child whose flesh of his foreskin is not circumcised, that soul shall be cut off from his people; he hath broken my covenant.” (Genesis 17:14)

This is serious language.

**Cut off from his people** means removed from the covenant community, no longer counted among God’s people.

It is not aimed at an eight day old baby making a decision.

It is aimed at any man or household in Abraham’s line that refuses the sign God required.

In plain words: this sign is not optional. If you want to be in this covenant, you carry the mark.

## A Mother of Nations

And now God turns to the person this whole promise has been missing:

> “And God said unto Abraham, As for Sarai thy wife, thou shalt not call her name Sarai, but Sarah shall her name be. And I will bless her, and give thee a son also of her: yea, I will bless her, and she shall be a mother of nations; kings of people shall be of her.” (Genesis 17:15–16)

Sarai becomes Sarah. The two names are close cousins, both carrying the idea of a princess, a noblewoman.

The big change is not the spelling. It is what God says about her.

For twenty four years the promise has been about Abram’s seed, and after Genesis 16, you could be forgiven for thinking any son of Abraham would do.

God ends that right here. *I will give thee a son also of her*. Of her. Of Sarah. The ninety year old. The barren one.

The covenant son is not coming through a servant or a shortcut.

He is coming through the wife everybody had quietly counted out.

And look at the blessing God puts on her. Not just a baby. A mother of nations. Kings of people.

The woman whose closed womb has been the obstacle for twenty four years is about to become the mother of the whole promise.

From here on, this book calls her Sarah.

## O That Ishmael Might Live

Then one of the most human moments in Genesis:

> “Then Abraham fell upon his face, and laughed, and said in his heart, Shall a child be born unto him that is an hundred years old? and shall Sarah, that is ninety years old, bear? And Abraham said unto God, O that Ishmael might live before thee!” (Genesis 17:17–18)

He laughs. Face down on the ground before the Almighty God, Abraham laughs. And be honest, you would too.

A hundred year old man and a ninety year old woman having a baby. He heard the words perfectly.

The problem is that they sound biologically ridiculous. And now the introduction from verse 1 lands with full force.

I am the Almighty God. The God who just said that is the only kind of God who could follow it with this.

Hold on to Abraham’s laugh, because Sarah is going to laugh in the next chapter too. Two laughs, one impossible promise.

We will look hard at both of them tomorrow.

But then listen to the very next thing out of Abraham’s mouth, because it is the most revealing sentence in the chapter.

*O that Ishmael might live before thee!* In plain words: what about Ishmael? Can he be the one?

Let him be the son who carries this.

Do not read past that too fast. That is a father talking. Ishmael is thirteen years old.

Abraham has raised him from a baby. He taught him everything he knows.

For thirteen years, this boy has been the answer to the oldest prayer Abraham ever prayed.

And now God is saying, the son I promised is coming another way. Imagine hearing that about your own son.

Of course Abraham’s first thought is Ishmael. He loves him.

And God’s answer is tender:

> “And as for Ishmael, I have heard thee: Behold, I have blessed him, and will make him fruitful, and will multiply him exceedingly; twelve princes shall he beget, and I will make him a great nation.” (Genesis 17:20)

Catch the first words. *As for Ishmael, I have heard thee*. Yesterday we learned what Ishmael means. God hears.

The angel gave Hagar that name at the well because the LORD had heard her suffering.

Now God says his name back to his father, and says, I have heard. The boy named God Hears is heard again.

And God does not discard him. Read the list. Blessed. Fruitful. Multiplied greatly.

Twelve princes, which means twelve tribal rulers will come from him. A great nation.

Ishmael is not a mistake God throws away.

He is a son God blesses, by name, with promises most men in the Bible never receive.

If you have ever felt like the plan B, read Genesis 17:20 again. God does not do plan B children.

He heard Abraham, and He blessed the boy.

## At This Set Time

Then comes the one word that settles everything:

> “But my covenant will I establish with Isaac, which Sarah shall bear unto thee at this set time in the next year.” (Genesis 17:21)

But. Ishmael is blessed.

But the covenant, this specific promise that runs down to the whole world being blessed, goes through Isaac.

Not because God loves one boy and not the other.

Because God made a plan, and the plan was always Sarah’s son, and no shortcut, no thirteen years, and no amount of fatherly love for Ishmael could move it.

God even names the baby before he exists. **Isaac** means he laughs. Think about that.

Abraham just laughed at the promise, and God names the promise Laughter.

Ishmael was named by God before he was born too, back at the well, so this is the second boy in this family to get his name from heaven.

The difference is what each name says. One says God hears.

The other says laughter, the sound a hundred year old man made face down in the dirt when God told him the impossible.

And then, for the first time in twenty four years, a date. *At this set time in the next year*.

Think about what Abraham has lived through. Great nation, no date. Stars in the sky, no date.

Fire between the pieces, no date. Ten years, then a shortcut, then thirteen more years.

And now, when God is ready, God says exactly what He will do and exactly when. Next year.

This time next year, Sarah holds a son.

That is the heart of this whole day. They could not force God’s plan. They could not force God’s timing.

They tried, and they got Ishmael and a broken household.

But when God’s appointed time came, He did not need their help, their plan, or their shortcut. He just said, next year.

And it was done.

## The Selfsame Day

Now watch how the chapter ends, because this is the part that makes the whole day worth reading:

> “And Abraham took Ishmael his son, and all that were born in his house, and all that were bought with his money, every male among the men of Abraham’s house; and circumcised the flesh of their foreskin in the selfsame day, as God had said unto him.” (Genesis 17:23)

**The selfsame day** means that very same day. God finishes speaking, and Abraham gets up off the ground and does it.

He circumcises Ishmael, thirteen years old. Every servant born in the camp. Every man bought with money.

And himself, at ninety nine.

Hundreds of men, a painful and deeply personal procedure, and Abraham does not sleep on it, consult anybody, or wait for a better week.

Same day.

Put that next to Genesis 16.

There, Abraham and Sarah had a promise and no instructions, and they built their own solution.

Here, God gives Abraham a hard, specific command, and he obeys it before the sun goes down.

That is the whole difference between forcing God’s plan and following it. One is you deciding what God must have meant.

The other is you doing exactly what God actually said.

So look at how far this story has moved.

From a couple trying to make the promise happen, to a God who speaks again after thirteen years and says, I never forgot.

Sarah will have the son. His name is Isaac. Ishmael is blessed, but the covenant runs through Isaac.

And it happens next year. And a ninety nine year old man who had every reason to argue says nothing, and obeys.

The lesson is not sit still and wait for God to do everything. Abraham had plenty to do, and he did it the same day.

The lesson is this.

You cannot force God’s plan into the shape you want, and you cannot make His timetable move by building a substitute.

Your job is to trust what God has actually said, and obey what He has actually given you to do.

Tomorrow, three strangers walk up to Abraham’s tent in the heat of the day, and Sarah is listening behind the door.

But today is not finished yet. Take the reflection questions first, then today’s action, and end your day in prayer.

## What To Watch For In Genesis 17

Check Abram’s age in the first verse and compare it to his age at the end of yesterday’s chapter.

The gap is the whole mood of today.

Two people get new names in this chapter. Watch exactly when the Bible starts using them.

Abraham is going to laugh. Then he is going to ask about Ishmael. Watch how God answers both.

**As you read, notice:**

* ✨ How God introduces Himself in verse 1
* 📜 The sign God attaches to this covenant
* 👶 What God promises about Sarah specifically
* ⏳ The exact time God gives for Isaac, and how fast Abraham obeys

And think about this question: **What did thirteen years of raising Ishmael do to the way Abram heard this chapter?**

## Reflection Questions

1. **What is your Ishmael, the thing you built while waiting that you have quietly started calling God’s plan?** Abraham raised his for thirteen years. God still blessed the boy. But God did not let him become the covenant. Name yours honestly.
2. **God renamed a childless man Father of Many Nations before any of it existed. What name is God speaking over you that your life does not match yet?** The wound became the prophecy. Ask God what He calls you.
3. **When God gives you something specific to do, how long does it take you to do it?** Abraham obeyed the selfsame day, at ninety nine, with a knife. Look at the gap between what God has said and what you have done.

## Today's Prayer

Father,

You are El Shaddai, the Almighty God.

Nothing I am facing is too hard for You, and nothing I have built in my waiting can change what You have planned.

I confess I have my Ishmaels. Things I made while waiting on You, that I slowly started treating like Your answer.

Thank You that You bless what I built out of weakness. But do not let me mistake it for Your covenant.

**Take the pen back, Father. Write the plan the way You meant it.**

Speak Your name over me the way You spoke Abraham over Abram.

Call me what I am going to be, even while my life still looks like what I was. And give me the faith to answer to it.

I have laughed at Your promises before, face down, because they sounded impossible.

Forgive the laugh, and then do the impossible anyway, the way You did for a hundred year old man and a ninety year old woman.

And when You give me something to do, make me a selfsame day kind of person. No sleeping on it.

No waiting for a better week. You speak, I move.

That is what Abraham learned in twenty four years, and I do not want to take that long.

**In Jesus’ name, amen.**
