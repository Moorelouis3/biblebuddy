Most people have read the beginning of the Bible. Far fewer have understood it.

Genesis 1 to 8 is where everything starts. The world. People. Work. Marriage. Sin. Death. The first murder. The flood. And a God who keeps coming to find people who keep hiding from Him.

Skip these chapters and the rest of the Bible is hard to follow. Understand them and almost everything after them makes more sense.

The Beginning of Everything walks you through all eight chapters in 21 days.

Here is how a day works.

  • A short reading, with what to watch for before you open it.
  • A clear explanation of what is actually happening in the text, in plain language.
  • Reflection questions that are genuinely hard to answer.
  • One concrete thing to do that day.
  • A written prayer to close.

About fifteen minutes. Every day stands on its own.

What the 21 days cover

  • Days 1 to 5. The first line of the Bible, light out of darkness, a world filled with life, people made in God's image, and the day God rested.
  • Days 6 and 7. The man, the ground he came from, the garden, the work he was given, and the first marriage.
  • Days 8 and 9. The question that started it, the hiding, and what God said and did on the worst day in history.
  • Days 10 to 12. Cain and Abel, a judgment, and a family line that gets darker before Seth is born.
  • Days 13 to 15. Ten generations from Adam to Noah, a world gone wrong, and one man told to build before there was any rain.
  • Days 16 to 21. Entering the ark, the waters rising, the God who remembered, the raven and the dove, dry ground, and an altar.

Written for people who are not Bible scholars.

Day 1 starts by explaining what Genesis even is and where the name comes from. No background is assumed. Where the older words need explaining, they are explained. Where sincere Christians read a passage differently, the study says so rather than pretending the question is settled.

Scripture throughout is the King James Version.

Who this is for

  • Anyone who has started at Genesis and stalled
  • New believers who want to begin where the Bible begins
  • Readers who know the famous stories but have never seen how they connect
  • Small groups and families working through Genesis together

Genesis is not only telling us how the world began. It is telling us what we were made for, what went wrong, and what kind of God we are dealing with.
