# Bible Study Note Style Spec

This file defines how every study note in Bible Buddy is written. It was built
from notes Louis wrote by hand for Genesis 1 through 30. Those notes are the
standard. Match them.

Read this file in full before writing a single note. Do not write from memory
of a previous chapter.

## 1. What a note actually is

A note is not a description of the verse. A note teaches the reader something
they did not know when they started reading.

Before writing any note, answer this in your head: what does the reader now
understand that they did not understand before?

If the answer is "what the verse says," the note has failed. The verse
already says what the verse says. The note exists to explain a word, correct
a wrong assumption, supply missing background, or land the meaning.

## 2. The shape

Every note has exactly three parts.

Title The exact phrase lifted from the KJV verse, in title case. Not a topic
label. Not a summary you invented.

Right: `Without Form And Void`
Right: `Whose Top May Reach Unto Heaven`
Wrong: `The Condition Of The Early Earth`

Body Four to eight lines. One idea per line. A blank line between every
single line.

Bullets Four bullets. Each one short. Each one carries a different idea.

## 3. Line rules

These are hard. Do not break them.

* One idea per line. If a line contains a second idea, split it into two
  lines.
* Never write a paragraph. If two sentences sit next to each other without a
  blank line between them, that is a bug.
* No stacked clauses. The moment you write "while," "rather than," "close
  enough that," or a second "and" in one sentence, stop and split it.
* Under 20 words per line. Most lines should be under 12.
* The white space does the work. Short lines with air around them read as
  teaching. Dense text reads as a textbook.

## 4. How to open

Every note opens with one of these four moves. Pick the one the verse
actually calls for.

Move 1: Define the word Use when the verse turns on a word the reader thinks
they understand but does not.

To mourn means much more than simply feeling sad.

Move 2: Correct the misreading Use when readers commonly assume something the
text does not say.

This does not mean the people thought they could physically climb into God's
dwelling place.

Move 3: Identify the vague reference Use when the verse contains a pronoun, a
place, or a group the reader cannot pin down.

The word they refers to the descendants of Noah.

Move 4: State the point flat Use when the verse carries a theological weight
that should be named immediately.

God was never absent from His creation.

Never open with a restatement of the verse. Never open with "This verse
describes" or "Here we see."

## 5. The tools

Reach for these in the body. Use the one the content needs. Do not use all
of them in one note.

Bold the term being defined. The pattern is: "word" means X.

"Without form" means it had not yet been shaped or organized. "Void" means it
was empty and unfilled.

Give the everyday analogy. Use when the concept is abstract. Keep it to two
lines, one for the picture, one for the point.

Think of a newly built house before anyone moves in.
The structure is there, but it is not yet furnished or lived in.

Supply historical background. Use when the term is cultural and the reader
has no frame for it. Name the thing in bold.

Many scholars believe it was likely a ziggurat, a massive stepped tower used
in ancient Mesopotamian worship.

Use an inline list for concrete components. When the thing being explained
has parts, introduce with a colon and list them. One repeated emoji for the
whole list, not varied.

It often included:
✨ Crying openly
✨ Wearing rough sackcloth instead of normal clothing
✨ Sitting on the ground in ashes
✨ Fasting for a period of time

Say what the text does not say. This is a real feature, not a hedge. It
protects the reader from reading in things that are not there.

It does not necessarily mean they were traveling from the Garden of Eden.
The Bible does not make that connection.

## 6. How to close

The last one or two lines of the body carry the meaning. Never end on
mechanics or logistics.

Close by naming the pattern:

Genesis 1 follows that same pattern.
First God forms the world.
Then He fills it.

Or by naming the weight:

The unfinished world was never abandoned.
It was under God's care from the very beginning.

## 7. Bullet rules

Four bullets. They are the compressed version of the note, not a summary of
it.

* Three to eight words each. No full sentences.
* Each bullet carries a different idea. If two bullets say the same thing,
  one gets cut.
* Never restate a body line. If a bullet repeats a sentence from the body
  almost word for word, rewrite it.
* The last bullet lands the takeaway. It is the so what, not another fact.
* They must stand alone. A reader who reads only the bullets should still
  learn something.

Right:
🌀 Without form means unshaped
🟦 Void means empty
🏗️ Creation is unfinished
📖 God will now form and fill it

Wrong:
📍 The rings sat near the border, close to the tabletop itself

That is a sentence from the body pasted in again.

## 8. Emoji

* One emoji per bullet.
* Match the emoji to that bullet's content. A tower gets 🗼. Water gets 🌊.
  Pride gets 💔.
* Do not repeat the same emoji across bullets in one note.
* The final bullet uses 📖 for a scripture point or ➡️ for an implication.
* Inline lists inside the body use one repeated emoji for the whole list.
* The note title gets one emoji matched to its subject.

## 9. Words

Never use hyphens. Not in compounds, not anywhere.

No em dashes. No semicolons.

No contractions. Write "does not," not "doesn't." Write "it is," not "it's."
Possessives like "God's" and "Abraham's" are fine.

Banned filler. These words signal the note is padding: positioned, utilize,
essentially, furthermore, moreover, indeed, notably, it is important to
note, we can see that, this passage demonstrates.

Banned soft hedges. roughly, somewhat, fairly, quite, rather (as an
intensifier), sort of.

Allowed honest hedges. When scholarship is genuinely divided, say so
plainly: "Many scholars believe," "it was likely," "the text does not tell
us." That is accuracy, not padding. Never invent certainty the text does not
have.

No academic register. If a line sounds like a commentary, rewrite it the way
you would say it out loud to one person.

## 10. Approved examples

These five were written by hand and are the target. Study the rhythm, not
just the rules.

### Without Form And Void

The earth already existed, but it was not yet ready for life.

"Without form" means it had not yet been shaped or organized.

"Void" means it was empty and unfilled.

Think of a newly built house before anyone moves in.

The structure is there, but it is not yet furnished or lived in.

Genesis 1 follows that same pattern.

First God forms the world.

Then He fills it.

🌀 Without form means unshaped
🟦 Void means empty
🏗️ Creation is unfinished
📖 God will now form and fill it

### The Spirit Of God Moved

God was never absent from His creation.

Even while the earth was dark and unfinished, His Spirit was already
present.

The word "moved" carries the idea of hovering, much like a bird watches
over its young.

Before God begins speaking order into the world, His presence is already
there.

The unfinished world was never abandoned.

It was under God's care from the very beginning.

🕊️ God's Spirit is present
🌊 Hovering over the waters
👀 Creation is under God's care
📖 God's presence comes before His work is completed

### As They Journeyed From The East

The word they refers to the descendants of Noah.

As the population grew, groups of people began migrating together.

The phrase from the east simply describes the direction they traveled.

It does not necessarily mean they were traveling from the Garden of Eden.

The Bible does not make that connection.

🚶 They refers to Noah's descendants
🧭 They migrated together
📖 From the east describes their direction of travel
➡️ The Bible does not connect this directly to Eden

### Whose Top May Reach Unto Heaven

This does not mean the people thought they could physically climb into
God's dwelling place.

The phrase describes building an extremely tall tower that reached high
into the sky.

Many scholars believe it was likely a ziggurat, a massive stepped tower
used in ancient Mesopotamian worship.

The tower represented human pride and self exaltation.

👀 Reach unto heaven means extremely high
🗼 It was likely a ziggurat
💔 The tower became a symbol of human pride
➡️ They sought greatness without God

### Abraham Came To Mourn For Sarah

To mourn means much more than simply feeling sad.

In the ancient Near East, mourning was a public expression of deep grief.

It often included:
✨ Crying openly
✨ Wearing rough sackcloth instead of normal clothing
✨ Sitting on the ground in ashes
✨ Fasting for a period of time
✨ Family and friends gathering to comfort those who were grieving

This was not a brief moment of sadness.

It was a season of honoring the person who had died.

Sarah had been Abraham's wife for many decades.

## 11. Rejected example

This is what the notes must never look like again.

### Over Against The Border Were The Rings, The Places For The Staves

The rings sat positioned right near the decorative border from verse 12,
close enough to the tabletop that the poles running through them would keep
the table roughly level and stable while it was being carried, rather than
tipping or dragging.

📍 The rings sat near the border, close to the tabletop itself
⚖️ This positioning kept the table stable and level while being carried
🚶 Placement mattered for a table meant to travel constantly with the camp

Everything wrong with it:

1. One 40 word sentence with four clauses stacked. Should be five separate
   lines.
2. Nothing is defined. Border, staves, rings all go unexplained.
3. No analogy. The reader gets no picture to hold.
4. The bullets restate the paragraph. Bullet one is sentence one again.
5. Bullets are full sentences, not compressed keys.
6. It ends on logistics. There is no meaning, no so what.
7. Filler everywhere: "sat positioned right near," "roughly level," "rather
   than tipping or dragging."
8. Only three bullets.

Here is the same verse rewritten correctly:

The rings were not placed just anywhere.

They sat high up, right near the border.

The border was the decorative rim from verse 12, running around the edge of
the tabletop.

That placement was on purpose.

Think about carrying a full tray of drinks.

Hold it low and loose and everything slides.

Grip it near the top edge and it stays flat.

The poles slid through the rings and the table stayed level while men
carried it.

This table was never meant to be empty.

The bread sat on it always.

So God did not just design the table.

He designed how it would travel.

📍 The rings sat high, near the border
⚖️ High placement kept the table level
🍞 The bread stayed on it while it moved
📖 God designed the carrying, not just the object

## 12. Chapters that resist teaching

Genealogies, dimension lists, offering quantities, and boundary descriptions
still get real notes. They are harder, not exempt.

For these, the teaching usually comes from one of three places:

* What the number or name meant to the original reader. A cubit means
  nothing to a modern reader. Say roughly how big it was in something they
  can picture.
* Why the detail was recorded at all. Someone chose to preserve this. That
  choice carries meaning.
* Where it points. A name in a genealogy is often a hinge to something later
  in the story.

Never fill these chapters with restated measurements. If you genuinely
cannot teach something, write a shorter note rather than a padded one.

## 13. Check before you save

Run every note against this list.

* [ ] Does the reader learn something the verse did not already tell them?
* [ ] Is every line one idea with a blank line after it?
* [ ] Is any line over 20 words?
* [ ] Did the note open with one of the four moves?
* [ ] Is the key term bolded and defined?
* [ ] Does the close land on meaning rather than mechanics?
* [ ] Are there exactly four bullets?
* [ ] Is every bullet under eight words?
* [ ] Does any bullet repeat a body line?
* [ ] Does the last bullet carry the takeaway?
* [ ] Any hyphens, em dashes, semicolons, or contractions?
* [ ] Any banned filler words?
* [ ] Read it out loud. Does it sound like a person teaching, or like a
      commentary?

If any answer is wrong, fix it before saving. Do not save and move on.
